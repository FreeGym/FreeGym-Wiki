# How Do Peptide, Steroid, Amine, Protein, and Lipid-Derived Hormones Differ?

The most important point is that these are **not five completely separate categories**. Steroids are one lipid-derived class, while peptide, protein and glycoprotein labels overlap within the amino-acid-chain family. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/)) ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/)) ([25](https://doi.org/10.7150/thno.61747))

- **Peptide and protein hormones belong to the same amino-acid-chain family.** The terminology overlaps, and the distinction is mainly one of size and structural complexity. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

- **Glycoprotein hormones are protein hormones with attached carbohydrate chains.** ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

- **Steroid hormones are themselves lipid-derived**, specifically cholesterol-derived. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- **Amine hormones are small molecules derived mainly from amino acids.** Catecholamines are synthesized from tyrosine, melatonin from tryptophan, and thyroid hormones by coupling iodinated tyrosyl residues. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/)) ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))

- The non-steroidal **lipid-derived signals emphasized in this article** are eicosanoid mediators such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes, which often act locally rather than as classical circulating hormones. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/)) ([15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26139350/))


Among the hormones and signaling molecules discussed here, a practical classification is therefore:

|Broader chemical family|Subtypes|
|---|---|
|Amino-acid-chain hormones|Peptides, proteins and glycoproteins ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))|
|Small amino-acid derivatives|Catecholamines, thyroid hormones and melatonin ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/)) ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))|
|Lipid-derived signals|Steroid hormones, eicosanoids and other lipid mediators ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/)) ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))|

These chemical differences matter because they influence how a hormone is synthesized, whether it can be stored, how it travels through blood, whether it can cross a cell membrane, where its receptor is located, and how quickly the signal begins and disappears. They predict tendencies, not absolute rules. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

## Peptide and protein hormones belong to one family

Peptide and protein hormones are both chains of amino acids produced through gene transcription and ribosomal translation. One recent biochemical framework uses **peptide** for chains of 2-50 amino acids and **protein** for chains longer than 50 amino acids. Other endocrine and drug-delivery literature still uses peptide more broadly, so the label is a convention rather than a physiological boundary. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8771547/))

Insulin illustrates the ambiguity. Mature insulin contains 51 amino acids, placing it just above the operational cutoff in that framework, while oral-delivery literature commonly discusses insulin among peptide drugs. The predominant 22-kDa form of human growth hormone is a single-chain protein containing 191 amino acids. Physiologically, insulin and growth hormone still belong to the same broad amino-acid-chain family. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6114276/)) ([18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30230185/)) ([25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8771547/))

Examples include:

- Amino-acid-chain hormones often called peptide or polypeptide hormones include TRH, oxytocin, vasopressin, glucagon, ACTH, insulin, IGF-1 and parathyroid hormone. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

- Larger protein hormones include growth hormone, prolactin and leptin. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6114276/))

- TSH, LH, FSH and hCG are classical glycoprotein hormones. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))


The peptide-versus-protein label tells you something about size, but it usually tells you less about hormone behavior than whether the molecule is water-compatible, how it is processed, which receptor it activates, and whether it has a binding protein. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

## How peptide and protein hormones are made

Peptide and protein hormones are gene-encoded. A cell transcribes the relevant gene into messenger RNA, translates that RNA on ribosomes associated with the rough endoplasmic reticulum, and sends the developing hormone through the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18184105/))

Many peptide hormones begin as **preprohormones**. The initial signal sequence directs the growing protein into the secretory pathway and is then removed, producing a prohormone. Enzymes can subsequently cleave and chemically modify that prohormone to create the active hormone. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18184105/))

For example:

- Preproinsulin becomes proinsulin and then insulin plus C-peptide. ([18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30230185/))

- Proglucagon is processed predominantly to glucagon in pancreatic alpha cells and to GLP-1 and related peptides in intestinal L cells. These are predominant patterns rather than completely exclusive compartments. Ex vivo human-islet studies have detected bioactive GLP-1 production in alpha cells, but its contribution to circulating GLP-1 in vivo remains uncertain. ([19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7592866/)) ([38](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22965295/))

- Pro-opiomelanocortin can generate ACTH, melanocyte-stimulating peptides and other products, depending on the tissue. ([20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30156493/))


This means that one gene does not always produce only one signaling molecule. Different tissues can process the same precursor differently because they express different processing enzymes. ([3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18184105/)) ([19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7592866/)) ([20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30156493/))

Not every secreted protein hormone is converted into several smaller hormones. The predominant 22-kDa growth hormone isoform is produced as a single-chain 191-amino-acid protein after signal-peptide removal and folding. The preprohormone-cleavage model applies most clearly to many smaller peptide hormones. ([17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6114276/))

## Glycoprotein hormones are protein hormones with carbohydrate chains

A **glycoprotein hormone** is not a separate chemical family from protein hormones. It is a protein hormone with carbohydrate chains covalently attached to it. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

The classical glycoprotein hormones are:

- Thyroid-stimulating hormone, or TSH. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

- Luteinizing hormone, or LH. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

- Follicle-stimulating hormone, or FSH. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

- Human chorionic gonadotropin, or hCG. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))


These hormones contain an alpha subunit and a beta subunit. The alpha subunit is shared, while the beta subunit is the principal determinant of receptor specificity. However, LH and hCG act through the same LH/CG receptor, and both subunits participate in receptor binding. Their carbohydrate chains aid folding and disulfide pairing and influence receptor interaction, biological activity, distribution and circulating half-life. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

These features help explain why two protein hormones can activate related receptors yet remain in the circulation for very different lengths of time. Glycosylation can substantially alter how quickly the kidney and liver remove a hormone. ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/))

## Many peptide and protein hormones can be stored

Many peptide and protein hormones are packaged into membrane-bound dense-core secretory granules. The cell can therefore manufacture the hormone in advance, hold it in reserve, and release it rapidly when an appropriate stimulus arrives. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))

Release commonly occurs through regulated, calcium-dependent exocytosis. A rise in intracellular calcium promotes fusion of the secretory granule with the cell membrane and discharge of its contents. This permits secretion to change rapidly even though manufacturing new protein takes longer. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))

Examples include:

- Insulin granules in pancreatic beta cells. ([18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30230185/)) ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))

- Glucagon granules in alpha cells. ([19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7592866/)) ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))

- ACTH granules in pituitary corticotrophs. ([20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30156493/)) ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))

- Vasopressin and oxytocin vesicles in neurosecretory terminals. ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))


Storage is common, but it is not universal. Some protein hormones are secreted more continuously through constitutive pathways, while others use a mixture of constitutive and regulated secretion. The accurate rule is therefore that peptide and protein hormones **can commonly be stored**, not that every member of the family must be stored. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/))

## How peptide and protein hormones travel

Many peptide and protein hormones are sufficiently water-compatible to circulate with little carrier-protein sequestration compared with hydrophobic steroid and thyroid hormones. This is a tendency, not a defining rule for every peptide or protein hormone. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

There are important exceptions. Growth hormone is partly bound to growth hormone-binding protein, while IGF-1 circulates mainly in complexes with IGF-binding proteins. Binding can alter a hormone's measured distribution and metabolic clearance, although the physiological role of growth hormone-binding protein is not fully resolved. ([5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33962049/)) ([39](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ghir.2005.11.001))

Many small peptide hormones are cleared within minutes through combinations of proteolysis, receptor-mediated uptake, and hepatic or renal clearance. Rates vary substantially with molecular size, binding proteins, glycosylation and resistance to enzymatic cleavage, so short circulation is not a defining requirement. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/)) ([5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33962049/))

## Peptide and protein hormones usually use cell-surface receptors

An intact peptide or protein hormone does not ordinarily cross the hydrophobic interior of the cell membrane. Its receptor therefore usually has a hormone-binding region exposed on the cell surface and a mechanism for transmitting the signal into the cell. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

Different peptide and protein hormones use different receptor families:

- Glucagon, ACTH, PTH, TSH, LH and FSH use G-protein-coupled receptors. ([27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4705372/)) ([28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22266723/)) ([29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21208602/))

- Insulin and IGF-1 use receptor tyrosine kinases. ([5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33962049/)) ([30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3941218/))

- Growth hormone and prolactin use related class I cytokine receptors that signal prominently through JAK2-STAT5 pathways. The long form of the leptin receptor is also a class I cytokine receptor, but it signals prominently through JAK2-STAT3 and should not be treated as identical to growth hormone or prolactin signaling. ([31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20664532/)) ([32](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5685068/)) ([33](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25232147/))

- ANP and BNP signal chiefly through the membrane guanylyl cyclase receptor NPR-A, and CNP chiefly through NPR-B. NPR-C primarily mediates clearance and can also signal through pathways that do not involve its own guanylyl cyclase activity. ([23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16291870/))


Depending on receptor class and cell type, cell-surface pathways can alter enzyme activity, ion transport, secretion, metabolism and gene transcription. A cell-surface receptor therefore does not mean that the hormone has only short-term effects. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16291870/)) ([30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3941218/)) ([31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20664532/))

## Steroid hormones are cholesterol-derived hormones

Steroid hormones are small lipid-derived molecules built from cholesterol and characterized by the steroid ring structure. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

The major human steroid hormones include:

- Cortisol. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- Aldosterone. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- Testosterone. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- Estradiol. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- Progesterone. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- DHEA and related androgen precursors. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))


Steroidogenic cells convert cholesterol into pregnenolone and then use cell-specific combinations of enzymes to produce the final hormone. Different enzyme patterns explain why the adrenal cortex, ovaries, testes and placenta can begin with cholesterol but produce different steroid mixtures. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

The acute control of steroidogenesis often begins with moving cholesterol into the mitochondria, where CYP11A1 converts it into pregnenolone. The steroidogenic acute regulatory protein, or StAR, is central to this cholesterol-transfer step in many steroidogenic tissues. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

## Most classic steroid hormones are synthesized when needed

Most classic adrenal and gonadal steroid hormones, including cortisol, aldosterone, estradiol and testosterone, are synthesized on demand rather than stored in secretory granules as finished hormones. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

Instead, they store cholesterol and cholesteryl esters as precursors. When the cell is stimulated, cholesterol is mobilized and converted enzymatically into active steroid hormones. The finished steroid then leaves the cell as it is produced rather than waiting for vesicular exocytosis. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

This is why steroid release is closely linked to the rate of steroid synthesis. ACTH is the principal acute regulator of adrenal glucocorticoid synthesis. Angiotensin II and extracellular potassium are the dominant physiological regulators of aldosterone synthesis. LH stimulates Leydig- and theca-cell steroidogenesis, while FSH induces granulosa-cell aromatase and supports estradiol production. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

The distinction is not simply "stored versus not stored." Peptide-producing cells often store the **finished signaling molecule**, whereas steroidogenic cells primarily store **precursor material and synthetic capacity**. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

## Steroids travel with carrier proteins

Steroid hormones are poorly soluble in the aqueous environment of plasma. Much of the circulating hormone is therefore reversibly bound to albumin or more specific binding proteins. The proportion and carrier identity vary by hormone and physiological state. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/))

Examples include:

- Cortisol binding to corticosteroid-binding globulin and albumin. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/))

- Testosterone and estradiol binding to sex hormone-binding globulin and albumin. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/))

- Progesterone binding to albumin and corticosteroid-binding globulin. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/))


Reversible binding creates a circulating reservoir, buffers fluctuations in free hormone and usually slows clearance. Most immediate receptor access depends on the unbound fraction. Bound hormone is therefore not permanently inert, but it is not equivalent to immediately receptor-available hormone. Carrier proteins can also influence delivery, metabolism and local availability. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/))

Changes in binding-protein concentration can change the measured **total** hormone concentration without producing a proportional change in the free concentration. The clinical implications are analyte-specific, so total cortisol, testosterone, estradiol and thyroid hormone results cannot all be corrected or interpreted by one generic rule. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/)) ([21](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

## Steroids classically use intracellular receptors

Because steroids can pass through lipid membranes more readily than peptide hormones, their classical receptors are inside the target cell. Steroid receptors belong to the nuclear receptor family and function as ligand-regulated transcription factors. ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/)) ([34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864526/))

After binding its hormone, the receptor changes conformation, interacts with regulatory proteins and influences gene transcription. The cell then changes the production of specific proteins. Some nuclear events can occur within minutes, but changes in gene expression commonly unfold over hours; timing and persistence vary by hormone, tissue and endpoint. ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/)) ([34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864526/))

This classical pathway helps explain why many steroid effects develop more slowly but persist longer than a rapid peptide or catecholamine signal. It is a tendency rather than a universal timing rule. ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/)) ([34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864526/))

However, steroids are not exclusively slow or genomic. Steroid receptors also exist in extranuclear locations, including near or within the plasma membrane. Steroids can activate kinases, ion channels and second-messenger pathways within seconds or minutes. Rapid and transcriptional steroid signaling interact rather than operating as completely separate systems. ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/)) ([34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864526/))

## Amine hormones are the most behaviorally diverse category

Amine hormones are small molecules produced by modifying amino-acid precursors. The main precursors discussed here are tyrosine and tryptophan. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/)) ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))

Major amine hormones include:

- Epinephrine, norepinephrine and dopamine, derived from tyrosine. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

- T4 and T3, formed from iodinated tyrosyl residues. ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/))

- Melatonin, derived from tryptophan through serotonin. ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))


The important point is that **amine origin does not predict one shared physiological behavior**. Catecholamines behave in several respects like peptide hormones. Thyroid hormones behave in several respects like steroid hormones. Melatonin combines features that do not fit either shortcut completely. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))

## Catecholamines behave like rapid water-compatible hormones

Depending on enzyme expression, catecholamine biosynthesis proceeds from tyrosine through L-DOPA and dopamine to norepinephrine and, in epinephrine-producing adrenal chromaffin cells, epinephrine. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

Catecholamines are stored in chromaffin secretory granules in the adrenal medulla. Acetylcholine released by preganglionic sympathetic splanchnic fibers triggers calcium-dependent exocytosis, rapidly releasing them into the blood. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

Catecholamines are relatively water-compatible. Epinephrine and norepinephrine activate cell-surface alpha- and beta-adrenergic G-protein-coupled receptors. Dopamine primarily activates D1-like and D2-like dopaminergic G-protein-coupled receptors. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/)) ([22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21303898/)) ([35](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3290771/))

Catecholamine signals therefore have several peptide-like characteristics:

- Vesicular storage. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

- Rapid exocytotic release. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

- Cell-surface receptors. ([22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21303898/)) ([35](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3290771/))

- Acute hemodynamic and metabolic effects. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

- Rapid metabolism and short circulating persistence. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))


Norepinephrine and epinephrine are removed by neuronal and extraneuronal transporters and inactivated by metabolizing enzymes. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

## Thyroid hormones are amines that behave like steroids

Thyroid hormones are chemically derived from tyrosine, but their handling differs substantially from catecholamines. ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/))

Thyroid follicular cells produce the large protein thyroglobulin and secrete it into the follicular lumen. Tyrosyl residues within thyroglobulin are iodinated to form MIT and DIT. Oxidative coupling of these residues generates thyroglobulin-bound T4 and T3. The iodinated thyroglobulin is stored extracellularly in the thyroid colloid, creating an unusually large hormone reserve. When thyroid hormone is needed, thyroglobulin is taken back into the cell and proteolytically processed to release T4 and T3. ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/))

Once in blood, thyroid hormones are almost entirely bound to plasma proteins, principally thyroxine-binding globulin, transthyretin and albumin. Strong protein binding contributes to the slow clearance and prolonged circulating half-life of T4. ([21](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([46](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10718550/))

Thyroid hormone entry into target cells is transporter-mediated, with MCT8 serving as one important transporter. Intracellular deiodinases can convert T4 into the more receptor-active T3 or into inactive metabolites. T3 then acts mainly through nuclear thyroid hormone receptors that regulate transcription. ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/))

Thyroid hormones are therefore the major exception to the idea that amine hormones are water-compatible, short-lived and membrane-receptor acting. Chemically they are amines, but physiologically they resemble steroid hormones in several respects. ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/)) ([21](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- Strong plasma-protein binding. ([21](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- Long circulating persistence. ([21](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- Transporter-mediated cellular entry. ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/))

- Nuclear receptors and major effects on gene transcription. ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/))


## Melatonin is a tryptophan-derived indoleamine

Melatonin is synthesized from tryptophan through serotonin and N-acetylserotonin. Pineal melatonin production rises during the biological night and falls during the day. ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))

Unlike catecholamines, pineal melatonin is not stored in large secretory granules. It is released as it is synthesized. It is amphiphilic, can cross cell membranes, circulates bound to albumin, and has a plasma half-life of roughly 40 minutes in humans. ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))

Its principal high-affinity receptors, MT1 and MT2, are cell-surface G-protein-coupled receptors. Melatonin can therefore cross membranes yet still produce many of its physiological effects through membrane receptors. ([13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514204/))

Melatonin shows why receptor location cannot be predicted from membrane permeability alone. A molecule may be able to enter cells while still using cell-surface receptors for its main endocrine signaling. ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/)) ([13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514204/))

## Steroids are lipid-derived, but not all lipid-derived signals are steroids

The term **lipid-derived hormone** is broader than steroid hormone. Steroids are one cholesterol-derived branch of lipid signaling. Other lipid-derived signals come from fatty acids or membrane lipids and have different structures and mechanisms. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/)) ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

In the practical classification used here, "steroid" identifies the cholesterol-derived steroid-hormone branch, while "other lipid-derived mediator" identifies the non-steroid lipid mediators emphasized here, particularly eicosanoids. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/)) ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

## Eicosanoids are mainly short-range lipid mediators

Eicosanoids are oxidized derivatives of 20-carbon polyunsaturated fatty acids. Arachidonic acid is the most familiar precursor, although other polyunsaturated fatty acids can also contribute. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/)) ([15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26139350/))

Major eicosanoid groups include:

- Prostaglandins, including prostacyclin. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

- Thromboxanes. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

- Leukotrienes. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

- Lipoxins. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/)) ([15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26139350/))

- HETEs and EETs. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))


Cyclooxygenase, lipoxygenase and cytochrome P450 pathways convert fatty-acid precursors into different eicosanoids. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

Unlike peptide hormones stored in secretory granules, free eicosanoid mediators are generally synthesized in response to stimulation from precursor polyunsaturated fatty acids commonly stored in membrane phospholipids. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

Most eicosanoids are short-lived local autocrine or paracrine mediators. For this reason, **lipid mediator** is often more precise than classical endocrine hormone. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/)) ([15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26139350/))

Many eicosanoids signal through cell-surface G-protein-coupled receptors, although some also bind nuclear receptors. Lipid origin therefore does not determine one receptor location. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

Eicosanoids are also not uniformly pro-inflammatory. Different products can promote or restrain inflammation, and some eicosanoids, especially lipoxins, participate in resolution. Other eicosanoids regulate blood-vessel tone, platelet activity, gastrointestinal protection, kidney function or reproduction. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/)) ([15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26139350/))

## Calcitriol is a useful boundary case

The vitamin D pathway begins with 7-dehydrocholesterol and produces calcitriol, the active hormonal form of vitamin D. Calcitriol is a **secosteroid**, meaning that one ring of the conventional steroid structure is open. ([16](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM198904133201506))

Calcitriol travels principally bound to vitamin D-binding protein, with a smaller albumin-bound fraction. It acts mainly through the intracellular vitamin D receptor, which partners with retinoid X receptor to regulate transcription. Calcitriol is structurally a secosteroid and shares major functional features with steroid hormones despite lacking an intact four-ring steroid nucleus. ([16](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM198904133201506)) ([36](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27742848/)) ([37](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839493/))

The fact that it is called a vitamin does not prevent its active metabolite from functioning as a hormone. ([16](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM198904133201506))

## Why "water-soluble versus lipid-soluble" is only a starting point

Solubility provides a useful first prediction:

- Water-compatible peptide hormones and catecholamines usually activate cell-surface receptors. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21303898/)) ([28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22266723/))

- Lipophilic steroid and thyroid hormones usually depend more on carrier proteins and intracellular receptors. ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/)) ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/)) ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/)) ([21](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))


But several exceptions prevent this from becoming an absolute rule:

- Thyroid hormones are amine-derived but behave like lipophilic steroid hormones in several respects. ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/)) ([21](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- Eicosanoids are lipid-derived but usually activate cell-surface receptors. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

- Melatonin crosses membranes but acts principally through membrane GPCRs. ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/)) ([13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514204/))

- Steroids have both nuclear and rapid extranuclear actions. ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/)) ([34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864526/))

- Peptide hormones can produce long-term transcriptional and structural effects. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3941218/))

- Protein binding can give some peptide and protein hormones much longer circulation than expected. ([5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33962049/)) ([39](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ghir.2005.11.001))


The hormone's chemistry affects how it reaches the receptor. The receptor and downstream machinery determine what the target cell actually does. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))

## Storage strategy provides another useful distinction

The classes can also be compared by what the endocrine cell keeps in reserve:

- **Peptide and protein hormones:** the finished hormone is often stored inside secretory granules. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2626200/))

- **Catecholamines:** the finished hormone is stored inside chromaffin granules. ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/))

- **Steroids:** precursor cholesterol is stored, while the active hormone is generally synthesized when needed. ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/))

- **Eicosanoids:** precursor polyunsaturated fatty acids are commonly retained in membrane phospholipids, while free mediators are generally synthesized in response to stimulation. ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

- **Melatonin:** the active hormone is synthesized and released rather than stored in a large reserve. ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/))

- **Thyroid hormones:** an unusually large extracellular reserve is stored within thyroglobulin in the follicular colloid. ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/))


This storage strategy helps determine how rapidly secretion can increase and how long production can continue, but the timing remains hormone- and tissue-specific. ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/)) ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/)) ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/)) ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/))

## Why the classification matters clinically

Chemical class can help generate hypotheses about how a hormone should be measured, but it does not replace analyte-specific clinical guidance. For the thyroid, testosterone and glucocorticoid examples below, results must be interpreted in relation to the clinical question, sampling time and physiological state, medications, acute or chronic illness, binding proteins, assay method, biological variation and clinical context. A reference interval is not automatically a diagnostic threshold or treatment target. ([21](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([40](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) ([41](https://doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnae016))

Binding proteins can change measured total hormone concentrations, but the appropriate response is analyte-specific. In men being evaluated for hypogonadism, altered SHBG or a borderline total testosterone concentration may justify free testosterone measured by equilibrium dialysis or estimated from total testosterone, SHBG and albumin with a validated equation; direct analog free-testosterone immunoassays are inaccurate. The same guideline requires symptoms and signs plus consistently low concentrations and recommends confirmation with two separate fasting morning total-testosterone measurements, so one value should not be used by itself to diagnose hypogonadism. ([40](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

For cortisol, pregnancy and oral estrogen can raise corticosteroid-binding globulin and total serum cortisol, while low albumin or corticosteroid-binding globulin can lower total cortisol without a proportional change in free cortisol. Circadian and pulsatile secretion, acute illness, medications, kidney function, the assay and the suspected disorder affect whether serum cortisol, salivary cortisol, urinary free cortisol or dynamic testing is appropriate. A single static cortisol result cannot answer every question about glucocorticoid status. ([41](https://doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnae016))

For thyroid assessment, TSH and free T4 are frontline tests in most routine settings, but pregnancy, illness, medications, binding-protein changes and assay interference can materially change interpretation. Discordance with the clinical picture, prior results or other biochemical findings should prompt consideration of interference rather than automatic diagnosis or treatment. ([21](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([42](https://doi.org/10.1210/er.2018-00119))

Estradiol interpretation depends on sex, age, reproductive state, menstrual or treatment timing, concentration range and assay method. Routine direct immunoassays may lack adequate accuracy at low concentrations; where free estradiol is clinically relevant, its measurement is also method-dependent rather than a universal solution to binding-protein effects. ([43](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2012-3780))

Preanalytic requirements are analyte- and assay-specific. ACTH should be collected in the specimen type and handled according to the receiving laboratory's validated protocol. A 2025 systematic review found that, across the included studies, mean ACTH concentration changes in uncentrifuged EDTA tubes remained below the review's prespecified 10% threshold for 6 hours at room temperature and at least 8 hours when refrigerated. Immediate icing and separation therefore are not universal requirements, but laboratories should retain locally validated acceptance criteria for their assay and transport workflow. Collection time and sleep-wake context still matter because ACTH and cortisol secretion are pulsatile and circadian, and a single ACTH result is not a stand-alone measure of hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal function. ([24](https://doi.org/10.11613/BM.2025.010502)) ([41](https://doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnae016))

The classification also helps explain treatment routes, but oral delivery does not divide cleanly by hormone class. Peptide and protein therapeutics are generally vulnerable to gastrointestinal degradation and poor epithelial permeability; insulin, growth hormone and many others are therefore usually administered by injection, although specialized oral formulations can partly address these barriers. Many corticosteroid drugs can be administered orally, but oral bioavailability, first-pass metabolism and systemic exposure are molecule- and formulation-specific. Levothyroxine is also administered orally, but absorption depends on formulation and timing and can be reduced by food, coffee, soy, fiber, calcium, iron and enteral nutrition. ([25](https://doi.org/10.7150/thno.61747)) ([44](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-024-01419-7)) ([45](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph14030206))

These principles are educational, not a basis for self-diagnosis, unsupervised hormone use or changing treatment without an analyte-specific clinical evaluation. Incorrect sample timing, assay interpretation or unsupervised treatment changes can obscure an endocrine disorder or cause harm. ([21](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([40](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) ([41](https://doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnae016)) ([42](https://doi.org/10.1210/er.2018-00119))

## The takeaway

**Peptides, proteins and glycoproteins are gene-encoded amino-acid chains. Many are stored in vesicles, circulate with little carrier-protein sequestration and signal through cell-surface receptors, but important exceptions exist.** ([1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3735987/)) ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/)) ([4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25767463/)) ([5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33962049/))

**Classic adrenal and gonadal steroid hormones are cholesterol-derived lipid hormones. Their active forms are usually synthesized when needed, circulate substantially bound to carrier proteins and classically regulate transcription through intracellular receptors, while rapid extranuclear signaling is also possible.** ([6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21051590/)) ([7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27113851/)) ([8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27729652/))

**Amine hormones must be identified individually. Catecholamines resemble peptide hormones in several aspects of storage and signaling, thyroid hormones resemble steroids in several aspects of transport and receptor action, and melatonin combines features of both.** ([9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390824/)) ([10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30886364/)) ([11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22945636/)) ([12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215696/)) ([13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514204/))

**Other lipid-derived signals, especially eicosanoids, are usually short-lived local mediators synthesized from membrane fatty acids and commonly act through cell-surface receptors.** ([14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32808658/)) ([15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26139350/))

The categories are useful because chemistry constrains hormone handling. But chemistry does not dictate everything. The final response depends on synthesis, storage, transport, metabolism, receptor location and the signaling machinery inside the target cell. ([2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425548/))
