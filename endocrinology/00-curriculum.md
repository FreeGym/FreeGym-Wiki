# Endocrinology Curriculum

## 1. What Are Hormones Actually Doing?

- Why does the body need hormones instead of only nerves?
- What is the difference between endocrine, paracrine, autocrine, neuroendocrine, and intracrine signaling?
- How do peptide, steroid, amine, protein, and lipid-derived hormones differ?
- Why do some hormones act within seconds while others change gene expression over hours or days?
- Why do hormones need receptors, binding proteins, carrier proteins, enzymes, and clearance systems?
- How can tiny hormone concentrations produce large body-wide effects?
- What makes a tissue sensitive, resistant, permissive, or unresponsive to a hormone?
- How do hormones coordinate growth, fuel use, reproduction, stress, temperature, sleep, appetite, blood pressure, bone, and electrolytes?

## 2. How Does The Body Regulate Itself?

- What is a set point?
- Why does negative feedback dominate endocrinology?
- When does positive feedback make physiological sense?
- What is the difference between a hypothalamic problem, a pituitary problem, a gland problem, and a target-tissue problem?
- Why do trophic hormones matter?
- Why can hormone excess suppress one gland but enlarge another?
- How do pulsatility, circadian rhythm, sleep, meals, exercise, stress, puberty, pregnancy, aging, and acute illness change endocrine function?
- What is hormone resistance?
- Why do endocrine systems often fail gradually before they fail obviously?

## 3. How Do Endocrinologists Know What Is Happening?

- Why is an endocrine lab value never just high or low?
- What is the difference between total hormone, free hormone, active hormone, precursor, metabolite, and trophic signal?
- Why do binding proteins change interpretation?
- Why does timing matter for cortisol, testosterone, prolactin, TSH, glucose, insulin, and growth hormone?
- Why are some hormones measured directly while others are inferred indirectly?
- How do stimulation and suppression tests work?
- Why do pregnancy, acute illness, supplements, medications, kidney disease, liver disease, and assay interference distort endocrine labs?
- How do clinicians distinguish disease from normal adaptation?
- Why can imaging mislead if the biochemical question has not been answered first?

## 4. How Do The Hypothalamus And Pituitary Coordinate The Body?

- Why does the brain need an endocrine interface?
- How do hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones control the anterior pituitary?
- Why is the posterior pituitary different from the anterior pituitary?
- How do TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH, GH, prolactin, ADH, and oxytocin fit into whole-body regulation?
- Why is prolactin mainly controlled by inhibition rather than stimulation?
- Why can one pituitary lesion cause many different syndromes?
- Why do pituitary tumors cause problems through hormone excess, hormone deficiency, mass effect, or all three?
- How do visual fields, MRI, dynamic tests, and target-gland labs fit together?
- Why must adrenal function be considered before replacing thyroid hormone in hypopituitarism?

## 5. Why Does Blood Sugar Rise, Fall, Or Become Toxic?

- What is glucose doing for the body?
- Why does insulin exist?
- Why does glucagon exist?
- How do liver, muscle, fat, gut, pancreas, brain, and kidney cooperate after a meal and during fasting?
- Why does insulin resistance happen?
- Why do beta cells fail?
- What is the difference between type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes, LADA, MODY, gestational diabetes, and secondary diabetes?
- Why do DKA and HHS happen?
- Why does hypoglycemia become dangerous so quickly?
- How do metformin, insulin, GLP-1 drugs, SGLT2 inhibitors, sulfonylureas, TZDs, and DPP-4 inhibitors work?
- Why does diabetes damage eyes, kidneys, nerves, vessels, heart, skin, and immune defense?

## 6. Why Does The Thyroid Control So Much?

- Why does the body need thyroid hormone?
- Why does iodine matter?
- How are T4 and T3 made, stored, transported, converted, and cleared?
- Why is TSH such a sensitive signal?
- Why can TSH, free T4, and free T3 disagree?
- Why does hypothyroidism slow the body down?
- Why does hyperthyroidism speed it up?
- Why does Graves disease stimulate the thyroid?
- Why do thyroiditis and Graves disease both cause thyrotoxicosis but behave differently?
- Why do thyroid nodules form?
- Why are some thyroid cancers indolent while others are dangerous?
- How do levothyroxine, antithyroid drugs, radioactive iodine, beta blockers, and surgery fit physiologically?

## 7. Why Does The Body Need Stress Hormones?

- What is stress physiology trying to protect?
- Why does cortisol follow a daily rhythm?
- How does the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis work?
- How do cortisol, catecholamines, aldosterone, and adrenal androgens differ?
- Why does adrenal failure cause fatigue, weight loss, low blood pressure, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, or crisis?
- Why does cortisol excess cause central weight gain, muscle wasting, diabetes, hypertension, bruising, mood change, and infection risk?
- Why do steroid medications suppress the adrenal axis?
- How do ACTH, renin, aldosterone, DHEA-S, and adrenal imaging reveal where the problem is?
- How do dexamethasone suppression, ACTH stimulation, and late-night salivary cortisol work?
- Why are pheochromocytomas dangerous even when small?

## 8. Why Are Salt, Water, And Blood Pressure Endocrine Problems?

- Why are sodium, potassium, water, and blood pressure hormonally regulated?
- How does aldosterone work?
- How does renin decide when to rise?
- Why does primary aldosteronism cause hypertension and sometimes low potassium?
- Why can adrenal, kidney, vascular, medication, and genetic problems all look like endocrine hypertension?
- How does ADH regulate water?
- Why do SIADH and diabetes insipidus produce opposite water problems?
- Why can sodium disorders be more about water than salt?
- How do diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, mineralocorticoid antagonists, vasopressin drugs, and fluids affect these systems?
- Why can correcting sodium too fast injure the brain?

## 9. Why Do Reproductive Hormones Cycle, Mature, And Decline?

- Why does puberty begin?
- How does the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis work?
- Why are reproductive hormones pulsatile?
- Why does the menstrual cycle have phases?
- Why does ovulation require timing rather than just hormone quantity?
- Why do amenorrhea, infertility, hirsutism, acne, and irregular bleeding overlap?
- Why does PCOS happen?
- Why does testosterone matter in all sexes, but differently?
- Why do menopause and ovarian insufficiency cause systemic changes?
- How do contraception, fertility drugs, anti-androgens, testosterone therapy, and menopause hormone therapy work?
- Why does pregnancy change nearly every endocrine reference point?

## 10. Why Does The Body Grow And Mature?

- What determines normal growth?
- Why do children grow in phases?
- How do growth hormone and IGF-1 differ?
- Why is growth hormone hard to measure directly?
- Why does growth hormone excess cause gigantism or acromegaly?
- Why does growth hormone deficiency look different in children and adults?
- How do thyroid hormone, cortisol, sex steroids, nutrition, sleep, chronic disease, and genetics affect growth?
- Why does bone age matter?
- How are short stature, tall stature, delayed puberty, and early puberty evaluated?
- Why can treating growth or puberty at the wrong time change adult outcomes?

## 11. Why Does Calcium Have To Be So Tightly Controlled?

- Why is calcium biologically essential and dangerous at the same time?
- What do PTH, vitamin D, calcitonin, FGF23, kidney, gut, and bone each do?
- Why does low calcium cause tingling, cramps, tetany, seizures, or arrhythmias?
- Why does high calcium cause stones, bone pain, abdominal symptoms, mood changes, and arrhythmias?
- Why does vitamin D deficiency cause bone and mineral problems?
- Why does kidney disease disrupt calcium, phosphate, PTH, and vitamin D?
- Why does osteoporosis happen?
- Why do fractures reflect bone strength, not just bone density?
- How do bisphosphonates, denosumab, anabolic bone drugs, vitamin D, calcium, calcimimetics, and parathyroid surgery work?

## 12. Why Do Appetite, Weight, And Metabolism Resist Simple Control?

- Why is body weight biologically defended?
- How do leptin, ghrelin, insulin, GLP-1, PYY, CCK, cortisol, thyroid hormone, sex hormones, sleep, and the brain interact?
- Why is obesity not just too many calories, even though energy balance still matters?
- Why does weight loss trigger hunger and reduced energy expenditure?
- Why do some endocrine diseases cause weight gain while others cause weight loss?
- Why do GLP-1 and GIP-based drugs work?
- Why does bariatric surgery affect hormones, not just stomach size?
- How do lipids, fatty liver, insulin resistance, inflammation, and cardiovascular risk connect?
- Which weight-loss, metabolism, and hormone claims are misleading?

## 13. What Happens When Endocrine Cells Become Tumors?

- Why do endocrine tumors often produce hormones?
- What is the difference between hyperplasia, adenoma, carcinoma, and neuroendocrine tumor?
- Why do tiny tumors sometimes cause huge syndromes?
- Why are pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, parathyroid, pancreatic, and neuroendocrine tumors so different?
- What are incidentalomas, and why are they not automatically harmless?
- What are MEN syndromes?
- Why do some endocrine tumors need biochemical diagnosis before biopsy or surgery?
- How do imaging, functional scans, genetics, surgery, radiation, ablation, and medical suppression fit together?

## 14. What Is Dangerous?

- What should never be casually self-treated?
- Why are adrenal crisis, thyroid storm, myxedema coma, DKA, HHS, severe hypoglycemia, severe hypercalcemia, pituitary apoplexy, pheochromocytoma crisis, and severe hyponatremia emergencies?
- Which symptoms suggest endocrine disease is becoming unstable?
- Why can giving the wrong hormone at the wrong time be harmful?
- Why can stopping chronic steroid therapy abruptly be dangerous?
- Why does pregnancy make some endocrine problems urgent?
- When does delay cause permanent harm?
- Which endocrine symptoms are common and nonspecific, and which should trigger urgent evaluation?

## 15. How Do Endocrine Treatments Actually Work?

- When do we replace a missing hormone?
- When do we block a hormone?
- When do we suppress a gland?
- When do we stimulate a gland?
- When do we remove, ablate, or irradiate tissue?
- Why do dose, timing, route, half-life, binding, conversion, and monitoring matter?
- Why do some endocrine treatments require slow titration?
- Why do pregnancy, age, kidney disease, liver disease, heart disease, and acute illness change treatment choices?
- How do clinicians decide whether success means symptom improvement, lab normalization, risk reduction, fertility, growth, fracture prevention, or crisis prevention?
- Why is long-term follow-up part of the treatment rather than an afterthought?
