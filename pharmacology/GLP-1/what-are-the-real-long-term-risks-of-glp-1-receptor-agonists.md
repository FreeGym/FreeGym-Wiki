# What Are the Real Long-Term Risks of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists?

GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) have a well-established gastrointestinal side-effect profile. The harder question is whether they also cause rare or delayed harms, particularly thyroid cancer, pancreatitis, persistent gastroparesis, and gallbladder disease, and whether those risks are justified by the benefits of treatment. These are the main competing claims evaluated here. This is a focused review of those four outcomes rather than a comprehensive inventory of every recognized adverse effect. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722); [Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))

A 2026 umbrella review of 60 meta-analyses included source analyses that, in aggregate, reported 1,751 randomized trials and 3,580,616 participants. Because primary trials overlapped across the meta-analyses, those totals should not be interpreted as counts of unique trials or participants. The clearest adverse effects were nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Evidence for pancreatitis, biliary disease, and cancer was less conclusive. Follow-up in the included evidence ranged from three months to 5.4 years or longer, which is useful for identifying common adverse effects but cannot definitively exclude very rare cancers or other outcomes with long latency.

_Funding note: The umbrella review was funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China, and the authors reported no conflicts of interest._

## Thyroid C-cell tumors: a real rodent finding, not a confirmed human risk

The thyroid concern originated from animal studies showing C-cell hyperplasia with GLP-1 receptor stimulation and thyroid C-cell tumors with semaglutide in rodents. The biological relevance of those findings to human thyroid tissue remains uncertain.

A multinational cohort study compared 98,147 GLP-1 RA users with approximately 2.49 million users of DPP-4 inhibitors across six countries. GLP-1 RA use was not associated with increased thyroid cancer during a median follow-up of approximately 1.8 to 3.0 years: the pooled weighted hazard ratio was 0.81, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.59 to 1.12. The study could not reliably examine medullary thyroid cancer separately, however, and its follow-up was too short to exclude a much later effect.

A separate target-trial emulation included 351,913 adults beginning one of four diabetes drug classes. Thyroid cancer was diagnosed in 0.17 percent of GLP-1 RA initiators, compared with 0.17 to 0.23 percent of the comparator groups. The overall modified intention-to-treat association was not statistically significant: hazard ratio 1.24, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.88 to 1.76. An increase was observed during the first year after treatment initiation, with a hazard ratio of 1.85 and a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.11 to 3.08. The corresponding estimates were 1.27, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.60 to 2.74, during years 1 to 2 and 0.78, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.43 to 1.42, at two or more years. An as-treated analysis also found an association, with a hazard ratio of 2.07 and a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.10 to 3.95. Thyroid ultrasonography was used more frequently during the early period. The authors considered increased surveillance and case detection a plausible explanation.

De novo thyroid carcinogenesis would not generally be expected to produce an excess of diagnosed cancers concentrated within the first year of exposure. The early concentration of diagnoses, combined with increased ultrasound use, therefore makes detection bias a plausible explanation, although it does not prove that explanation or exclude acceleration of pre-existing disease. ([Brito et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoto.2024.4852))

The best-supported conclusion is that the rodent C-cell signal is genuine, but a sustained increase in overall human thyroid cancer has not been established. Current studies are reassuring about risk over the first several years, but they do not include enough medullary thyroid cancers or sufficiently long follow-up to prove that the very-long-term risk is zero. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s033lbl.pdf); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722); [Baxter et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2024.0387); [Brito et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoto.2024.4852))

_Funding note: The target-trial research was funded by the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute. Dr Singh Ospina was supported by the National Cancer Institute. One author reported outside grants from Novo Nordisk and advisory or consulting relationships with Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly; those relationships were not the study's source of funding._

## Pancreatitis: conflicting evidence and a possible small increase

The claim that pancreatitis is clearly elevated and clinically significant goes beyond what the current evidence supports. ([Wen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70113); [Faour et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajmo.2025.100114); [Ayoub et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14030944); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722))

A 2025 systematic review of 62 randomized trials, involving 66,232 participants with a mean follow-up of approximately 43.5 weeks, found a modest increase in pancreatitis overall: risk ratio 1.44, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.09 to 1.89. When the trials were divided according to background medication use, the subgroup estimates remained directionally similar but were less precise and were not statistically significant. That loss of statistical significance does not demonstrate that the association disappeared or that background medication explained it. Pancreatitis was rare, and the statistical treatment of trials with no pancreatitis events adds uncertainty to any pooled estimate. The analysis also included tirzepatide and retatrutide, which act at more than one receptor, so it is best interpreted as evidence on GLP-1-based therapies rather than as a pure GLP-1 RA class estimate.

A large Veterans Health Administration study used a new-user, active-comparator design and matched 88,972 GLP-1 RA initiators to 88,972 DPP-4 inhibitor initiators. The mean follow-up was 923 days for GLP-1 RA users and 917 days for DPP-4 inhibitor users. Acute pancreatitis occurred in 0.31 percent of GLP-1 RA users and 0.24 percent of DPP-4 inhibitor users, corresponding to an odds ratio of 1.28. The absolute difference over that approximately 2.5-year follow-up was 0.07 percentage points. The same study also reported more pancreatic cancer diagnoses among GLP-1 RA users, but that outcome is outside the four outcomes evaluated here and cannot establish causation given the observational design and limited latency. The population consisted predominantly of older male veterans, and people with previous pancreatitis, alcohol-related diagnoses, or pancreatic disorders were excluded, limiting generalizability to higher-risk groups.

A separate 2025 propensity-score-matched analysis compared 81,872 GLP-1 RA users with 81,872 nonusers in a selected population with type 2 diabetes and without several major pancreatitis risk factors. It found no higher pancreatitis risk at 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years. The study's restrictive eligibility criteria and nonuser comparison limit its applicability to people with greater baseline risk and prevent it from settling the class-wide question. ([Ayoub et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14030944)) The 2026 umbrella review classified pancreatitis as an unresolved and exploratory safety signal rather than a confirmed causal class effect. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722))

The most defensible conclusion is that a large class-wide increase in pancreatitis has not been demonstrated. A small increase cannot be excluded, and pancreatitis can be serious even when it is rare. The evidence cited here is especially limited for people with previous pancreatitis because the Veterans Health Administration analysis excluded them, and the matched nonuser analysis selected a low-comorbidity population. ([Wen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70113); [Faour et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajmo.2025.100114); [Ayoub et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14030944); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722))

_Funding note: The Veterans Health Administration analysis used VA and HCA Healthcare research resources rather than funding from a GLP-1 manufacturer. The authors reported no relevant financial conflicts._ ([Faour et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajmo.2025.100114))

## Delayed gastric emptying, gastroparesis, and persistence are different questions

Discussion of gastroparesis and severe gastrointestinal effects often combines three separate questions. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6); [Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704); [American College of Gastroenterology clinical guideline](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000001874))

1. Do GLP-1 RAs delay gastric emptying?

2. Are they associated with a higher incidence of clinically diagnosed gastroparesis?

3. Do abnormal motility or symptoms persist after the medication is discontinued?


The evidence provides a considerably clearer answer to the first question than to the other two. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6); [Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704); [American College of Gastroenterology clinical guideline](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000001874))

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 prospective studies, including eight randomized trials and 300 participants, estimated that GLP-1 RAs prolonged gastric-emptying half-time by an average of 74 minutes. The direction of effect supports delayed gastric emptying, but the 74-minute estimate should not be treated as a universal class average. The effect appeared greater early in treatment and with short-acting drugs, although these subgroup patterns were exploratory. The underlying studies were small and highly heterogeneous, most lasted less than six months, and the certainty of the estimated magnitude was rated very low.

Delayed gastric emptying is a physiological measurement; it is not automatically equivalent to symptomatic gastroparesis. The [American College of Gastroenterology clinical guideline](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000001874) characterizes gastroparesis by compatible symptoms and objective evidence of delayed gastric emptying in the absence of mechanical obstruction. Claims-database diagnoses do not necessarily establish all of those elements.

A 2025 retrospective cohort study examined 55,460 adults with obesity but without diabetes. Diagnosed gastroparesis occurred at rates of 6.5 per 1,000 person-years among semaglutide users, 2.1 per 1,000 person-years among bupropion-naltrexone users, and 1.1 per 1,000 person-years after sleeve gastrectomy. After adjustment, the hazard ratio was 3.33 for semaglutide versus bupropion-naltrexone and 6.14 for semaglutide versus sleeve gastrectomy.

This is a meaningful safety signal, but the study identified gastroparesis through insurance diagnosis codes rather than standardized gastric-emptying tests. The treatment groups differed substantially at baseline, and mean follow-up was 1.0 year in the semaglutide group versus about 2 years in the comparator groups. The study therefore supports an association with diagnosed gastroparesis but cannot establish the incidence of objectively confirmed drug-induced gastroparesis. ([Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704); [American College of Gastroenterology clinical guideline](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000001874))

The available prospective gastric-emptying studies do not establish whether abnormal motility or symptoms persist after treatment is stopped. They measured gastric-emptying half-time during treatment periods ranging from days to months, and the meta-analysis did not report standardized post-discontinuation testing. It therefore cannot establish the frequency, duration, or causal attribution of persistent post-treatment gastroparesis. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6))

The evidence therefore supports three different conclusions:

- Delayed gastric emptying is an established pharmacological effect. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6))

- Clinically diagnosed gastroparesis appears uncommon in claims data but may occur more often among semaglutide users than among the comparators studied. ([Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704))

- The frequency, duration, and causal attribution of persistent gastroparesis after discontinuation remain unknown because the prospective studies were not designed to measure those outcomes after treatment withdrawal. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6))


_Funding note: The gastric-emptying meta-analysis used internal departmental funding and did not receive GLP-1 manufacturer funding. The claims-based gastroparesis study received database-access funding from Intuitive Surgical; two authors were employees and another was an intern at the company. Because sleeve gastrectomy was one of the comparators, the magnitude of that comparison should be interpreted with particular caution, even though the sponsor reportedly had no role in the study design or analysis._ ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6); [Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704))

## Gallbladder and biliary disease: the clearest uncommon organ-specific risk

Among the less common outcomes reviewed here, gallbladder disease has one of the strongest evidence bases. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 randomized trials involving 103,371 participants found that GLP-1 RA treatment increased the composite risk of gallbladder or biliary disease by 37 percent: risk ratio 1.37, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 1.23 to 1.52. The estimated absolute increase was 27 additional events per 10,000 treated people per year, or approximately 0.27 percentage points per year.

The same analysis found increased risks for:

- Cholelithiasis: risk ratio 1.27. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- Cholecystitis: risk ratio 1.36. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- Other biliary disease: risk ratio 1.55. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- Weight-loss trials: risk ratio 2.29, compared with 1.27 in diabetes or other indications. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- Higher doses: risk ratio 1.56, compared with 0.99 at lower doses. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- Treatment longer than 26 weeks: risk ratio 1.40, compared with 0.79 for shorter treatment.


Rapid or substantial weight loss may explain part of this association. However, saying that the risk comes from weight loss rather than the drug creates a false distinction: when medication causes rapid weight loss and the weight loss promotes gallstones, it is still a treatment-mediated adverse effect. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

There may also be a pathway independent of weight loss. GLP-1 activity can reduce gallbladder motility and delay gallbladder emptying, potentially through suppression of cholecystokinin secretion. Both weight-loss-mediated and direct motility effects may therefore contribute.

The increased gallbladder and biliary risk is supported by randomized-trial evidence, but the average absolute increase is modest. Individual risk may be greater with large or rapid weight loss, higher doses, or longer exposure; the meta-analysis did not quantify risk according to pre-existing gallstone susceptibility. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

_Funding note: The gallbladder meta-analysis was supported by public and academic grants. The authors reported no conflicts of interest, and the funders had no role in the analysis or publication decision._

## Is the safety profile acceptable given the benefits?

This depends heavily on why the medication is being used and the person's baseline health risk. ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))

The SELECT randomized trial enrolled 17,604 adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease but without diabetes. Over a mean follow-up of 39.8 months, major cardiovascular events occurred in 6.5 percent of participants assigned to semaglutide and 8.0 percent assigned to placebo. The hazard ratio was 0.80, representing an absolute reduction of 1.5 percentage points in this high-risk population.

The SELECT safety analysis illustrates the trade-off. Serious adverse events were less frequent with semaglutide than placebo, 33.4 versus 36.4 percent, largely because of fewer cardiovascular events. However, adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation occurred in 16.6 percent of the semaglutide group and 8.2 percent of the placebo group. Gastrointestinal disorders caused discontinuation in 10.0 versus 2.0 percent, and gallbladder-related disorders occurred in 2.8 versus 2.3 percent. Nonserious adverse events outside specified collection criteria were not systematically collected, so the lower serious-adverse-event rate should not be interpreted as evidence of better overall tolerability.

For a person with established cardiovascular disease and obesity who resembles the SELECT population, an absolute reduction in heart attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death may reasonably outweigh a modest gallbladder risk and a substantial chance of treatment-limiting gastrointestinal symptoms. The calculation is different for someone with a lower baseline probability of cardiovascular events, because the absolute benefit would be smaller even if the relative drug effect were similar. ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))

SELECT studied semaglutide 2.4 mg in a specific high-risk population. It does not prove the same benefit-risk balance for every GLP-1 RA, every dose, or use primarily for small amounts of weight loss. ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))

_Funding note: SELECT and its safety analysis were funded by Novo Nordisk, the manufacturer of semaglutide, and the investigator group included authors with relationships to the sponsor. The randomized, blinded design and adjudicated cardiovascular outcomes are important strengths, but the manufacturer funding should remain part of the interpretation._ ([Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))

## Quick reference values

- Common gastrointestinal effects in randomized-trial meta-analyses: nausea odds ratio 2.47; vomiting 2.78; diarrhea 1.94. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722))

- Thyroid cancer, 2026 umbrella review: odds ratio 1.43, 95 percent confidence interval 0.95 to 2.13; not statistically significant or robust. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722))

- Thyroid cancer, multinational cohort: hazard ratio 0.81, 95 percent confidence interval 0.59 to 1.12. ([Baxter et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2024.0387))

- Acute pancreatitis, matched veteran cohort over mean follow-up of approximately 2.5 years: 0.31 percent with GLP-1 RAs versus 0.24 percent with DPP-4 inhibitors; odds ratio 1.28. ([Faour et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajmo.2025.100114))

- Gastric-emptying half-time: estimated mean prolongation of 74 minutes, based on highly heterogeneous, very-low-certainty evidence; this is not a universal class average. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6))

- Diagnosed gastroparesis in adults with obesity but no diabetes: 6.5 per 1,000 person-years with semaglutide versus 2.1 with bupropion-naltrexone. ([Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704))

- Gallbladder or biliary disease: risk ratio 1.37; approximately 27 additional events per 10,000 people treated per year. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- SELECT cardiovascular outcome: 6.5 percent with semaglutide versus 8.0 percent with placebo over a mean of 39.8 months. ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563))

- SELECT treatment discontinuation because of adverse events: 16.6 percent with semaglutide versus 8.2 percent with placebo. ([Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))


## Evidence still needed

Longer active-comparator surveillance is needed to determine whether thyroid cancer incidence changes after substantially longer exposure. These studies need enough participants and validated pathology data to evaluate medullary thyroid cancer separately rather than grouping all thyroid cancers together. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722); [Baxter et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2024.0387); [Brito et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoto.2024.4852))

Pancreatitis studies need to include or separately analyze people with previous pancreatitis, gallstones, substantial alcohol exposure, severe hypertriglyceridemia, and other major baseline risk factors. Trials and observational studies should report adjudicated pancreatitis rather than relying only on diagnosis codes or nonspecific pancreatic-enzyme elevations. ([Wen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70113); [Faour et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajmo.2025.100114); [Ayoub et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14030944))

Gastrointestinal studies need standardized symptom assessments and objective gastric-emptying measurements before treatment, during treatment, and after discontinuation. Without a planned washout and follow-up period, the incidence and duration of persistent drug-associated gastroparesis cannot be established. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6); [American College of Gastroenterology clinical guideline](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000001874); [Aneke-Nash et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgast-2024-001704))

Gallbladder outcomes should be prespecified and reported consistently in weight-loss trials, including the amount and speed of weight loss, drug dose, exposure duration, gallstone history, and whether events required hospitalization or surgery. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

## Practical interpretation

- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and delayed gastric emptying are established treatment effects. Gastrointestinal symptoms are also a major reason people stop treatment. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722); [Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6); [Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))

- Gallbladder and biliary disease are established but uncommon risks. Rapid weight loss contributes, but it is unlikely to be the only mechanism. ([He et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338))

- A small pancreatitis increase remains possible, but the evidence is conflicting and does not establish a large, clinically important class-wide increase. ([Wen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70113); [Faour et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajmo.2025.100114); [Ayoub et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14030944); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722))

- A sustained increase in overall human thyroid cancer risk has not been confirmed. Current evidence is reassuring over several years but cannot exclude a very rare or substantially delayed effect, particularly for medullary thyroid cancer. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s033lbl.pdf); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.4722); [Baxter et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1089/thy.2024.0387); [Brito et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoto.2024.4852))

- Persistent gastroparesis after discontinuation has not been quantified in the prospective gastric-emptying studies reviewed here. Delayed emptying during treatment should not automatically be treated as proof of permanent gastric injury. ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12630-026-03114-6); [American College of Gastroenterology clinical guideline](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000001874))

- For people who resemble the SELECT population - adults with overweight or obesity, established cardiovascular disease, and no diabetes - semaglutide 2.4 mg had a favorable cardiovascular benefit-risk profile despite more treatment discontinuation and gallbladder events. That conclusion should not be generalized automatically to every patient, dose, molecule, or low-risk use. ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563); [Kushner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24222))
