# Fibrates for Triglyceride Lowering: Another Surrogate Endpoint Cautionary Tale

Fibrates are a useful test of the surrogate-endpoint problem because they reliably lower triglycerides and usually raise HDL cholesterol, while cardiovascular outcomes have varied substantially across drugs, trial populations, and background therapy. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20462635/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

The fairest conclusion is not that triglyceride-rich lipoproteins are harmless. Genetic evidence strongly implicates the remnant-lipoprotein pathway in atherosclerosis. The caution is narrower: **the available evidence does not support plasma triglyceride concentration alone as a drug-independent surrogate for cardiovascular benefit**. Lowering triglyceride mass can occur without reducing the concentration of circulating atherogenic particles, and therapies producing broadly similar triglyceride changes can have very different clinical outcomes. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

## What triglyceride concentration does and does not measure

A triglyceride result measures the amount of triglyceride cargo being transported in plasma. It does not directly count the number of atherogenic particles carrying that cargo. ([European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

Each VLDL, remnant, IDL, or LDL particle contains one molecule of apolipoprotein B. Plasma ApoB concentration therefore approximates the concentration of circulating atherogenic particles, whereas triglycerides and remnant cholesterol describe different components of their lipid content. A drug can lower triglycerides by removing whole particles, by unloading triglyceride from existing particles, or by accelerating conversion of larger triglyceride-rich particles into smaller cholesterol-containing particles. Triglyceride concentration alone cannot distinguish these possibilities. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

This distinction is central to the fibrate story. A fall in triglycerides is biologically informative, but it does not guarantee that fewer ApoB-containing particles are entering the arterial wall. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

## The early gemfibrozil trials were genuinely positive

The Helsinki Heart Study randomized 4,081 middle-aged men with dyslipidemia but no clinical coronary disease to gemfibrozil or placebo. Over five years, cardiac endpoints occurred at rates of 27.3 versus 41.4 per 1,000, corresponding to a 34% reduction, with a 95% confidence interval of 8.2% to 52.6%. Total mortality was not reduced. Gemfibrozil changed several lipid variables, including triglycerides, HDL, LDL, and non-HDL cholesterol, so the trial did not isolate triglyceride lowering as the causal mechanism. (Frick et al., 1987) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3313041/))

VA-HIT randomized 2,531 men with established coronary disease, low HDL cholesterol, and relatively low LDL cholesterol. Gemfibrozil lowered triglycerides by 31% and raised HDL by 6%, without significantly changing LDL. The primary event rate fell from 21.7% to 17.3%, a 22% relative reduction, with a 95% confidence interval of 7% to 35%, and a 4.4-percentage-point absolute reduction over a median of 5.1 years. All-cause mortality was not significantly reduced. (Rubins et al., 1999) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10438259/))

These were important clinical successes, not merely favorable subgroup analyses. But they were predominantly male, pre- or early-statin-era trials, and gemfibrozil altered more than triglyceride concentration. They established that gemfibrozil could prevent coronary events in selected populations; they did not establish that any drug producing the same triglyceride reduction would produce the same outcome. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3313041/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10438259/))

A subsequent fibrate meta-analysis found a 10% relative reduction in major cardiovascular events and a 13% reduction in coronary events overall, but no clear reduction in stroke, cardiovascular mortality, or all-cause mortality. The pooled trials spanned treatment eras, including several conducted before contemporary LDL-lowering therapy became routine, so the overall estimate does not directly answer the effect of adding a fibrate to modern statin therapy. (Jun et al., 2010) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20462635/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

## FIELD: a null primary endpoint with favorable secondary signals

FIELD randomized 9,795 people with type 2 diabetes who were not taking a statin at study entry to fenofibrate or placebo. The primary outcome, coronary death or nonfatal myocardial infarction, occurred in 5.2% of the fenofibrate group and 5.9% of the placebo group, a nonsignificant result: hazard ratio 0.89, 95% confidence interval 0.75 to 1.05. (Keech et al., 2005) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16310551/))

A broader secondary composite of total cardiovascular events was reduced from 13.9% to 12.5%, hazard ratio 0.89, with fewer nonfatal myocardial infarctions and revascularizations. Interpretation was complicated by unequal initiation of other lipid-lowering treatment: approximately 17% of placebo participants versus 8% of fenofibrate participants started additional therapy, predominantly statins. That crossover may have narrowed the treatment contrast, but it does not convert the prespecified primary result into a positive trial. (Keech et al., 2005) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16310551/))

A subsequent FIELD analysis identified participants with triglycerides of at least 2.3 mmol/L, approximately 204 mg/dL, plus low HDL cholesterol. Their five-year cardiovascular event risk was 17.8%, and fenofibrate was associated with a 27% relative reduction in total cardiovascular events, hazard ratio 0.73, 95% confidence interval 0.58 to 0.91, with a number needed to treat of 23. However, the treatment-by-subgroup interaction had a P value of 0.053. The finding therefore suggested, but did not establish, differential benefit in this phenotype and remains a subgroup result rather than the result of a trial randomized specifically within that phenotype. (Scott et al., 2009) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2646035/))

FIELD received support from Laboratoires Fournier, the fenofibrate manufacturer, as well as Australian public research funding; the study was reported as being coordinated independently. The commercial support does not invalidate the results, but it belongs in their interpretation. ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1266033/))

## ACCORD-Lipid: no benefit from routine fenofibrate addition

ACCORD-Lipid tested a more contemporary question: whether fenofibrate adds cardiovascular benefit to statin therapy. It randomized 5,518 people with type 2 diabetes receiving simvastatin to fenofibrate or placebo. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/))

After a mean of 4.7 years, the annual primary-event rate was 2.2% with fenofibrate and 2.4% with placebo, hazard ratio 0.92, 95% confidence interval 0.79 to 1.08. No prespecified secondary cardiovascular outcome was significantly improved. The trial therefore did not support routine fenofibrate addition for the broad statin-treated diabetic population. (ACCORD Study Group, 2010) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/))

The high-triglyceride, low-HDL subgroup was more provocative. Participants with triglycerides above 204 mg/dL and HDL below 34 mg/dL appeared to benefit, but the treatment-by-subgroup interaction had a P value of 0.057. This provided weak but statistically inconclusive evidence of treatment-effect heterogeneity, and multiplicity and sampling variation limit confidence in the finding. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/))

Extended ACCORDION follow-up preserved the overall neutral result while again finding a lower event rate among participants who had entered the trial with this dyslipidemic phenotype, hazard ratio 0.73, 95% confidence interval 0.56 to 0.95. This was post-trial follow-up of the original randomized groups, not an independent randomized confirmation. The investigators themselves concluded that a dedicated trial was needed to confirm the finding. It therefore remained a plausible hypothesis, not definitive evidence of a treatment-responsive subgroup. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2594262))

ACCORD was funded principally by the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute. Abbott donated fenofibrate and placebo, and Merck donated simvastatin; the manufacturers were reported to have had no role in trial conduct or analysis. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1001282))

## PROMINENT was the strongest prospective test of the subgroup hypothesis with a selective PPAR-alpha modulator

PROMINENT enrolled 10,497 people with type 2 diabetes, triglycerides of 200 to 499 mg/dL, low HDL cholesterol, and well-controlled LDL cholesterol, with most receiving statin therapy. In other words, it deliberately enrolled approximately the phenotype that had looked favorable in the FIELD and ACCORD analyses. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2646035/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/))

Pemafibrate produced substantial biochemical changes. Compared with placebo, it lowered triglycerides by 26.2%, VLDL cholesterol by 25.8%, remnant cholesterol by 25.6%, and ApoC-III by 27.6%. Yet ApoB increased by 4.8%. The cardiovascular endpoint was unchanged: hazard ratio 1.03, 95% confidence interval 0.91 to 1.15. The trial was terminated early for futility, with no persuasive rescue in the prespecified subgroups. Pemafibrate was also associated with more adverse renal events and venous thromboembolism and fewer diagnoses of nonalcoholic fatty-liver disease. (Das Pradhan et al., 2022) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

PROMINENT therefore weakens the argument that the high-triglyceride, low-HDL subgroup has already been shown to receive cardiovascular benefit from fibrates. Earlier subgroup analyses predicted benefit; a large trial designed around that phenotype did not confirm it. Because pemafibrate is a selective PPAR-alpha modulator rather than an interchangeable substitute for every older fibrate, the result is strongest against a class-wide subgroup claim, not proof that every fibrate molecule must behave identically. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

The ApoB result offers a possible explanation. In absolute terms, pemafibrate lowered remnant cholesterol by approximately 7 mg/dL but increased LDL cholesterol by approximately 10 mg/dL and ApoB by approximately 5 mg/dL. A 2024 analysis combined those changes with observational data from a PROMINENT-like Copenhagen cohort and estimated an event hazard ratio of 1.05, 95% confidence interval 0.96 to 1.14, close to PROMINENT's observed 1.03. This is consistent with the interpretation that triglyceride-rich particles were remodeled without a net reduction in total atherogenic particle burden. It is nevertheless an observational modeling exercise, not randomized proof of the mechanism. (Doi et al., 2024) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38678642/))

The modeling paper also disclosed relevant industry relationships, including relationships with Kowa and Amarin. PROMINENT itself was funded by Kowa Research Institute, a subsidiary of the manufacturer of pemafibrate. These conflicts should be noted, although the trial's unequivocally neutral result is not commercially favorable. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38678642/))

## Does the atherogenic-dyslipidemia subgroup still survive?

There is a coherent case for the subgroup. A meta-analysis of five large fibrate trials identified 4,671 participants with high triglycerides and low HDL and estimated a 30% cardiovascular risk reduction in that phenotype, compared with a nonsignificant 6% reduction in participants without atherogenic dyslipidemia. The analysis used trial-specific lipid cutoffs across heterogeneous older trials, which limits its confirmatory weight. (Bruckert et al., 2011) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/))

But that meta-analysis was based on subdivisions of older trials and preceded PROMINENT. PROMINENT was much closer to the confirmatory experiment the subgroup hypothesis required, and it was neutral. Differences among gemfibrozil, fenofibrate, and pemafibrate, statin use, achieved ApoB changes, trial populations, and endpoint definitions leave some residual uncertainty, but the present evidence does **not** establish fibrates as an outcome-improving treatment for statin-treated patients solely because they have high triglycerides and low HDL. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

The subgroup should therefore be described as **unconfirmed**, rather than either accepted or definitively disproved. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

## REDUCE-IT and STRENGTH show why triglyceride change alone is insufficient

REDUCE-IT randomized 8,179 statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides and either established cardiovascular disease or diabetes plus additional risk factors to icosapent ethyl, 4 g per day, or mineral-oil placebo. Over a median of 4.9 years, the primary endpoint occurred in 17.2% versus 22.0%, hazard ratio 0.75, 95% confidence interval 0.68 to 0.83. Hospitalization for atrial fibrillation or flutter was more frequent, 3.1% versus 2.1%, and serious bleeding was numerically higher, 2.7% versus 2.1%, P = 0.06. (Bhatt et al., 2019) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/))

STRENGTH enrolled 13,078 similarly high-risk, statin-treated patients with high triglycerides and low HDL. It tested a carboxylic-acid formulation containing both EPA and DHA against corn oil. The primary endpoint occurred in 12.0% versus 12.2%, hazard ratio 0.99, 95% confidence interval 0.90 to 1.09, and the trial was stopped for futility. (Nicholls et al., 2020) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

The placebo-adjusted triglyceride differences at 12 months were roughly 18% to 20% in both trials. The event results nevertheless diverged sharply. In STRENGTH, ApoB also did not differ significantly between groups despite the triglyceride reduction. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

Several explanations remain possible:

- REDUCE-IT used purified EPA, whereas STRENGTH used EPA plus DHA. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

- The formulations and achieved EPA and DHA exposures differed. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

- REDUCE-IT used mineral oil as placebo, whereas STRENGTH used corn oil. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

- The populations and durations of exposure were not identical. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

- EPA may have membrane, plaque, inflammatory, antithrombotic, or electrophysiologic effects that are not captured by triglyceride concentration. (Mason et al., 2020) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32212849/))


Because REDUCE-IT and STRENGTH were separate trials, their comparison cannot isolate the roles of EPA purity, DHA, comparator oil, achieved drug exposure, population differences, or other intervention-specific effects. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

A REDUCE-IT biomarker substudy found that several inflammatory and atherosclerosis-related biomarkers, as well as LDL cholesterol, rose over time in the mineral-oil group while changing minimally in the icosapent group. The investigators explicitly concluded that the implications for the clinical-event result were uncertain. Mineral oil could have enlarged the observed treatment difference, but available analyses cannot show that it explains the full 25% relative reduction. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35762321/))

Nor does STRENGTH prove that EPA itself is ineffective. It shows that this particular EPA-plus-DHA formulation did not prevent events. Conversely, REDUCE-IT proves the efficacy of **icosapent ethyl in the population and regimen studied**; it does not prove that triglyceride lowering in general is beneficial. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

Both trials were industry funded. REDUCE-IT was funded by Amarin Pharma. STRENGTH was funded by AstraZeneca, which participated in study design and maintained the trial database. These disclosures are especially relevant when evaluating formulation-specific claims and the unresolved placebo debate. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

## What Mendelian randomization supports

Mendelian randomization studies support a causal role for triglyceride-rich lipoprotein metabolism, but they also help explain why triglyceride concentration can be an unreliable pharmacologic surrogate. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265341/); [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770))

A Copenhagen study found that a genetically predicted 39 mg/dL increase in remnant cholesterol was associated with an estimated 2.8-fold higher risk of ischemic heart disease. The authors cautioned that pleiotropy could not be completely excluded and called for confirmation through additional genetic instruments and randomized trials. (Varbo et al., 2013) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265341/))

A larger analysis of 654,783 participants compared triglyceride-lowering variants in the lipoprotein-lipase pathway with LDL-lowering variants in the LDL-receptor pathway. The two pathways were associated with almost identical reductions in coronary risk per 10 mg/dL lower ApoB. In multivariable analyses, the associations of triglycerides and LDL cholesterol became null after accounting for ApoB, while ApoB remained associated with risk. (Ference et al., 2019) ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770))

This does not mean that triglycerides are biologically irrelevant. It suggests that the clinically important feature may be the concentration of ApoB-containing particles generated by triglyceride-rich lipoprotein metabolism, rather than the quantity of triglyceride carried inside them at one moment. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

Genetic exposure is lifelong, whereas drug treatment begins later and may produce additional metabolic effects. Mendelian randomization therefore identifies promising pathways; it cannot guarantee that every drug altering one biomarker within that pathway will improve outcomes. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770))

## ApoB and remnant cholesterol are more informative - but not interchangeable

The fibrate evidence supports greater use of ApoB and remnant-related measurements when interpreting hypertriglyceridemia. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38678642/))

ApoB estimates total atherogenic particle concentration across VLDL, remnants, IDL, and LDL. It can reveal residual particle burden when LDL cholesterol appears controlled or when triglyceride-rich particles contain relatively little cholesterol per particle. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

Remnant cholesterol estimates the cholesterol carried in triglyceride-rich remnant particles. It is closer to the proposed arterially retained component of the triglyceride-rich lipoprotein pathway than triglyceride mass itself, but it is frequently calculated rather than directly measured and does not count particles. ([European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

Calculated remnant cholesterol is commonly defined as total cholesterol minus HDL cholesterol minus LDL cholesterol. When LDL cholesterol is itself calculated with the Friedewald equation, the resulting remnant cholesterol equals triglycerides divided by 5 in mg/dL and is therefore not an independent measurement of triglycerides. Other LDL-estimation methods do not impose that exact identity, but calculated remnant cholesterol still inherits error from the LDL estimate. (Szili-Torok and Tietge, 2021) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33832613/))

PROMINENT illustrates why ApoB and remnant cholesterol should be considered together: remnant cholesterol fell, but ApoB increased. The post-trial modeling suggests that the modest remnant-cholesterol reduction was offset by increases in LDL cholesterol and total particle concentration. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38678642/))

It is therefore reasonable to argue that ApoB and remnant cholesterol provide more outcome-relevant information than triglycerides alone. They are not interchangeable: ApoB reflects atherogenic particle concentration, whereas remnant cholesterol reflects cholesterol mass within a triglyceride-rich fraction. It is not yet established that a formal treat-to-ApoB or treat-to-remnant strategy would outperform all triglyceride-based strategies, because the fibrate trials did not directly randomize patients between those measurement approaches. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38678642/))

## Severe hypertriglyceridemia and pancreatitis are a different question

Cardiovascular residual-risk trials generally enrolled people with moderately elevated triglycerides. Their results should not be directly transferred to patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia, in whom the immediate clinical concern may be acute pancreatitis rather than atherosclerosis. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34332805/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910758/))

Fibrates substantially lower triglycerides and remain a guideline-supported option for severe hypertriglyceridemia. However, the direct randomized evidence that fibrates prevent pancreatitis is much weaker than is often implied. In a meta-analysis of seven fibrate cardiovascular trials involving 40,162 participants, pancreatitis occurred in 84 participants assigned to fibrates and 60 assigned to placebo, relative risk 1.39, 95% confidence interval 1.00 to 1.95, P = 0.053. Baseline triglycerides in those trials were only approximately 145 to 184 mg/dL. Those data are not a valid test of treatment in patients with triglycerides of 500, 1,000, or several thousand mg/dL. (Preiss et al., 2012) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34332805/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910758/))

The most accurate formulation is therefore:

**Fibrates are used to lower very high triglycerides in an effort to reduce pancreatitis risk, but direct pancreatitis-outcome evidence for fibrates in severely hypertriglyceridemic patients is limited.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34332805/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910758/))

Newer evidence shows that pancreatitis prevention can be demonstrated directly. In two trials involving 1,061 patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia, the ApoC-III antisense drug olezarsen markedly lowered triglycerides and reduced acute pancreatitis events, with a pooled rate ratio of 0.15, 95% confidence interval 0.05 to 0.40. Elevations in liver-enzyme levels and thrombocytopenia were more common with the 80-mg dose, and a dose-dependent increase in hepatic fat fraction was observed. These trials were funded by Ionis Pharmaceuticals. The result supports triglyceride-rich lipoprotein lowering in the severe-pancreatitis setting, but it does not establish a fibrate effect and does not answer the separate ASCVD question. (Marston et al., 2026) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41211918/))

On June 24, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved olezarsen, as an adjunct to diet, to reduce triglycerides and the risk of acute pancreatitis in adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia, defined as fasting triglycerides of at least 500 mg/dL. This approval directly reflects the pancreatitis-outcome evidence and does not alter the separate conclusion about triglycerides as a cardiovascular surrogate. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-treatment-shown-reduce-risk-acute-pancreatitis-adults-severe-hypertriglyceridemia))

## Evidence-weighted verdict on the competing claims

### 1. The magnitude of triglyceride lowering alone is insufficient to predict cardiovascular benefit

**Strongly supported.**

REDUCE-IT and STRENGTH produced roughly similar triglyceride reductions, yet one reduced cardiovascular events by 25% and the other was neutral. PROMINENT lowered triglycerides by 26% but increased ApoB and was neutral. These results show that percentage triglyceride reduction is not sufficient to predict cardiovascular benefit. Particle removal, ApoB change, drug-specific effects, adverse effects, comparator choice, and trial context all matter. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

REDUCE-IT and PROMINENT did not produce numerically identical triglyceride reductions, but both produced substantial reductions that were disproportionate to their very different event results. Cross-trial comparisons establish that the triglyceride change alone is insufficient; they do not identify which mechanistic differences caused the divergent outcomes. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

### 2. Fibrates retain specific roles despite the null broad trials

**Partly supported.**

Their role in severe hypertriglyceridemia remains reasonable, but the pancreatitis-prevention evidence for fibrates is indirect. The high-triglyceride, low-HDL cardiovascular subgroup is biologically plausible and supported by repeated subgroup observations, but PROMINENT substantially weakened the claim that this phenotype identifies patients who reliably benefit from PPAR-alpha modulation. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34332805/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910758/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

Fenofibrate also has a separate, randomized evidence base for slowing progression of early diabetic retinopathy. In the LENS trial, progression to referable diabetic retinopathy or maculopathy, or treatment for either condition, occurred in 22.7% with fenofibrate versus 29.2% with placebo, hazard ratio 0.73, 95% confidence interval 0.58 to 0.91. This is a noncardiovascular role and does not change the ASCVD conclusion. (Preiss et al., 2024) ([NEJM Evidence](https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2400179))

Broad rejection of fibrates would therefore be too strong, but broad cardiovascular endorsement of them in the high-triglyceride, low-HDL subgroup would also exceed the evidence. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34332805/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [NEJM Evidence](https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2400179))

### 3. ApoB and remnant cholesterol should receive more attention than triglycerides alone

**Supported for risk interpretation, not yet proven as a treatment-target strategy.**

Genetic evidence and PROMINENT both suggest that net change in ApoB-containing particle concentration is more predictive than the triglyceride percentage change. Remnant cholesterol adds information about cholesterol mass in the triglyceride-rich pathway, while ApoB integrates particle concentration across all atherogenic lipoproteins. Neither marker should be treated as interchangeable with the other. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38678642/))

Neither marker should be presented as a perfect standalone answer, and the superiority of a formal ApoB- or remnant-targeted treatment algorithm has not been demonstrated by the fibrate trials. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

### 4. REDUCE-IT does not vindicate triglyceride lowering as a generic mechanism

**Strongly supported.**

REDUCE-IT validates icosapent ethyl, not every triglyceride-lowering drug or omega-3 formulation. STRENGTH's null result despite similar triglyceride lowering makes an intervention-specific effect plausible. The mineral-oil comparator creates additional uncertainty, but available evidence does not establish that mineral oil explains the complete REDUCE-IT result. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35762321/))

- **Helsinki Heart Study:** gemfibrozil reduced coronary events by 34%; no total-mortality reduction. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3313041/))

- **VA-HIT:** gemfibrozil lowered triglycerides by 31% and reduced the primary event rate by 22%. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10438259/))

- **FIELD:** primary coronary endpoint not significantly reduced; a broader secondary cardiovascular composite improved modestly. The marked-dyslipidemia subgroup interaction was borderline. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16310551/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2646035/))

- **ACCORD-Lipid:** adding fenofibrate to simvastatin did not significantly reduce cardiovascular events overall; the high-triglyceride, low-HDL subgroup remained hypothesis-generating. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/))

- **PROMINENT:** triglycerides and remnant cholesterol fell substantially, ApoB rose, and cardiovascular events were unchanged. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

- **REDUCE-IT:** icosapent ethyl reduced the primary endpoint by 25%. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/))

- **STRENGTH:** EPA-plus-DHA lowered triglycerides but did not reduce cardiovascular events. ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

- **LENS:** fenofibrate reduced progression of early diabetic retinopathy or maculopathy. ([NEJM Evidence](https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2400179))

- **CORE-TIMI 72a and CORE2-TIMI 72b:** olezarsen reduced acute pancreatitis in severe hypertriglyceridemia and received FDA approval for that indication in June 2026. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41211918/); [U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-treatment-shown-reduce-risk-acute-pancreatitis-adults-severe-hypertriglyceridemia))


## Practical interpretation

- Triglyceride lowering should not be assumed to reduce cardiovascular events unless the specific intervention has outcome-trial evidence. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

- In statin-treated patients with diabetes and moderately elevated triglycerides, routine fenofibrate or pemafibrate addition is not supported for broad cardiovascular-event prevention. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

- High triglycerides plus low HDL identify higher-risk patients, but they do not yet identify a definitively fibrate-responsive cardiovascular subgroup. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21052016/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20228404/); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

- ApoB is the most direct routinely available marker of total atherogenic particle concentration. Non-HDL cholesterol measures cholesterol mass in all non-HDL particles, and remnant cholesterol estimates cholesterol mass in triglyceride-rich particles; neither cholesterol measure counts particles or is interchangeable with ApoB. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485))

- Severe hypertriglyceridemia should be treated as a separate pancreatitis-prevention problem. Fibrates remain a practical triglyceride-lowering option, but the direct pancreatitis-outcome evidence for them is limited. Olezarsen now has randomized pancreatitis-outcome evidence and FDA approval for adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34332805/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910758/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41211918/); [U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-treatment-shown-reduce-risk-acute-pancreatitis-adults-severe-hypertriglyceridemia))

- Fenofibrate has randomized evidence for slowing progression of early diabetic retinopathy, a separate non-ASCVD role. ([NEJM Evidence](https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2400179))

- REDUCE-IT should be interpreted as evidence for prescription icosapent ethyl in the studied population, not as evidence for omega-3 supplements, EPA-plus-DHA formulations, or triglyceride lowering in general. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))


The fibrate story therefore does not falsify the causal importance of triglyceride-rich remnants. It shows why **a causal pathway, a measurable biomarker, and a successful drug target are not the same thing**. For cardiovascular prevention, the most consistent interpretation is that therapies must reduce arterial exposure to ApoB-containing particles, or provide another independently beneficial effect, rather than merely lower the triglyceride number on the laboratory report. ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2722770); [European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/42/47/4791/6362485); [New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628/); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))
