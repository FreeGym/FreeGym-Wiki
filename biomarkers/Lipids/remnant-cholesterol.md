# Remnant Cholesterol: The Real Culprit Hiding in Triglycerides?

Remnant cholesterol is a credible marker of atherogenic triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, but calling it the "real culprit" overstates what the evidence can separate. The best current synthesis is that cholesterol-bearing remnant particles contribute to atherosclerosis, while the total number of atherogenic apoB-containing particles remains at least as important clinically. Calculated remnant cholesterol can add context, especially when triglycerides are elevated, but it is not yet a standardized stand-alone treatment target. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))

## What remnant cholesterol is

In much of the clinical literature, remnant cholesterol means the cholesterol carried in triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and their smaller products, broadly including VLDL, IDL, and chylomicron remnants. This is an operational definition, not one cleanly standardized analyte: some methods include nascent VLDL, some include IDL, and most routine methods cannot prove that a measured particle has actually undergone lipolysis. "Triglyceride-rich lipoprotein/remnant cholesterol" is therefore the more precise description. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293))

The mechanistic case is plausible. Smaller VLDL and remnant particles can cross the arterial endothelium, become trapped in the intima, and deliver cholesterol to the developing plaque. The largest nascent chylomicrons are generally too large to enter, but their smaller remnants can. Each triglyceride-rich particle carries one apoB molecule, so remnant cholesterol describes the cholesterol mass in a particle population while apoB approximates the number of circulating atherogenic particles across VLDL, IDL, LDL, and other apoB-containing classes. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551))

It is reasonable to say that triglycerides are not the principal material retained in plaque. It is too strong to say that triglycerides are metabolically cleared "without lasting effect." Lipolysis of triglyceride-rich particles also releases fatty acids and other products that may affect endothelial and inflammatory pathways. High triglycerides are therefore a marker of greater triglyceride-rich-lipoprotein burden, reflecting variable combinations of particle number and triglyceride content, as well as part of a broader disturbed metabolic state. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551))

## What the standard calculation really calculates

Calculated remnant cholesterol is usually defined as:

**Remnant-C = Total-C - HDL-C - LDL-C**

The arithmetic is simple, but its meaning depends on how LDL-C was obtained. If the laboratory uses the Friedewald equation, then:

**LDL-C = Total-C - HDL-C - TG/5** in mg/dL

Therefore, when Friedewald LDL-C is actually reported:

**Remnant-C = TG/5** in mg/dL, or approximately **TG/2.2** in mmol/L.

In that common setting, calculated remnant cholesterol is not an independent value hidden in the lipid panel. It is triglycerides rescaled under a fixed assumption about the triglyceride-to-cholesterol ratio in VLDL. The value may still be easier to interpret as cholesterol mass, but it adds no new raw laboratory information. The Friedewald equation was classically intended for triglyceride concentrations below 400 mg/dL, and this algebraic identity neither validates the assumed VLDL triglyceride-to-cholesterol ratio in an individual nor applies when the laboratory uses another LDL-C method. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152); [Faridi et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2019.06.001))

With Martin-Hopkins LDL-C, the residual is effectively triglycerides divided by an adjustable factor rather than the fixed Friedewald factor of five. Sampson LDL-C produces a different nonlinear residual. If LDL-C is measured with a homogeneous assay, remnant cholesterol becomes non-HDL-C minus measured LDL-C and incorporates the assay-specific classification and analytic errors of total cholesterol, HDL-C, and LDL-C. None of these alternatives turns the residual into a measurement of metabolically identified remnant particles. ([Martin et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.280532); [Sampson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2020.0013); [Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293))

## What the epidemiology shows

A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 studies found that people in higher remnant-cholesterol categories had greater risks of cardiovascular disease, coronary disease, stroke, cardiovascular mortality, and all-cause mortality. For overall cardiovascular disease, the pooled relative risk for high versus low remnant cholesterol was 1.53. Most included evidence was observational, with heterogeneous populations, equations, cut points, and adjustment models, so this establishes a strong risk association rather than an independent treatment target. ([Yang et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2023.03.012))

One of the strongest tests of incremental association pooled ARIC, MESA, and CARDIA data from 17,532 adults followed for a median of 18.7 years. Calculated remnant cholesterol remained associated with atherosclerotic cardiovascular events after adjustment that included LDL-C and apoB, and participants with high remnant cholesterol despite lower LDL-C also had higher risk. The authors described the finding beyond apoB as surprising and called for mechanistic confirmation. Shared equation error, one-time lipid measurements, residual confounding, and the fact that association does not equal improved prediction all limit the conclusion. The cohorts were publicly funded, but several authors disclosed industry relationships, and two were coinventors on a pending LDL-C estimation patent. ([Quispe et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab432))

## What Mendelian randomization adds

The landmark Copenhagen study included 73,513 participants, 11,984 with ischemic heart disease, and 15 genetic variants. The instrumental-variable estimate was an odds ratio of 2.8, 95 percent confidence interval 1.9 to 4.2, for ischemic heart disease per genetically predicted 1 mmol/L, or 39 mg/dL, higher nonfasting calculated remnant cholesterol, compared with an observational hazard ratio of 1.4, 95 percent confidence interval 1.3 to 1.5. This supports a causal role for the biology represented by remnant cholesterol and reduces susceptibility to ordinary lifestyle confounding and reverse causation. It does not prove that remnant cholesterol itself, rather than correlated changes in particle number or composition, is the uniquely causal component. The authors explicitly noted that pleiotropy could not be excluded and called for confirmation with additional genetic instruments or randomized trials. ([Varbo et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.08.1026))

A related Copenhagen analysis associated genetically higher remnant cholesterol with both higher C-reactive protein and ischemic heart disease, while genetically higher LDL-C was associated with ischemic heart disease but not C-reactive protein. This is evidence about one systemic inflammatory marker, not proof that remnant cholesterol is the only inflammatory lipid exposure or that lowering it will reverse plaque inflammation. ([Varbo et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.113.003008))

More recent genetic analyses differ in what they imply about risk beyond apoB, although they did not model identical exposures. One UK Biobank multivariable Mendelian-randomization study estimated that triglyceride-rich lipoprotein/remnant cholesterol retained a strong association with coronary disease after adjustment for apoB in one model and when modeled alongside LDL-C in another. In contrast, other large multivariable genetic analyses using triglycerides as the triglyceride-rich-lipoprotein-related exposure, rather than calculated remnant cholesterol, found that coronary risk tracked the change in apoB particle number, with triglycerides or LDL-C adding little once apoB was accounted for. The later remnant-focused work was primarily publicly or academically funded, but several authors disclosed industry consulting relationships. The scientific dispute is therefore not whether apoB particles cause atherosclerosis, but whether remnant particles truly carry greater risk per particle than LDL, as Bjornson's SNP-cluster analysis inferred without directly measuring remnant-particle apoB, and whether current measurements can quantify that difference reliably. ([Bjornson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehad337); [Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045); [Richardson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062))

Mendelian randomization is powerful, but it is not randomization to a drug or laboratory target. It estimates the effects of lifelong genetically influenced exposure and can still be biased by horizontal pleiotropy, linkage, population structure, weak instruments, and collinearity among tightly related lipid traits. It strengthens the causal case for triglyceride-rich apoB particles, but it does not by itself validate calculated remnant cholesterol as the best clinical marker. ([Varbo et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.08.1026); [Richardson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062))

## Does it improve risk prediction beyond LDL-C and apoB?

There is evidence that very high calculated remnant cholesterol can reclassify some people beyond a conventional risk model that includes LDL-C. In 41,928 Danish primary-prevention participants, a prespecified value at or above the 95th percentile improved some threshold-based measures of myocardial-infarction risk classification. Across the entire remnant-cholesterol range, however, the change in discrimination was small: the myocardial-infarction C-index change was 0.003 and the net reclassification improvement was 0 percent. Adjustment for apoB or triglycerides and replacing LDL-C with non-HDL-C attenuated the gains. The cohort was White and Danish, and the prediction models were developed and assessed in the same cohort without reported internal validation or independent external validation. Two of the three authors disclosed industry relationships. ([Doi et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2022.03.384))

The defensible conclusion is narrower than "LDL-C misses the risk." Remnant cholesterol can identify a phenotype of residual risk, especially at extreme values or when LDL-C and triglyceride-rich lipoproteins are discordant. It has not established sufficient incremental clinical utility to replace non-HDL-C or apoB in routine risk assessment, and the 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety guideline does not recommend that substitution. ([Quispe et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab432); [Doi et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2022.03.384); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))

## Calculated versus specialized remnant-cholesterol assessment

"Direct remnant cholesterol" is not one standardized test. Ultracentrifugation separates particles by a chosen density boundary, not by metabolic history; immunoseparation captures selected remnant-like particles; homogeneous assays use reagent-specific selectivity; and NMR spectroscopy computationally assigns lipid signals to modeled particle-size classes. None can prove that a particle has undergone lipolysis. NMR is lipoprotein subclass profiling, not physical fractionation, and different platforms or investigator-selected subclasses can produce different remnant definitions. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Chen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-020-01311-w); [Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293))

A large Copenhagen comparison used a homogeneous assay traceable to cholesterol below density 1.019 g/mL, including IDL, while the calculated value approximated cholesterol below 1.006 g/mL. The methods therefore did not define the same fraction. Direct and calculated measures had similar associations with ischemic heart disease and myocardial infarction overall. A small discordant group with high direct but not high calculated remnant cholesterol had higher myocardial-infarction risk, but calculated remnant cholesterol produced the larger net reclassification improvement in that same study. The specialized assay was also performed on long-frozen samples whose values increased during storage, so the investigators used percentile groups rather than clinically transferable absolute cutoffs. This does not establish that the assay is generally more accurate or clinically superior. The study received Danish public and nonprofit funding. ([Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293))

Other comparisons show meaningful individual disagreement. In 1.35 million lipid profiles, estimated and ultracentrifugal remnant-like cholesterol correlated at 0.76. Among profiles with triglycerides below 355 mg/dL, Friedewald-derived estimated remnant-like-particle cholesterol and TG/5 correlated at exactly 1.00. In a separate 98-person study, a residual calculated with directly measured LDL-C correlated only moderately with NMR-assigned values. It tended to overestimate relative to that NMR definition in the lowest triglyceride quartile and underestimate in the highest quartile, which began around 233 mg/dL. This small study did not validate either method in severe hypertriglyceridemia. Because comparator methods define different particle fractions, the direction of disagreement is not universal. ([Faridi et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2019.06.001); [Chen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-020-01311-w))

The Denka TRL-C homogeneous assay identified some people with high cholesterol in its broader triglyceride-rich-lipoprotein and IDL fraction who were not classified as high by the residual calculation. More broadly, specialized remnant assessments define different particle fractions, and a consensus clinical measurement method has not been established. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Chen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-020-01311-w); [Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293))

## What the fibrate trials actually show

The claim that fibrates failed because they lowered triglycerides without lowering remnants is contradicted by PROMINENT. The trial randomized 10,497 adults with type 2 diabetes, triglycerides of 200 to 499 mg/dL, low HDL-C, and well-controlled LDL-C; nearly all were receiving statins. At four months, the placebo-adjusted between-group effects of pemafibrate were a 26.2 percent reduction in triglycerides, a 25.6 percent reduction in reported remnant cholesterol, and a 4.8 percent increase in apoB. Cardiovascular events were unchanged: hazard ratio 1.03, 95 percent confidence interval 0.91 to 1.15. ([Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

PROMINENT does not prove that remnant particles are harmless. It shows that reducing triglyceride and remnant-cholesterol mass when the total concentration of apoB-containing particles does not fall need not reduce events. The trial did not measure remnant-particle number specifically. Cholesterol mass, triglyceride mass, and particle number are related but not interchangeable treatment responses. PROMINENT was funded and sponsored by Kowa Research Institute, the pemafibrate manufacturer, and sponsor employees participated in the study; multiple academic authors also disclosed pharmaceutical relationships. The null result is nevertheless directly relevant to the proposed mechanism. ([Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645))

A 2025 meta-regression examined 43 statin, ezetimibe, and PCSK9-inhibitor trials and reached a favorable overall conclusion about remnant lowering, although its LDL-adjusted random-effects estimate for remnant cholesterol was not statistically significant. This was not a remnant-target trial: the interventions had multiple correlated effects, and calculated remnant cholesterol was reconstructed from trial averages. As the authors noted, the trial-level analysis does not prove that remnant-cholesterol lowering itself caused the observed event reduction. The senior author disclosed multiple pharmaceutical relationships. ([Nordestgaard and Nordestgaard, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf337))

## What icosapent ethyl proves, and what it does not

REDUCE-IT randomized 8,179 statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides to 4 g/day of icosapent ethyl or mineral-oil placebo. The primary composite cardiovascular endpoint occurred in 17.2 percent versus 22.0 percent, a hazard ratio of 0.75. This establishes clinical benefit for that drug in that selected population; it does not establish that remnant-cholesterol lowering mediated the benefit. In subgroup analyses, relative benefit was similar across baseline triglyceride categories and among participants above or below 150 mg/dL at one year; these baseline-subgroup and post-randomization analyses do not establish the mechanism of benefit. In a post hoc biomarker substudy, the mineral-oil group showed increases in several atherogenic and inflammatory markers; whether mineral oil caused those changes and how much they affected the event estimate remain disputed. REDUCE-IT was funded by Amarin Pharma, the manufacturer, and included company employees and investigators with financial relationships. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792); [Ridker et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.122.059410))

STRENGTH provides a useful contrast. In 13,078 high-risk, statin-treated patients, an EPA-DHA formulation lowered triglycerides but did not reduce major cardiovascular events compared with corn oil, with a hazard ratio of 0.99. AstraZeneca funded the trial and helped develop the protocol; company employees were coauthors, and academic authors disclosed industry relationships. Differences in formulation, achieved blood levels, placebo, and biological effects prevent a clean head-to-head inference, but the result reinforces that triglyceride lowering alone is not a validated explanation for event reduction. ([Nicholls et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258))

## Emerging and newly approved remnant-lowering drugs

RNA-targeted drugs against APOC3 or ANGPTL3 can produce large reductions in triglycerides and calculated remnant cholesterol. In exploratory analyses at week 24, the three quarterly plozasiran regimens produced placebo-adjusted reductions in calculated remnant cholesterol of 42.9 to 48.9 percentage points, while the tested zodasiran doses produced reductions of 73 to 82 percentage points; apoB and other lipoproteins also changed. Neither phase 2 trial was designed to determine whether cardiovascular events fall or whether any eventual benefit is mediated specifically by remnant cholesterol. Both trials were funded by Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals, which participated extensively in study design, conduct, data analysis and interpretation, manuscript preparation, and publication decisions; company employees were among the authors. ([Ballantyne et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2404143); [Rosenson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2404147))

The field has advanced since those phase 2 reports. Plozasiran, now marketed as Redemplo, was approved by the FDA in November 2025 as an adjunct to diet to reduce triglycerides in adults with familial chylomicronemia syndrome. In the 75-participant PALISADE trial of persistent chylomicronemia, acute pancreatitis occurred in 2 of 50 participants assigned to plozasiran and 5 of 25 assigned to placebo over 12 months, corresponding to an odds ratio of 0.17, 95 percent confidence interval 0.03 to 0.94. Olezarsen, marketed as Tryngolza, was approved for adults with familial chylomicronemia syndrome in December 2024, and its indication was expanded in June 2026 to reduce triglycerides and acute-pancreatitis risk in adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia. Across the CORE and CORE2 trials, which included 1,061 participants with severe hypertriglyceridemia, the pooled rate ratio for acute pancreatitis with olezarsen versus placebo was 0.15, 95 percent confidence interval 0.05 to 0.40. ([Watts et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409368); [Marston et al., 2026, CORE and CORE2](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2512761); [FDA, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-drug-reduce-triglycerides-adult-patients-familial-chylomicronemia-syndrome); [FDA, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-drug-reduce-triglycerides-adults-familial-chylomicronemia-syndrome); [FDA, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-treatment-shown-reduce-risk-acute-pancreatitis-adults-severe-hypertriglyceridemia))

In the 1,349-participant ESSENCE-TIMI 73b trial, olezarsen reduced triglycerides by 58.4 to 60.6 percentage points relative to placebo at six months and also lowered remnant cholesterol, non-HDL-C, and apoB, but the trial was not designed to test atherosclerotic cardiovascular events. A 12-month coronary-CTA substudy found no statistically significant difference in noncalcified plaque volume despite substantial reductions in triglycerides and remnant cholesterol. Zodasiran remains investigational. These developments establish substantial triglyceride lowering and, in very-high-triglyceride populations, evidence of reduced acute-pancreatitis risk. They do not establish prevention of atherosclerotic cardiovascular events or mediation specifically through remnant cholesterol. ([Bergmark et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2507227); [Marston et al., 2026, ESSENCE imaging](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080012); [ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT07037771](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07037771))

These agents may provide stronger tests of triglyceride-rich-lipoprotein biology, although their broad effects on apoB-containing lipoproteins will still make remnant-cholesterol-specific causality difficult to isolate. An adequately powered cardiovascular-outcome trial, paired with apoB and detailed lipoprotein measurements, is needed to determine whether these drugs reduce atherosclerotic cardiovascular events and to explore whether any benefit tracks changes in remnant cholesterol, particle number, or other drug effects. Even such analyses may not identify a unique mediator because these changes are tightly correlated. None of the cited intervention trials randomized patients to a remnant-cholesterol target or demonstrated a reduction in atherosclerotic cardiovascular events specifically attributable to selectively lowering remnant cholesterol independently of apoB, non-HDL-C, or LDL-C. ([Nordestgaard and Nordestgaard, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf337); [Bergmark et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2507227); [Marston et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080012); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))

## Diet and lifestyle

Dietary change, weight loss, physical activity, glycemic control, and reduced alcohol intake can lower triglycerides and alter postprandial triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, but remnant-cholesterol-specific outcome evidence is sparse. In a CORDIOPREV substudy of participants with type 2 diabetes, postprandial triglyceride and remnant-cholesterol responses improved from baseline in the Mediterranean-diet group, while no significant improvement was seen in the low-fat group. The main CORDIOPREV trial later reported fewer recurrent cardiovascular events with the Mediterranean diet, but it did not establish remnant-cholesterol lowering as the mediator. CORDIOPREV received Spanish public grants and support from olive-oil-related nonprofit foundations; participants were supplied with extra-virgin olive oil or comparator food packs. The substudy authors reported no conflicts of interest. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Gomez-Marin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqy144); [Delgado-Lista et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2822%2900122-2))

The practical inference is therefore conventional: lifestyle interventions can improve the metabolic setting that produces triglyceride-rich particles, but their health value should not be reduced to a single unvalidated remnant-cholesterol target. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Delgado-Lista et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2822%2900122-2))

## Quick reference

- **Calculation:** Total-C - HDL-C - LDL-C. If LDL-C is Friedewald-calculated, remnant-C is algebraically TG/5 in mg/dL, but this identity does not validate the assumed VLDL composition in an individual. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

- **What it represents:** Cholesterol mass assigned to triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, not their particle number. ApoB better approximates the total number of atherogenic particles. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551))

- **Suggested flags:** A 2016 European consensus proposed 30 mg/dL fasting and 35 mg/dL nonfasting as abnormal values. These are consensus flags, not randomized-trial-derived treatment targets, and values are method dependent. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

- **Specialized assessment:** Homogeneous assays, immunoseparation, ultracentrifugation, and NMR do not measure identical fractions. No universal reference method has been established, and the 2026 guideline does not set a remnant-cholesterol treatment goal. ([Chen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-020-01311-w); [Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))

- **Clinical target:** The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety guideline uses LDL-C and non-HDL-C goals and recommends selective apoB measurement to refine residual risk, especially with elevated triglycerides; it does not establish a remnant-cholesterol treatment goal. ([Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))


## Practical interpretation

- Calculating remnant cholesterol from a standard panel is reasonable as a descriptive aid, and it requires no additional blood test. When Friedewald LDL-C is used, the value is algebraically TG/5 and adds no information beyond that rescaling. Because measurement methods define different fractions, results should be interpreted according to the LDL-C method and assay used. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152); [Chen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-020-01311-w); [Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293))

- An elevated value can flag increased cholesterol mass assigned to triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and a phenotype associated with residual cardiovascular risk that may be less obvious from LDL-C alone. It should be interpreted alongside non-HDL-C, apoB when appropriate, triglycerides, the LDL method, and the person's overall cardiovascular risk. ([Quispe et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab432); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))

- Specialized assays can characterize different lipoprotein fractions in research, but the methods are not interchangeable and a consensus clinical method has not been established. The 2026 guideline bases clinical assessment on standard lipid measures and recommends selective apoB measurement; it does not establish a role for routine advanced remnant-cholesterol testing. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Chen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12944-020-01311-w); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))

- The strongest conclusion is that atherogenic triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and their remnants matter, not that measured remnant-cholesterol mass matters more than LDL-C or apoB. The best-supported position is that remnant cholesterol is biologically and epidemiologically important, but adopting it as an independent primary treatment target is premature. ([Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045); [Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [Blumenthal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423))
