# Is “Good Cholesterol” Actually Good?

HDL cholesterol is useful as a cardiovascular risk marker, but “good cholesterol” is too simple. Across many cohorts, lower HDL-C is generally associated with higher cardiovascular risk, yet randomized trials and Mendelian randomization studies do not support HDL-C concentration itself as a reliable causal treatment target. Raising the number pharmacologically has not reliably prevented cardiovascular events, while HDL-related functions remain biologically plausible but clinically unvalidated. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00392-024-02420-7); [Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400969)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

## What HDL-C measures

HDL-C is the amount of cholesterol contained within high-density lipoprotein particles in a blood sample. It is not a direct measurement of the number of HDL particles, the movement of cholesterol from tissues to the liver, or properties such as cholesterol efflux, antioxidant activity, or anti-inflammatory activity. ([von Eckardstein et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac605)) ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/16/1394/6807326 "https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/16/1394/6807326"))

HDL particles are heterogeneous: particles with similar cholesterol content can differ in size, protein composition, lipid composition, and biological activity. Two people with the same HDL-C concentration therefore need not have HDL particles with the same functional properties. ([von Eckardstein et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac605)) ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/16/1394/6807326 "https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/16/1394/6807326"))

This distinction matters because a laboratory concentration can be a useful risk marker without being the causal process that should be targeted with treatment. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619); [Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

## Why HDL was called “good cholesterol”

The observational association is real and strong. An individual-participant meta-analysis of 68 prospective studies included 302,430 people without known vascular disease at baseline and 12,785 coronary heart disease events. After adjustment for nonlipid risk factors and other lipid measures, each 15 mg/dL higher HDL-C concentration was associated with an approximately 22 percent lower risk of coronary heart disease. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

That result establishes prognostic association, not causation. A variable can identify people at higher or lower risk without being the mechanism that must be altered to change their outcomes. The HDL hypothesis therefore required a stronger test: whether interventions that selectively raise HDL-C reduce cardiovascular events. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619); [Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2); [D’Andrea et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.2224)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

## What happened when researchers raised HDL-C

### Torcetrapib: HDL rose and outcomes worsened

In the ILLUMINATE trial, 15,067 high-risk patients received atorvastatin plus either torcetrapib or placebo. Torcetrapib raised HDL-C by 72.1 percent and lowered LDL-C by 24.9 percent, but it increased major cardiovascular events by 25 percent and all-cause mortality by 58 percent. ([Barter et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0706628)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/"))

Torcetrapib also raised systolic blood pressure by about 5.4 mm Hg and produced changes consistent with increased mineralocorticoid activity. The trial therefore does not show that raising HDL itself caused harm. It documented torcetrapib-specific blood-pressure and mineralocorticoid changes, but it could not definitively determine whether the excess risk arose from those off-target effects, CETP inhibition, or another mechanism. It does show that a very large HDL-C increase could not compensate for the drug's harmful effects. ([Barter et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0706628)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** Pfizer funded the trial, manufactured torcetrapib, and had employees among the authors. The harmful result should not be dismissed, but the commercial involvement needs to be noted. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/"))

### Dalcetrapib: HDL rose and outcomes did not change

The dal-OUTCOMES trial randomized 15,871 patients after an acute coronary syndrome. Dalcetrapib raised HDL-C by approximately 31 to 40 percent while having little effect on LDL-C, but it did not reduce the primary cardiovascular outcome: hazard ratio 1.04, 95 percent confidence interval 0.93 to 1.16. The trial was stopped for futility. ([Schwartz et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1206797)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1206797 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1206797"))

A second outcomes trial, dal-GenE, randomized 6,147 patients with a recent acute coronary syndrome who had the prespecified _ADCY9_ AA genotype. The primary endpoint occurred in 9.5 percent of the dalcetrapib group and 10.6 percent of the placebo group: hazard ratio 0.88, 95 percent confidence interval 0.75 to 1.03, P = 0.12. Nominally fewer myocardial infarctions and a favorable prespecified but nonrandomized on-treatment analysis were observed, but the prespecified primary result was not statistically significant, so the findings were not confirmatory. ([Tardif et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac374)) ([European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/43/39/3947/6646961 "Pharmacogenetics-guided dalcetrapib therapy after an acute coronary syndrome: the dal-GenE trial"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** F. Hoffmann-La Roche funded dal-OUTCOMES, developed dalcetrapib, and had company employees among the authors. DalCor Pharmaceuticals funded dal-GenE, owned the underlying data, and employed several authors. ([Schwartz et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1206797); [Tardif et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac374)) ([European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/43/39/3947/6646961 "Pharmacogenetics-guided dalcetrapib therapy after an acute coronary syndrome: the dal-GenE trial"))

### Evacetrapib: a 133 percent HDL increase produced no benefit

In ACCELERATE, 12,092 patients with high-risk vascular disease received evacetrapib or placebo. Evacetrapib raised HDL-C by 133.2 percent and lowered LDL-C by 31.1 percent, but the primary cardiovascular outcome occurred in 12.9 percent of the evacetrapib group and 12.8 percent of the placebo group: hazard ratio 1.01, 95 percent confidence interval 0.91 to 1.11. The trial was stopped early for futility. ([Lincoff et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** Eli Lilly funded the trial and employed several authors. The reported result was neutral rather than favorable to the sponsor’s drug. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581"))

### Anacetrapib: a real benefit, but not clean evidence for HDL

REVEAL randomized 30,449 statin-treated patients with atherosclerotic vascular disease. Anacetrapib raised HDL-C by approximately 104 percent, but it also lowered non-HDL cholesterol by about 18 percent. Major coronary events occurred in 10.8 percent of the anacetrapib group and 11.8 percent of the placebo group, a rate ratio of 0.91. ([HPS3/TIMI55-REVEAL Collaborative Group, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444"))

Anacetrapib is therefore an important exception to the otherwise unsuccessful CETP-inhibitor trials. It does not, however, isolate the effect of raising HDL-C. Because the drug simultaneously lowered non-HDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B-containing particles, the trial cannot establish whether its HDL increase contributed to the benefit. Genetic evidence gives stronger causal support to apoB-containing lipoproteins than to HDL-C, making reduction of atherogenic particles the better-supported explanation, although REVEAL itself did not isolate that mechanism. ([Richardson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062); [HPS3/TIMI55-REVEAL Collaborative Group, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444)) ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062 "https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** Merck funded REVEAL and supplied the treatments. The University of Oxford’s Clinical Trial Service Unit independently designed and conducted the trial, analyzed the data, and retained control over publication, which reduces but does not eliminate the relevance of the commercial funding. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28847206/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28847206/"))

### The CETP class is still being studied

Obicetrapib is a newer CETP inhibitor that substantially changes both HDL-C and atherogenic lipoproteins. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of three trials involving 3,088 participants found that it raised HDL-C by approximately 126 percent while lowering LDL-C by 31.8 percent and apoB by 19.4 percent. The review primarily assessed lipid changes and short-term safety rather than a dedicated cardiovascular-outcomes endpoint. ([Kayani et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vasdi.2026.02.004)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41763910/ "Lipid-modifying efficacy and safety of obicetrapib in high-risk cardiovascular patients: A systematic review and meta-analysis - PubMed"))

In the 2026 BROOKLYN trial, obicetrapib raised HDL-C by 138.7 percent while lowering LDL-C by 36.3 percent and apoB by 24.4 percent in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. This is evidence that the drug changes lipoproteins, not that its HDL effect improves outcomes. ([Nicholls et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-04179-4)) ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-04179-4 "Obicetrapib in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia: the BROOKLYN randomized clinical trial | Nature Medicine"))

A pooled 365-day analysis of BROADWAY and BROOKLYN included 2,884 participants. A broad composite of coronary heart disease death, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, or coronary revascularization occurred in 3.9 percent with obicetrapib and 5.0 percent with placebo: hazard ratio 0.77, 95 percent confidence interval 0.54 to 1.11, P = 0.16. A narrower coronary composite occurred in 3.2 versus 4.7 percent: hazard ratio 0.68, 95 percent confidence interval 0.46 to 1.00, nominal P = 0.048. Because the broader endpoint was not statistically significant, the narrower result was borderline, and the analysis was not a dedicated, adequately powered outcomes trial, these findings are suggestive but nonconfirmatory. ([Nicholls et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.07.056)) ([JACC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0735109725073838 "Impact of Obicetrapib on Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events in High-Risk Patients: A Pooled Analysis"))

The dedicated PREVAIL cardiovascular-outcomes trial, which enrolled approximately 9,500 participants, was ongoing as of July 2026. Until a definitive outcomes result is available, obicetrapib’s cardiovascular-outcomes benefit remains unestablished, and any future benefit could not automatically be attributed to its HDL-C increase because the drug also substantially lowers atherogenic lipoproteins. ([PREVAIL trial, NCT05202509](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05202509)) ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05202509 "Obicetrapib and Cardiovascular Outcomes: PREVAIL"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** BROOKLYN and BROADWAY were sponsored by NewAmsterdam Pharma. Seven BROOKLYN authors were company employees and held shares or options, several academic authors reported consulting or research relationships with the company, and the pooled analysis also included company employees among its authors. ([Nicholls et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-04179-4); [Nicholls et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.07.056)) ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-04179-4 "Obicetrapib in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia: the BROOKLYN randomized clinical trial | Nature Medicine"))

## What the niacin trials showed

AIM-HIGH randomized 3,414 patients with established cardiovascular disease, low HDL-C, and elevated triglycerides who were already receiving intensive statin therapy. Extended-release niacin increased HDL-C by approximately 25 percent, lowered triglycerides by 29 percent, and lowered LDL-C by 12 percent, but did not reduce the primary cardiovascular outcome: hazard ratio 1.02, 95 percent confidence interval 0.87 to 1.21. The trial was stopped early for lack of benefit. ([AIM-HIGH Investigators, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1107579)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085343/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085343/"))

AIM-HIGH was led and primarily supported by the US National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, but Abbott provided additional funding and the niacin and placebo; Merck supplied simvastatin. The commercial contributors did not control the final analysis according to the trial report. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1107579 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1107579"))

HPS2-THRIVE randomized 25,673 high-risk, statin-treated patients to extended-release niacin plus laropiprant or placebo. Treatment raised HDL-C by approximately 6 mg/dL and lowered LDL-C by approximately 10 mg/dL, but did not significantly reduce major vascular events: 13.2 versus 13.7 percent, rate ratio 0.96, 95 percent confidence interval 0.90 to 1.03. It also increased several categories of serious adverse events, including disturbances in diabetes control, new diabetes, gastrointestinal events, infections, bleeding, and musculoskeletal events. Because the trial tested niacin together with laropiprant, not every adverse effect can be assigned to niacin alone. ([HPS2-THRIVE Collaborative Group, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1300955)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25014686/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25014686/"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** Merck funded HPS2-THRIVE and supplied the study drugs. The University of Oxford was the independent regulatory sponsor, and the study also received public and charitable support. ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1300955 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1300955"))

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 cardiovascular-outcomes trials involving 35,760 participants found no overall cardiovascular benefit from niacin in contemporary prevention. Apparent benefits were largely confined to older niacin-monotherapy trials conducted before statins became standard treatment. ([D’Andrea et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.2224)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30977858/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30977858/"))

That meta-analysis was supported by the Laura and John Arnold Foundation rather than a niacin manufacturer, providing an important independent synthesis of the commercially supported trials. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6481429/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6481429/"))

## What Mendelian randomization changed

Mendelian randomization uses genetic variants associated with an exposure to test whether lifelong differences in that exposure are associated with disease. It is not identical to a drug trial and depends on assumptions including valid genetic instruments and the absence of material horizontal pleiotropy, population stratification, and selection bias. When those assumptions are reasonably met, it can be less vulnerable to several forms of confounding that affect conventional observational studies. ([Davies et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k601)) ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/362/bmj.k601 "Reading Mendelian randomisation studies: a guide, glossary, and checklist for clinicians"))

In a major 2012 analysis, a variant in the _LIPG_ gene raised HDL-C by approximately 5.4 mg/dL. Based on the epidemiological association, that difference would have predicted a 13 percent reduction in myocardial infarction. The observed odds ratio was 0.99, indicating no detectable protection. A genetic score composed of 14 HDL-associated variants was also not significantly associated with myocardial infarction. ([Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/"))

A systematic review published online in 2024 and in final issue form in 2026 identified 21 Mendelian-randomization records with reported sample counts totaling up to 5.8 million across records. Although simple analyses could produce an inverse association between genetically predicted HDL-C and coronary disease, that association was highly heterogeneous and disappeared when correlated traits such as apoA-I or apoB were considered. ([Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00392-024-02420-7)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38407584/ "Causal association between lipoproteins and risk of coronary artery disease"))

Multivariable Mendelian randomization has similarly found that the causal signal among correlated lipid measures is concentrated in apoB, whereas apparent HDL-C and apoA-I associations attenuate when apoB is included in the model. Because highly correlated lipoprotein measures make exclusive causal interpretation challenging, this finding is best treated as convergent evidence rather than standalone proof. ([Richardson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062)) ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062 "https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062"))

These studies weaken the claim that HDL-C concentration is independently protective. They do not prove that every protein, particle subtype, or biological pathway involving HDL is irrelevant. ([Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2); [Richardson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00392-024-02420-7)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607825/"))

## HDL function: a biologically plausible but unproven hypothesis

Cholesterol efflux capacity measures, under laboratory conditions, how effectively a person’s HDL-containing serum accepts cholesterol from cells such as macrophages. It is an ex vivo assay rather than a direct measurement of cholesterol transport through the body. ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418)) ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418/full "Cholesterol Efflux Capacity and Its Association With Adverse Cardiovascular Events"))

A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of 20 studies involving 25,132 participants found that people in the highest cholesterol-efflux category had a 37 percent lower risk of adverse cardiovascular events than those in the lowest category in the crude categorical analysis. Each standard-deviation increase was associated with an approximately 20 percent lower risk, while the adjusted pooled association was smaller, at approximately hazard ratio 0.76. However, the included studies used different assays and endpoint definitions, between-study heterogeneity was high, and evidence of small-study effects or publication bias was reported. These findings support further research but do not establish cholesterol-efflux capacity as a standardized independent risk marker or treatment target. ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34966797/ "Cholesterol Efflux Capacity and Its Association With Adverse Cardiovascular Events"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** This positive meta-analysis was funded by CSL Behring, a company developing an apoA-I infusion intended to increase cholesterol efflux. Three authors were CSL Behring employees, while other authors reported commercial relationships. The paper states that the funder did not participate in the analysis or publication decision. Sponsorship and author ties remain relevant risk-of-bias considerations, especially given assay heterogeneity and the need for independent replication, but they do not by themselves determine whether the estimate is valid. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418/full "Frontiers | Cholesterol Efflux Capacity and Its Association With Adverse Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis"))

An independent analysis combining six prospective cohorts, 14,438 participants, and 1,570 incident coronary events did not find an unequivocal independent association between cholesterol efflux capacity and coronary heart disease after accounting for HDL-C and HDL subclasses. ([Kuusisto et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.13479)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35289444/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35289444/"))

The largest direct clinical test came from AEGIS-II. The trial randomized 18,219 patients shortly after myocardial infarction to receive CSL112, a plasma-derived apoA-I infusion that substantially increases cholesterol efflux capacity, or placebo. Cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke within 90 days occurred in 4.8 percent of the CSL112 group and 5.2 percent of the placebo group: hazard ratio 0.93, 95 percent confidence interval 0.81 to 1.05. There was also no significant benefit in the prespecified time-to-first-event analyses at 180 or 365 days. ([Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400969)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38587254/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38587254/"))

A prespecified exploratory analysis that counted recurrent as well as first events found nominally fewer total cardiovascular deaths, myocardial infarctions, and strokes at 180 days, rate ratio 0.87, 95 percent confidence interval 0.77 to 0.99, P = 0.04, and at 365 days, rate ratio 0.89, 95 percent confidence interval 0.80 to 0.99, P = 0.04. Because the primary endpoint was negative and these were secondary, multiplicity-sensitive analyses, the findings are hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. ([Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.08.001)) ([JACC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0735109724080380 "ApoA-I Infusions and Burden of Ischemic Events After Acute Myocardial Infarction: Insights From the AEGIS-II Trial"))

**Funding and relevant conflicts of interest:** AEGIS-II was funded by CSL Behring, and multiple authors were employees or had financial relationships with the company. The negative primary result was not favorable to the product, but sponsor involvement remains relevant to assessment of prespecification, analysis, and reporting. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38587254/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38587254/"))

Cholesterol efflux may still contain useful prognostic or biological information, but the evidence does not justify saying that it “matters more than HDL-C” as an established clinical fact. Assays are not standardized for routine use, observational findings are inconsistent, and an intervention that increased efflux failed to reduce its prespecified primary cardiovascular outcome, although exploratory recurrent-event analyses generated a signal. Other proposed HDL properties, including antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity, have even less standardized clinical testing. ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418); [Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400969); [Ruiz-Ramie et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000000568)) ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418/full "Frontiers | Cholesterol Efflux Capacity and Its Association With Adverse Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis"))

## Exercise and “naturally higher” HDL

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of 148 randomized exercise trials involving 8,673 participants found that exercise training increased HDL-C by an average of approximately 2.11 mg/dL. The effect was modest and varied considerably among studies. The prediction interval included both a decrease and a larger increase, so the pooled average should not be treated as an expected individual response. Risk-of-bias concerns were also common across the included trials. ([Smart et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z)) ([Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z "https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z"))

Studies examining exercise and HDL function have produced mixed results. Some report improvements in cholesterol efflux or other functional measurements, while others do not; the assays, exercise programs, populations, and study sizes differ substantially. ([Ruiz-Ramie et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000000568)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492243/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6492243/"))

The evidence therefore does not establish that an exercise-induced HDL increase mediates the health effects of exercise. “Naturally high” HDL is a heterogeneous category that may accompany a favorable physiological state, but that does not mean the HDL-C concentration itself is the protective agent. Exercise should not be judged as successful or unsuccessful according to whether a person’s HDL-C rises. ([Smart et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z); [Ruiz-Ramie et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000000568)) ([Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z "https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z"))

## Can HDL-C be too high?

The evidence is consistent with a nonlinear association in some populations, but there is no universally established danger threshold at 80 or 90 mg/dL. ([Mamede et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2024.06.002); [Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2022.0912)) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1933287424002034 "Association between very high HDL-C levels and mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis - ScienceDirect"))

A 2024 meta-analysis included eight observational studies, 1,004,584 people without coronary artery disease, and 133,646 people with HDL-C of at least 80 mg/dL. Compared with HDL-C of 40 to 60 mg/dL, very high HDL-C was not associated with significantly higher cardiovascular mortality overall: hazard ratio 1.05, 95 percent confidence interval 0.94 to 1.17. Very high HDL-C was associated with a lower risk of fatal and nonfatal coronary events, with a hazard ratio of 0.79. ([Mamede et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2024.06.002)) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1933287424002034 "Association between very high HDL-C levels and mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis - ScienceDirect"))

In dose-response analyses from the same meta-analysis, the modeled association with cardiovascular mortality increased at substantially higher concentrations - above approximately 94 mg/dL in men and 116 mg/dL in women. These model-derived subgroup estimates were close to the threshold of statistical significance and came from observational data, so they should not be treated as precise clinical cut-points. ([Mamede et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2024.06.002)) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1933287424002034 "Association between very high HDL-C levels and mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis - ScienceDirect"))

Results differ in people who already have coronary artery disease. A 2022 analysis of 14,478 UK Biobank participants and 5,467 Emory Cardiovascular Biobank participants with coronary disease found a U-shaped relationship. In the UK cohort, HDL-C above 80 mg/dL was associated with higher all-cause mortality, hazard ratio 1.96, and cardiovascular mortality, hazard ratio 1.71, compared with 40 to 60 mg/dL. A similar association with all-cause mortality was observed in the Emory cohort; its cardiovascular-mortality estimate was directionally consistent but less precise and did not reach conventional statistical significance. ([Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2022.0912)) ([JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2792282 "Association Between High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol Levels and Adverse Cardiovascular Outcomes in High-risk Populations | Dyslipidemia | JAMA Cardiology | JAMA Network"))

These studies show that very high HDL-C should not automatically be interpreted as extra protection. They do not establish that high HDL itself is toxic or that “dysfunctional HDL” caused the excess risk. HDL function was not directly tested as the causal mechanism, and observational associations remain vulnerable to residual confounding and reverse causation. ([Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2022.0912); [Mamede et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2024.06.002)) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1933287424002034 "Association between very high HDL-C levels and mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis - ScienceDirect"))

## Should HDL-C be removed from risk calculation?

The drug-trial and genetic evidence supports the conclusion that HDL-C should not be treated as an isolated therapeutic target. It does not prove that HDL-C has no value in risk prediction. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619); [Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2); [D’Andrea et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.2224)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

Prediction and causation answer different questions. HDL-C can help identify people at higher observed risk even when raising HDL-C does not change that risk. The prospective epidemiological association remains strong, while the intervention and genetic evidence shows that manipulating the HDL-C number is not equivalent to correcting the underlying risk state. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619); [Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

Any proposal to remove HDL-C from a prediction model should be tested directly for its effects on discrimination, calibration, and, where relevant, clinical usefulness. It cannot be inferred merely from the failure of HDL-raising drugs. ([Steyerberg et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181c30fb2)) ([Epidemiology](https://journals.lww.com/epidem/fulltext/2010/01000/assessing_the_performance_of_prediction_models__a.22.aspx "Assessing the performance of prediction models: a framework for traditional and novel measures"))

## Quick reference

- **Observational association:** Each 15 mg/dL higher HDL-C was associated with approximately 22 percent lower coronary risk in a large prospective meta-analysis. Association does not establish causation. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

- **Torcetrapib:** HDL-C +72 percent; cardiovascular events and mortality increased, alongside higher blood pressure and mineralocorticoid effects. ([Barter et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0706628)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17984165/"))

- **Dalcetrapib:** HDL-C +31 to 40 percent in dal-OUTCOMES, with no cardiovascular benefit. In genotype-selected dal-GenE, the point estimate favored dalcetrapib but the prespecified primary endpoint was not statistically significant. ([Schwartz et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1206797); [Tardif et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac374)) ([European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/43/39/3947/6646961 "Pharmacogenetics-guided dalcetrapib therapy after an acute coronary syndrome: the dal-GenE trial"))

- **Evacetrapib:** HDL-C +133 percent and LDL-C -31 percent; no cardiovascular benefit. ([Lincoff et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1609581"))

- **Anacetrapib:** HDL-C +104 percent and non-HDL cholesterol -18 percent; major coronary events reduced by 9 percent, but the trial cannot separate the HDL effect from reduction of atherogenic particles. ([HPS3/TIMI55-REVEAL Collaborative Group, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444"))

- **Obicetrapib:** HDL-C increased substantially while LDL-C, apoB, non-HDL cholesterol, and lipoprotein(a) fell. Pooled one-year event findings were suggestive but nonconfirmatory, and the dedicated PREVAIL outcomes trial was ongoing as of July 2026. ([Nicholls et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.07.056); [PREVAIL trial, NCT05202509](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05202509)) ([JACC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0735109725073838 "Impact of Obicetrapib on Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events in High-Risk Patients: A Pooled Analysis"))

- **Niacin:** AIM-HIGH and HPS2-THRIVE raised HDL-C but did not reduce events on top of statin therapy. ([AIM-HIGH Investigators, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1107579); [HPS2-THRIVE Collaborative Group, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1300955)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085343/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085343/"))

- **Genetics:** Mendelian-randomization studies do not support a simple, independent causal effect of higher HDL-C. ([Voight et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2812%2960312-2); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00392-024-02420-7)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3419820/ "Plasma HDL cholesterol and risk of myocardial infarction - PMC"))

- **HDL function:** Higher cholesterol efflux is associated with lower risk in some observational studies, but estimates are heterogeneous, assays are not standardized, and CSL112 did not reduce the prespecified primary endpoint in AEGIS-II. Exploratory recurrent-event findings were suggestive but nonconfirmatory. ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418); [Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400969); [Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.08.001)) ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2021.774418/full "Cholesterol Efflux Capacity and Its Association With Adverse Cardiovascular Events"))

- **Very high HDL-C:** There is no universal danger threshold at 80 to 90 mg/dL; associations vary by sex, concentration, and whether coronary disease is already present. ([Mamede et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2024.06.002); [Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2022.0912)) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1933287424002034 "Association between very high HDL-C levels and mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis - ScienceDirect"))


## Practical interpretation

- HDL-C is best understood as a **risk marker, not an isolated treatment target**. A low value may identify higher observed risk, but the evidence does not support prescribing treatment simply to raise the HDL-C number. ([Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2009.1619); [D’Andrea et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.2224); [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00392-024-02420-7)) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/ "https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284229/"))

- A high HDL-C concentration should not be interpreted as offsetting the risk associated with elevated LDL-C, non-HDL cholesterol, apoB, or established cardiovascular disease. The anacetrapib and genetic evidence gives stronger support to reducing apoB-containing particles than to increasing HDL-C. ([HPS3/TIMI55-REVEAL Collaborative Group, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444); [Richardson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003062)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444 "https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706444"))

- Exercise can produce a small average increase in HDL-C, but its value should not be assessed according to the HDL response. Current studies do not establish that the HDL change mediates the effects of exercise. ([Smart et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z); [Ruiz-Ramie et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOL.0000000000000568)) ([Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z "https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02115-z"))

- HDL function is not ready to replace HDL-C in routine care. Cholesterol-efflux testing lacks standardization, has inconsistent independent prognostic evidence, and has not reduced a prespecified primary cardiovascular outcome in an outcomes trial, although exploratory recurrent-event findings remain hypothesis-generating. ([Kuusisto et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.13479); [Gibson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400969)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35289444/ "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35289444/"))

- Unexpectedly very high HDL-C should not automatically be labeled beneficial or harmful. It should be interpreted alongside the person’s overall lipid profile, cardiovascular history, and other risk factors rather than through a universal cutoff. ([Mamede et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2024.06.002); [Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamacardio.2022.0912)) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1933287424002034 "Association between very high HDL-C levels and mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis - ScienceDirect"))
