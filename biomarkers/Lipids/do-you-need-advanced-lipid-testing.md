# Do You Need Advanced Lipid Testing?

Advanced lipid testing is not a single test. ApoB, lipoprotein(a), LDL particle number, particle size, remnant cholesterol, and oxidized LDL answer different questions and have different levels of supporting evidence. [2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [Sehayek et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [Morze et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207) [Byrne et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2026.03.026) [Gao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29173602/)

The strongest case is not for ordering a comprehensive advanced panel in everyone. It is for measuring lipoprotein(a), or Lp(a), at least once in adulthood and using ApoB when the standard lipid panel may misrepresent atherogenic particle burden or when the result could change treatment. Evidence for routine LDL particle sizing or broad NMR fractionation is weaker after ApoB and Lp(a) are considered, and oxidized LDL lacks established test-guided outcome evidence. [2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [Morze et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207) [Gao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29173602/)

## What the standard lipid panel measures

LDL cholesterol, or LDL-C, measures the amount of cholesterol carried inside LDL particles. Non-HDL cholesterol measures the cholesterol carried inside all non-HDL particles. These are cholesterol-mass measurements, not direct measures of how many atherogenic particles are circulating. [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303)

ApoB reflects the concentration of atherogenic particles because the major atherogenic lipoproteins each contain an ApoB molecule. Two people can therefore have the same LDL-C but different ApoB concentrations if their particles carry different amounts of cholesterol. When LDL-C and ApoB are discordant, risk generally tracks more closely with ApoB. [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303) [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024)

Lp(a) is a separate ApoB-containing particle. Its risk is not fully captured by total ApoB particle burden, which is why ApoB and Lp(a) provide complementary rather than interchangeable information. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)

## ApoB has the strongest case among particle tests

A 2025 systematic review identified 15 discordance studies involving 593,354 participants. ApoB outperformed LDL-C in all nine studies that compared them. ApoB outperformed non-HDL-C in seven of nine studies, was equivalent in one, and was inferior in one. LDL particle number outperformed LDL-C in two of three comparisons, but the evidence base was smaller. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024)

A large UK Biobank analysis illustrates why high correlations between lipid markers do not make them clinically interchangeable. Among 293,876 adults followed for approximately 11 years, ApoB was highly correlated with LDL-C, but the range of ApoB values at any given LDL-C was wide. At an LDL-C of 130 mg/dL, approximately 95 percent of corresponding ApoB values ranged from 85.8 to 108.8 mg/dL. Among people with LDL-C near that level, 10-year ASCVD incidence was 7.3 percent when ApoB was at least one standard deviation above its expected value and 4.0 percent when it was at least one standard deviation below it. Residual ApoB predicted events after LDL-C, non-HDL-C, or triglycerides were considered, whereas the residual cholesterol measures did not predict events after ApoB was included. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258)

Discordance is particularly common in people with metabolic risk, but it is not confined to them. In a nationally representative study of 12,688 US adults, higher-than-expected ApoB was more common with diabetes, obesity, elevated triglycerides, and other metabolic abnormalities. However, substantial ApoB variation was also present among metabolically healthy adults, meaning that a strategy limited exclusively to obvious metabolic syndrome will miss some discordant cases. Because this analysis was cross-sectional and did not include cardiovascular outcomes, it demonstrates that discordance exists outside obvious metabolic risk but does not establish that broader ApoB testing improves outcomes. [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073)

Together with prospective discordance studies and current guidance, these findings support considering ApoB when diabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome, or hypertriglyceridemia raises concern that LDL-C may understate particle burden, and after treatment when achieved LDL-C is below 70 mg/dL but residual lipoprotein-related risk is suspected. They also leave room for broader testing when cost and access are not barriers, because metabolic appearance alone does not identify every discordant patient. [2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258)

## How often does ApoB change formal risk classification?

Discordance studies provide stronger evidence that ApoB predicts risk more accurately than LDL-C than current studies provide that adding ApoB routinely moves patients across clinically meaningful treatment thresholds. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

A 2026 analysis pooled 10,519 adults from CARDIA, the Framingham Offspring Study, and MESA. Adding ApoB to PREVENT-estimated risk produced a continuous net reclassification improvement of 0.28 overall. The effect was concentrated in adults aged 18 to 39, in whom the continuous net reclassification improvement was 0.67 and categorical reclassification also improved. Reclassification was not statistically significant among adults aged 40 or older. [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

Importantly, ApoB did not improve overall model discrimination or mean calibration in that study. Net reclassification measures can therefore look favorable without showing that a model is substantially better at separating future cases from noncases. The result supports a potential role for ApoB, particularly in estimating long-term risk in younger adults, but it does not establish that everyone will receive a different treatment recommendation. [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

The practical value of ApoB may consequently be greatest when it identifies an individual whose particle burden is materially higher or lower than LDL-C suggests. That is a narrower claim than saying ApoB dramatically improves population-level discrimination. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258) [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

## When ApoB is most likely to be useful

The 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline states that ApoB testing can be useful to improve risk assessment and guide therapy once LDL-C and non-HDL-C goals have been met, particularly when triglycerides are elevated, especially above 200 mg/dL, diabetes is present, or achieved LDL-C is below 70 mg/dL. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [Published correction](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001457)

ApoB is most likely to influence a decision when:

- LDL-C is normal or modest but triglycerides, diabetes, obesity, or metabolic syndrome raise concern for cholesterol-depleted particles and a higher-than-expected particle count. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073)
- LDL-C is low during treatment but residual particle burden may support intensification. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)
- LDL-C and non-HDL-C give different impressions of treatment adequacy. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258)
- The decision to initiate or intensify treatment remains uncertain after conventional risk assessment, and the result would be used to refine rather than replace that assessment. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)
- Premature ASCVD or a strong family history is difficult to reconcile with an apparently reassuring standard panel, prompting evaluation for Lp(a) or a specific lipid or lipoprotein disorder. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac361)

A low ApoB in a person with high LDL-C indicates relatively fewer, more cholesterol-rich particles than LDL-C alone suggests. However, it does not automatically mean that the patient is being overtreated. Severe LDL-C elevation, familial hypercholesterolemia, existing ASCVD, coronary calcium, family history, and cumulative lifetime exposure still matter. ApoB should refine the interpretation of LDL-C, not serve as a veto against otherwise well-supported treatment. [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258) [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)

## Lp(a) is a separate test to measure at least once

The case for broadly measuring Lp(a) is different from the case for routinely ordering a full advanced panel. The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline recommends measuring Lp(a) at least once. It treats values of at least 125 nmol/L or 50 mg/dL as a risk-enhancing factor and estimates that values of at least 250 nmol/L or 100 mg/dL are associated with approximately twice the ASCVD risk seen at the population median. The mg/dL and nmol/L values are parallel guideline thresholds, not exact unit conversions. An elevated result is used to support more intensive LDL-C lowering and management of other modifiable risk factors. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac361)

Reclassification evidence for Lp(a) has been mixed. In the Bruneck cohort of 826 adults followed for 15 years, adding Lp(a) modestly increased the C-index and produced a 39.6 percent net reclassification improvement among participants initially classified as intermediate risk. The study used Framingham and Reynolds risk models and therefore did not test contemporary PREVENT-based 10-year reclassification. [Journal of the American College of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2014.03.061) [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

In the newer pooled-cohort analysis using PREVENT, Lp(a) did not significantly improve overall reclassification, whether treated as a continuous measure or divided at 50 mg/dL. Dichotomized Lp(a) improved reclassification only within MESA, not consistently across all three cohorts. [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

Thus, Lp(a) is a well-supported risk marker even though it will not move every patient into a different formal risk category. Its main value is identifying an inherited source of risk that the standard panel and ApoB alone may not reveal. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac361) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)

## LDL particle number and NMR testing

NMR LDL particle number, or LDL-P, measures the concentration of LDL particles. It addresses a similar question to ApoB, although ApoB counts a broader set of atherogenic particles rather than LDL alone. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf554)

LDL-P can outperform LDL-C when the two are discordant. However, newer evidence suggests that ApoB is at least as useful and may be more accurate. In a 2025 UK Biobank study of 41,099 participants, risk rose when ApoB was higher than expected relative to LDL-P, while discordantly high LDL-P was not independently associated with greater events. The authors concluded that ApoB more accurately identified cardiovascular risk when the two particle measures disagreed. [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf554)

A separate 2025 UK Biobank analysis of 207,368 adults evaluated total particle count, VLDL and LDL particle types, subclasses, and particle diameter. Total ApoB-containing particle count predicted coronary disease. Average VLDL and LDL particle diameter, IDL count, and LDL size subclasses were not associated with disease after particle count was considered. Small and very small VLDL subclasses retained associations in some adjusted analyses, particularly at lower ApoB-containing particle counts, but adding VLDL particle distribution did not improve prediction beyond total particle count. Lp(a) added independent information. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)

These findings argue against routinely paying for LDL size, "Pattern A versus Pattern B," or extensive NMR fractionation when ApoB and Lp(a) are already available. They do not address every specialist use in unusual lipid disorders, but they do not establish routine decision value for a detailed subclass report beyond total atherogenic particle burden. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)

## Remnant cholesterol

Remnant cholesterol is often calculated as total cholesterol minus HDL-C minus LDL-C, which is equivalent to non-HDL-C minus LDL-C. It can therefore usually be estimated from an ordinary lipid panel without ordering a separate advanced assay. When LDL-C is calculated with the Friedewald equation, calculated remnant cholesterol reduces algebraically to triglycerides divided by five in mg/dL, so it is not an analytically independent measurement. [Friedewald et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4337382/) [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401)

In a primary-prevention cohort of 17,532 adults, higher calculated remnant cholesterol predicted ASCVD after adjustment for LDL-C and ApoB. The adjusted hazard ratio was 1.65 for log-transformed remnant cholesterol, and people with high remnant cholesterol but low LDL-C had higher risk than concordant participants. The investigators nevertheless concluded that the mechanism and value of specifically targeting remnant cholesterol required further study. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab432)

A 2026 systematic review and trial-level meta-regression examined 43 randomized trials containing at least 1,000 participants and at least two years of planned follow-up. Reductions in remnant cholesterol were not associated with fewer deaths, myocardial infarctions, or strokes, while reductions in LDL-C and non-HDL-C were associated with benefit. Because this was not a randomized comparison of a remnant-specific treatment target, it cannot establish individual-level causality. The analysis does not prove that remnants are biologically unimportant, but it does not validate remnant cholesterol as a separate treatment target. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2026.03.026)

Remnant cholesterol is therefore useful as a description of triglyceride-rich dyslipidemia, particularly when triglycerides are elevated. Current evidence does not establish a need to purchase an additional remnant assay or treat to an independent remnant-cholesterol goal. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab432) [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2026.03.026)

## Oxidized LDL

A 2017 meta-analysis of 12 observational studies found that higher circulating oxidized LDL was associated with clinical ASCVD events, with a pooled relative effect of approximately 1.79. The authors emphasized the need for better prospective cohorts and intervention studies. [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015)

Because the included studies were observational, the meta-analysis did not establish a treatment threshold, demonstrate that lowering a measured oxidized LDL value prevents events, or show that management guided by the assay is superior to management based on established risk factors and ApoB-containing particle burden. [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015)

Oxidized LDL is biologically interesting and may be useful in research. The cited evidence does not support it as a routine clinical test. [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015)

## Do advanced-test-guided decisions improve outcomes?

The cited evidence leaves a major testing-strategy gap: prognostic association and treatment-response analyses do not directly show that assigning patients to advanced-test-guided care improves outcomes compared with well-executed standard care. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319871733) [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303)

A systematic review of 29 randomized lipid-lowering trials containing 332,912 participants used trial-level meta-regression and found that every 10 mg/dL reduction in ApoB was associated with a relative risk of 0.93 for major cardiovascular events. However, benefit depended on the treatment mechanism: statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9-directed therapies that increase LDL-receptor clearance showed benefit, whereas therapies that reduced ApoB through other mechanisms did not show the same relationship. [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319871733)

This supports ApoB as a marker of the effect of effective lipid-lowering therapies. It does not answer the separate question of whether assigning patients to ApoB-guided care produces better outcomes than assigning them to LDL-C-guided care. [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319871733)

A related but distinct treatment question has now been tested. On September 4, 2026, Novartis reported topline results from the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Lp(a)HORIZON trial in 8,323 patients with established cardiovascular disease and elevated Lp(a). Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a), but the trial did not meet its primary endpoint of reducing a composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization in the overall study population. Only topline results were available in the announcement, and the company said full results would be presented at an upcoming medical congress. This does not invalidate Lp(a) as a risk marker or answer whether Lp(a) testing improves care, but it shows that lowering Lp(a) with one agent in one secondary-prevention population did not reduce the announced primary endpoint. [Novartis](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-announces-lpahorizon-phase-iii-topline-results-pelacarsen-patients-elevated-lpa-and-established-cardiovascular-disease-cvd) [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)

The key missing trial would randomize comparable patients to treatment guided by:

1. A standard lipid panel and conventional risk assessment, or
2. ApoB, Lp(a), or a broader advanced panel,

and then measure cardiovascular events, adverse effects, medication use, and costs. The evidence reviewed here instead comes from observational discordance studies, secondary analyses and meta-regressions of treatment trials, guidelines, and economic simulations, not from a randomized comparison of ApoB-guided versus LDL-C-guided care. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319871733) [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303) [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)

## Cost-effectiveness

A 2026 computer simulation modeled 250,000 statin-eligible US adults without established ASCVD. Compared with a non-HDL-C treatment goal, an ApoB goal was projected to produce 1,324 additional quality-adjusted life-years at an incremental cost of $40.2 million, or approximately $30,300 per quality-adjusted life-year gained. The model selected ApoB as the preferred strategy in 65 percent of probabilistic analyses. [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303)

The uncertainty was substantial: the uncertainty intervals for both incremental quality-adjusted life-years and costs crossed zero. This makes the result supportive rather than definitive. It shows that ApoB-guided care can be economically plausible under the study's assumptions, not that its cost-effectiveness has been demonstrated in a clinical trial. The study was supported by an American Heart Association career-development award; reported outside relationships included consultancies with 3M and Boehringer Ingelheim. [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303)

A 2025 Lp(a) microsimulation modeled 10,000 adults aged 40 to 69 without cardiovascular disease. An elevated Lp(a) result triggered statin initiation plus blood-pressure treatment. Testing changed treatment in 1,807 people and was estimated to be cost-effective in Australia and cost-saving in the United Kingdom and the other countries included in the adaptation analysis. [Atherosclerosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2025.120447)

That result depends on the assumption that clinicians and patients act on an elevated result by intensifying proven risk-factor treatment. It is therefore a model of an entire screening-and-treatment pathway, not simply the price or predictive value of the Lp(a) assay. Several authors reported financial relationships with companies developing lipid- and Lp(a)-directed therapies, including Novartis, Amgen, Silence Therapeutics, Roche, Ionis, and others; the favorable estimates should be interpreted with that context in mind. [Atherosclerosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2025.120447)

Neither economic analysis evaluated comprehensive panels containing particle size, subclass fractionation, and oxidized LDL. Their favorable estimates therefore should not be extrapolated to an expensive bundled panel. [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303) [Atherosclerosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2025.120447)

## Funding and conflict-of-interest context

The evidence favoring ApoB is not solely based on industry-funded research. The large 2024 UK Biobank discordance study was supported by an unconditional grant from the nonprofit Doggone Foundation, although several authors reported pharmaceutical advisory, speaking, consulting, or research relationships. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258)

The 2024 NHANES ApoB-variation study reported that one investigator had received grants from Amgen and Esperion Therapeutics and outside personal fees; the other disclosed relationships were largely public or academic funding. [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073)

The 2025 NMR particle analysis was funded through Swedish public and nonprofit research programs. Two senior investigators reported extensive grants or consulting relationships with pharmaceutical and diagnostics companies. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)

These disclosures do not by themselves determine whether the findings are valid. The replicated discordance result deserves greater weight because it appears across multiple cohorts and analyses, whereas a recommendation that ApoB should completely replace conventional measures remains an interpretation rather than a randomized test-strategy result. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847401) [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303)

## Evaluating the competing claims

### 1. Advanced testing should be routine for everyone

**Too broad if "advanced testing" means a complete panel.**

An Lp(a) measurement at least once in adulthood is now recommended broadly. Routine ApoB testing for all adults remains debatable: discordance exists outside obvious high-risk groups, and ApoB testing costs were a small component of one economic model, but current US guidance recommends selective rather than universal use. The evidence does not support universal LDL particle sizing, NMR subclass analysis, or oxidized LDL testing. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073) [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207) [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015)

### 2. Testing is most useful in selected populations and borderline decisions

**Best supported overall.**

ApoB is most likely to change interpretation in diabetes, elevated triglycerides, low treated LDL-C, and other settings with suspected discordance, including obesity or metabolic syndrome. When premature ASCVD or a strong family history is difficult to reconcile with the standard panel, Lp(a) measurement and evaluation for a specific lipid disorder may be at least as relevant. The exception to selective use is Lp(a), for which at least one measurement is recommended even without these features. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073) [European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac361)

### 3. ApoB captures most of the additional value

**Largely correct for particle testing, but incomplete without Lp(a).**

ApoB generally provides more useful particle information than LDL-P, particle size, or detailed subclass fractionation. However, Lp(a) carries additional risk not fully represented by total ApoB. The most defensible streamlined approach is therefore ApoB plus a separate Lp(a) measurement, not ApoB alone. [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf554) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)

### 4. Advanced testing is being oversold

**Correct for some tests, too dismissive for others.**

Broad panels can create impressive-looking reports without demonstrating that each reported fraction changes treatment or improves outcomes. That criticism applies particularly to particle-size labels, extensive NMR subclass reports, and oxidized LDL. It does not justify dismissing ApoB in discordant patients or Lp(a) as an inherited risk enhancer. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207) [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015) [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)

## Quick reference

- **Lp(a):** Measure at least once in adulthood. It is particularly relevant with premature ASCVD, a family history of premature ASCVD or high Lp(a), or otherwise unexplained risk. A result of at least 125 nmol/L or 50 mg/dL is a risk-enhancing factor under the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac361)
- **ApoB:** Most useful when LDL-C may not reflect particle burden, especially with diabetes, elevated triglycerides, low treated LDL-C, or another reason to suspect discordance. Obesity and metabolic syndrome increase the probability of discordantly high ApoB but do not identify every case. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [JAMA Cardiology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2820073)
- **LDL-P:** Can identify discordance but usually offers little beyond ApoB and may be less predictive when the two disagree. [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf554)
- **LDL particle size and subclasses:** Associated with metabolic phenotype, but do not consistently improve risk prediction after total particle count is considered. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207)
- **Remnant cholesterol:** Usually calculable from the standard panel. It is predictive observationally, but is not established as an independent treatment target. [Friedewald et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4337382/) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab432) [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2026.03.026)
- **Oxidized LDL:** Observationally associated with ASCVD, without established test-guided outcome benefit. [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015)

## Practical interpretation

- A standard lipid panel remains the starting point when the patient's overall risk, LDL-C, non-HDL-C, triglycerides, and treatment decision all point in the same direction. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423)
- Lp(a) is the strongest candidate for broad testing at least once because it can reveal inherited risk that is otherwise not captured by the standard panel. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac361)
- ApoB is the most useful additional particle test when cholesterol mass and particle burden may be discordant. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024)
- A discordant ApoB result may support changing the intensity of treatment, but it must be interpreted alongside absolute LDL-C, family history, existing ASCVD, coronary calcium, age, and lifetime exposure. [2026 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423) [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258)
- ApoB and Lp(a) account for most of the actionable value of advanced particle testing. Detailed particle size and NMR fractionation usually add little predictive information after total particle count is considered, while oxidized LDL lacks a validated test-guided treatment pathway. [European Heart Journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf207) [Canadian Journal of Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2017.07.015)
- The central limitation is not whether these biomarkers predict events. Many do. It is whether measuring them and acting on them improves outcomes compared with well-executed standard care. The cited literature does not include a definitive randomized comparison of an advanced-test-guided strategy with standard lipid-guided care. [Journal of Clinical Lipidology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2025.05.024) [European Journal of Preventive Cardiology](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319871733) [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847303)
