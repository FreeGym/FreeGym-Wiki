# What Do Triglycerides Actually Tell You?

Triglycerides are a major circulating lipid, but a routine triglyceride result is easy to overinterpret. It reports the concentration of triglyceride carried across circulating lipoproteins. It does not directly count atherogenic particles, measure remnant cholesterol, diagnose insulin resistance, or tell you LDL particle size. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

The most accurate interpretation is that triglycerides provide information about triglyceride-rich lipoprotein traffic, metabolic health, and, at very high concentrations, pancreatitis risk. Their relationship with cardiovascular disease is real, but the triglyceride molecule itself may not be the main atherogenic agent. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

## What the triglyceride number measures

Triglycerides circulate mainly inside triglyceride-rich lipoproteins. These include liver-derived very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL), intestine-derived chylomicrons, and the remnant particles produced as these lipoproteins are metabolized. In fasting blood, VLDL usually contributes most of the measured triglyceride. After meals, chylomicrons and their remnants add to the value. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

A high triglyceride result can reflect more VLDL particles, larger triglyceride-loaded particles, slower clearance of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, or some combination of these. Because triglyceride-rich lipoproteins vary in triglyceride content, triglyceride concentration is not itself a particle count and should not be assumed to mean that VLDL particles are both large and numerous. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

As lipoprotein lipase removes triglyceride from VLDL and chylomicrons, the particles shrink and become relatively enriched in cholesterol. These partially metabolized particles are commonly called remnants. Each liver-derived VLDL particle and each of its remnants contains one apoB100 molecule, while each intestine-derived chylomicron and each of its remnants contains one apoB48 molecule. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

## Why elevated triglycerides often signal metabolic dysfunction

Elevated triglycerides commonly travel with insulin resistance, obesity or type 2 diabetes, low HDL-C, and a greater proportion of small dense LDL particles. This cluster is often called atherogenic dyslipidemia. Insulin resistance can increase the delivery of fatty acids to the liver, increase hepatic VLDL production, and impair the clearance of triglyceride-rich particles. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

High triglycerides can therefore be a useful window into metabolic health. However, they are not specific to insulin resistance. Alcohol intake, uncontrolled diabetes, hypothyroidism, kidney or liver disease, pregnancy, medications, sepsis or severe physiological stress, and inherited lipid disorders can also raise triglycerides. A high result should be interpreted as a signal to look for the underlying pattern, not as a diagnosis by itself. ([Berglund et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2011-3213)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3431581/))

## The cardiovascular question: marker, cause, or both?

The best-supported interpretation is that the triglyceride-rich lipoprotein pathway is causal, while the routine triglyceride concentration is an imperfect marker of that pathway. ([Varbo et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.08.1026); [Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045); [Björnson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehad337))

In a Mendelian randomization study of 73,513 people, a genetically predicted 1 mmol/L higher non-fasting remnant cholesterol concentration was associated with an estimated 2.8-fold higher risk of ischemic heart disease. This supported a causal contribution from remnant lipoproteins, although the authors noted that pleiotropic effects of the genetic variants could not be completely excluded. ([Varbo et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.08.1026)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265341/))

A later genetic analysis involving 654,783 participants compared triglyceride-lowering variants in the lipoprotein lipase pathway with LDL-C-lowering variants in the LDL receptor pathway. When both were scaled to the same reduction in apoB, their associations with coronary heart disease were almost identical. This suggests that the reduction in apoB-containing particle number may be more important than whether the particles primarily carry triglyceride or cholesterol. ([Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6439767/))

Another multivariable Mendelian randomization analysis reported that triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and remnants might be more atherogenic per particle than LDL. Differences between this result and the earlier analysis depended partly on the genetic variants selected and whether exposure was modeled as plasma triglycerides or as triglyceride-rich lipoprotein/remnant cholesterol. The evidence therefore supports a causal role for triglyceride-rich particles, but the exact relative atherogenicity of a remnant particle compared with an LDL particle remains unsettled. ([Björnson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehad337)) ([European Heart Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/39/4186/7207673))

The likely atherogenic exposure is the circulating apoB-containing particle and the cholesterol and other material it delivers to the arterial wall, potentially combined with inflammatory products released during triglyceride hydrolysis. Triglyceride mass is useful because it tracks this pathway, but the triglyceride molecule should not automatically be treated as the causal unit. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045))

## A major limitation from randomized treatment trials

A 2026 systematic review and trial-level meta-regression examined 43 large randomized trials with at least 1,000 participants and planned follow-up of at least two years. Reductions in calculated remnant cholesterol were not independently associated with reductions in myocardial infarction, stroke, or all-cause mortality. Reductions in LDL-C and non-HDL-C were associated with clinical benefit. ([Byrne et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2026.03.026)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42031588/))

This does not prove that remnant particles are harmless. The analysis compared diverse drugs at the trial level, relied largely on calculated rather than directly measured remnant cholesterol, and cannot reproduce the effects of lifelong genetic exposure. It does show that calculated remnant cholesterol is not yet a validated stand-alone treatment target and that lowering a triglyceride-related laboratory value does not automatically lower cardiovascular risk. ([Byrne et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2026.03.026)) ([University of Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/the-association-between-remnant-cholesterol-and-cardiovascular-ev/))

## What triglyceride-lowering drug trials show

### REDUCE-IT

REDUCE-IT randomized 8,179 statin-treated patients with established cardiovascular disease or diabetes plus additional risk factors, triglycerides of 135 to 499 mg/dL, and LDL-C of 41 to 100 mg/dL. Participants received icosapent ethyl, a purified EPA product, at 4 g per day or a mineral-oil placebo. Over a median of 4.9 years, the primary cardiovascular endpoint occurred in 17.2 percent of the icosapent ethyl group and 22.0 percent of the placebo group, corresponding to a hazard ratio of 0.75. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792))

Hospitalization for atrial fibrillation or flutter was more frequent with icosapent ethyl, at 3.1 percent versus 2.1 percent. Serious bleeding occurred in 2.7 percent versus 2.1 percent, although that difference did not reach conventional statistical significance. The trial therefore established benefit for this specific prescription product in a selected high-risk population, not for generic fish oil or triglyceride lowering by any method. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792))

The mineral-oil comparator complicates interpretation. LDL-C and several inflammatory and atherosclerosis-related biomarkers increased in the mineral-oil group, while remaining comparatively stable in the icosapent ethyl group. Because the trial did not include a neutral third comparator, it cannot determine how much these mineral-oil-associated changes contributed to the difference in events. This uncertainty does not erase the randomized outcome, but it makes the size and mechanism of the benefit harder to attribute entirely to icosapent ethyl. Regulatory analyses estimated that, if the LDL-C and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein changes were causal, they might account for roughly 3 percent of the net clinical benefit, making it unlikely that mineral oil explains most of the 25 percent relative risk reduction. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792); [Ridker et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.122.059410)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35762321/))

### PROMINENT

PROMINENT randomized 10,497 patients with type 2 diabetes, triglycerides of 200 to 499 mg/dL, and low HDL-C. At four months, the placebo-adjusted effects of pemafibrate were reductions in triglycerides of 26.2 percent, VLDL cholesterol of 25.8 percent, remnant cholesterol of 25.6 percent, and apoC-III of 27.6 percent. ApoB increased by 4.8 percent, and cardiovascular events were not reduced. The hazard ratio for the primary endpoint was 1.03. ([Das Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342113/))

PROMINENT is important because it separated improvement in triglyceride-related concentrations from improvement in clinical outcomes. The fall in triglycerides and remnant cholesterol, accompanied by a 4.8 percent increase in apoB, is consistent with altered particle composition rather than a reduction in the overall number of circulating apoB particles. ([Das Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342113/))

### STRENGTH

STRENGTH randomized 13,078 statin-treated high-risk patients with elevated triglycerides and low HDL-C to 4 g per day of an EPA-DHA carboxylic-acid formulation or corn oil. The trial was stopped for futility, with a hazard ratio of 0.99 for the primary cardiovascular endpoint. This further shows that cardiovascular benefit cannot be inferred from omega-3 use or triglyceride lowering alone. ([Nicholls et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667577/))

Taken together, these trials show that a reduction in triglycerides is not by itself a reliable surrogate for cardiovascular benefit. Outcomes may depend on the specific drug, its effects on apoB-containing particles and other biological pathways, the comparator, and the population studied. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792); [Das Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [Nicholls et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258))

## Funding and conflict-of-interest context

The major outcome trials in this area were manufacturer-funded. REDUCE-IT was funded by Amarin Pharma, the manufacturer of icosapent ethyl. PROMINENT was funded by Kowa Research Institute, the developer of pemafibrate. STRENGTH was funded by AstraZeneca, which developed the omega-3 formulation tested in that trial. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792); [Das Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [Nicholls et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792))

Industry funding does not make a result false. In this case, both the strongly positive REDUCE-IT trial and the negative PROMINENT and STRENGTH trials were industry-funded. It does mean that conclusions should be based on convergence across independent cohorts, genetic studies, systematic reviews, and multiple randomized trials rather than any single manufacturer-sponsored study. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792); [Das Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [Nicholls et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258))

## The triglyceride-to-HDL ratio

The triglyceride-to-HDL-C ratio combines two features of atherogenic dyslipidemia: elevated triglycerides and low HDL-C. It has been proposed as an inexpensive proxy for insulin resistance and for the presence of smaller, denser LDL particles. ([McLaughlin et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-139-10-200311180-00007); [Hanak et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjcard.2004.03.069))

In a 2003 study of 258 overweight adults without diabetes, a ratio of at least 3.0 when both values were expressed in mg/dL had 64 percent sensitivity and 68 percent specificity for insulin resistance measured with an insulin-suppression test. That is enough to make the ratio a possible screening clue, but not accurate enough to diagnose or exclude insulin resistance in an individual. ([McLaughlin et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-139-10-200311180-00007)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14623617/))

A 2024 systematic review included 32 studies and 49,782 participants. Most studies were cross-sectional and commonly defined insulin resistance using HOMA-IR rather than direct clamp-based or insulin-suppression methods. The relationship was generally present, but the best-performing thresholds varied by sex, ethnicity, study population, and the method used to define insulin resistance. ([Baneu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12071493)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39062066/))

Ethnic variation is particularly important. In a study of 98 overweight African American adults, the TG/HDL-C ratio performed poorly as a marker of insulin resistance, with an area under the receiver-operating curve of 0.56. The authors concluded that fasting triglycerides and the ratio were not reliable insulin-resistance markers in that population. ([Sumner et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.165.12.1395)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15983289/))

The ratio can also correlate with LDL phenotype. In a study of 658 samples assessed with nuclear magnetic resonance, a higher TG/HDL-C ratio was associated with the small dense LDL pattern known as phenotype B. However, the ratio remained a proxy and did not directly measure LDL particle size or particle number. ([Hanak et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjcard.2004.03.069)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15246907/))

The ratio is unit-dependent. A ratio of 3.0 when triglycerides and HDL-C are both entered in mg/dL corresponds to approximately 1.3 after directly converting both values to mmol/L. Published studies have sometimes used separately derived or rounded SI thresholds, so values such as 3.0 or 1.3 should not be treated as universal biological cutoffs. ([Bittner et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ahj.2008.11.014); [Baneu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12071493))

The most defensible interpretation is that the TG/HDL-C ratio is a useful low-cost clue when it agrees with the rest of the metabolic picture. It is not a diagnostic test for insulin resistance, a direct measurement of small dense LDL, or a substitute for assessing glucose regulation, blood pressure, adiposity, apoB, and non-HDL-C. ([McLaughlin et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-139-10-200311180-00007); [Baneu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12071493); [Sumner et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.165.12.1395); [Hanak et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjcard.2004.03.069))

## Fasting versus non-fasting triglycerides

Triglycerides are the most meal-sensitive component of the standard lipid panel. This does not mean non-fasting values are invalid. It means the fasting state should be known and the result interpreted against an appropriate threshold. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

In the Women's Health Study, non-fasting triglycerides were independently associated with cardiovascular events, with particularly strong associations when blood was collected two to four hours after a meal. However, the fasting and non-fasting groups contained different participants, and meals were not standardized. ([Bansal et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.3.309)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17635891/))

In the Copenhagen City Heart Study, higher non-fasting triglycerides were associated with myocardial infarction, ischemic heart disease, and death during long-term follow-up in both women and men. These studies established that a recent meal does not remove the prognostic value of the triglyceride result. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.3.299)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17635890/))

A stronger comparison came from 8,270 participants who had both fasting and non-fasting lipid measurements approximately four weeks apart. Fasting and non-fasting lipid concentrations had similar associations with incident coronary and atherosclerotic cardiovascular events, with high concordance in clinical risk categories. This supports comparable clinical utility rather than a claim that non-fasting triglycerides are always superior. ([Mora et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.0392)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31135812/))

The European Atherosclerosis Society and European Federation of Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine recommend routine non-fasting lipid testing. Their consensus recommends that laboratory reports flag fasting triglycerides at or above 150 mg/dL, or 1.7 mmol/L, and non-fasting triglycerides at or above 175 mg/dL, or 2.0 mmol/L. The same statement recommends considering a fasting repeat when non-fasting triglycerides exceed 440 mg/dL, or 5.0 mmol/L. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27122601/))

Non-fasting testing is therefore a reasonable routine default because it is convenient and predicts risk at least comparably in most settings. Fasting measurement remains useful when non-fasting triglycerides exceed 440 mg/dL, when severe hypertriglyceridemia is already known, when following recovery from hypertriglyceridemic pancreatitis, or before starting drugs that may cause severe hypertriglyceridemia. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27122601/))

## Remnant cholesterol: useful estimate, imperfect measurement

Remnant cholesterol is intended to represent the cholesterol carried in triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and their remnants. In routine practice it is often calculated as follows. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

**Remnant cholesterol = total cholesterol - HDL-C - LDL-C**

This is an estimate, not a direct count of remnant particles. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

When LDL-C itself is estimated with the Friedewald equation, calculated remnant cholesterol becomes mathematically equivalent to triglycerides divided by 5 in mg/dL, or divided by approximately 2.2 in mmol/L. In that situation, the remnant-cholesterol value is largely a repackaging of the triglyceride result rather than an independent measurement. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

Immunoseparation methods, density-based ultracentrifugation, and calculated approaches do not necessarily measure the same remnant fractions. A comparison of these three approaches found little agreement among them, which limits interchangeability between studies and laboratories. So-called direct assays are not standardized as a universal gold standard and may also capture non-remnant triglyceride-rich lipoproteins. ([Toth et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacl.2019.09.001)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31629703/)) ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551))

In a cohort of 16,207 people, direct remnant-cholesterol measurement identified an additional group representing approximately 5 percent of participants who had cholesterol-rich but relatively triglyceride-poor remnants that were missed by the calculated value. These participants had approximately 1.8 times the risk of myocardial infarction. This suggests that direct and calculated remnant cholesterol are not interchangeable, although this was an observational cohort and it did not test or establish a treatment threshold. ([Varbo and Nordestgaard, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab293)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34023898/))

ApoB answers a related but different question: it estimates the total number of circulating atherogenic lipoprotein particles. Non-HDL-C estimates the cholesterol carried across all apoB-containing particles. Calculated remnant cholesterol assigns part of that cholesterol to the triglyceride-rich fraction. None of these measurements is a perfect substitute for the others. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

## Very high triglycerides are a different clinical problem

At mild to moderate elevations, the discussion is mainly about metabolic health and long-term cardiovascular risk. At very high concentrations, pancreatitis becomes a more immediate concern. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

A European consensus identifies triglycerides above 880 mg/dL, or 10 mmol/L, as a potentially life-threatening concentration requiring attention because of pancreatitis risk. Risk does not suddenly begin at one exact threshold, and concern increases as levels rise, particularly once they approach or exceed approximately 1,000 mg/dL. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27122601/))

The two CORE phase 3 trials randomized 1,063 adults with triglycerides of at least 500 mg/dL; 1,061 were included in the primary analysis. At six months, the placebo-adjusted least-squares mean reductions in triglycerides ranged from approximately 49 to 72 percentage points, depending on dose and trial. Olezarsen also reduced adjudicated acute-pancreatitis events in the pooled analysis, with a rate ratio of 0.15. The higher dose was associated with more liver-enzyme elevations, thrombocytopenia, and hepatic fat accumulation. These trials provide outcome evidence that olezarsen can reduce acute-pancreatitis events in adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia while substantially lowering triglycerides, but they do not establish that the triglyceride change itself mediated the benefit or establish a cardiovascular benefit. The trials were funded by Ionis Pharmaceuticals, the manufacturer of olezarsen. ([Marston et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2512761)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2512761))

## Verdict on the competing claims

- **"Triglycerides are independently causal for cardiovascular disease."** Directionally correct, but too simple. Genetic evidence supports a causal role for triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and remnants. The routine triglyceride concentration is an indirect marker, and the causal exposure appears to involve the apoB-containing particles, their cholesterol cargo, and possibly products of triglyceride hydrolysis rather than the triglyceride molecule alone. ([Varbo et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.08.1026); [Ference et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.20045); [Björnson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehad337)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265341/))

- **"Triglycerides are primarily a marker of metabolic dysfunction."** Correct but incomplete. Elevated triglycerides frequently signal insulin resistance, obesity or diabetes, alcohol exposure, or another metabolic or secondary driver. However, the associated remnant and VLDL particles probably also contribute directly to atherosclerosis. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Berglund et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2011-3213)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

- **"The TG/HDL-C ratio is one of the most useful and underutilized measurements on a lipid panel."** Overstated. It is inexpensive and can provide a useful metabolic clue, but its diagnostic performance is modest, thresholds vary, and accuracy differs across populations. It should support, not replace, more direct clinical assessment. ([McLaughlin et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-139-10-200311180-00007); [Baneu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12071493); [Sumner et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.165.12.1395)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14623617/))

- **"Non-fasting triglycerides should be the standard because they predict events better."** Routine non-fasting testing is well supported, but the reason should be stated more carefully. It is easier for patients and predicts cardiovascular risk similarly in most settings. Some cohorts found stronger associations after meals, while direct within-person comparisons found broadly comparable prediction. Fasting and non-fasting measurements are complementary rather than competitors. ([Bansal et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.3.309); [Mora et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.0392); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17635891/))


## Quick reference values

- The EAS/EFLM consensus recommends that laboratory reports flag fasting triglycerides at or above 150 mg/dL, or 1.7 mmol/L. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

- The same consensus recommends that laboratory reports flag non-fasting triglycerides at or above 175 mg/dL, or 2.0 mmol/L. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

- A non-fasting result above 440 mg/dL, or 5.0 mmol/L, is a reason to consider repeating the measurement while fasting. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

- Triglycerides above 880 mg/dL, or 10 mmol/L, represent a potentially life-threatening concentration because of pancreatitis risk. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152))

- A TG/HDL-C ratio around 3.0 using mg/dL values can be treated as a possible metabolic flag, not a diagnostic cutoff. ([McLaughlin et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-139-10-200311180-00007))

- A ratio of 3.0 using mg/dL values corresponds to approximately 1.3 after direct conversion to mmol/L, but published SI thresholds vary. ([Bittner et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ahj.2008.11.014); [Baneu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12071493))

- Calculated remnant cholesterol is total cholesterol minus HDL-C minus LDL-C. When LDL-C is estimated with the Friedewald equation, calculated remnant cholesterol is effectively triglycerides divided by 5 in mg/dL. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))


## Practical interpretation

- A triglyceride result should not be interpreted in isolation. ApoB or non-HDL-C provides additional information about atherogenic particle burden, while glucose regulation, adiposity, alcohol exposure, medications, and medical conditions provide metabolic context. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551); [Berglund et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2011-3213)) ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8670783/))

- Mild or moderate elevation often signals increased VLDL and remnant traffic and should prompt investigation of the metabolic or secondary driver. It does not prove that the triglyceride molecule itself is directly damaging the artery. ([Ginsberg et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehab551))

- A high TG/HDL-C ratio strengthens suspicion of an insulin-resistant or atherogenic-dyslipidemia pattern, but it cannot diagnose insulin resistance or directly measure LDL particle size. ([Baneu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12071493); [Hanak et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjcard.2004.03.069)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39062066/))

- Non-fasting testing is suitable for routine cardiovascular assessment. A fasting repeat becomes more useful when non-fasting triglycerides exceed 440 mg/dL or when severe hypertriglyceridemia or hypertriglyceridemic pancreatitis is relevant. ([Nordestgaard et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehw152)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27122601/))

- Cardiovascular benefit should never be inferred from the percentage reduction in triglycerides alone. REDUCE-IT showed benefit with one specific EPA treatment, while PROMINENT and STRENGTH showed that substantial triglyceride lowering can occur without fewer cardiovascular events. ([Bhatt et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792); [Das Pradhan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2210645); [Nicholls et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792))

- At severe concentrations, the clinical priority changes. Preventing pancreatitis and identifying genetic, metabolic, medication-related, or dietary drivers becomes the immediate priority. ([Berglund et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2011-3213); [Marston et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2512761)) ([New England Journal of Medicine](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2512761))
