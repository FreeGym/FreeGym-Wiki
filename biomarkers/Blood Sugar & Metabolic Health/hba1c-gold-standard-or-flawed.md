# HbA1c: Gold Standard or Flawed?

HbA1c is best understood as a standardized, clinically validated proxy for chronic glucose exposure and glycemic risk, not as a direct glucose measurement. It deserves its central role in diabetes care because it is guideline-recognized and linked to microvascular risk, but it becomes misleading when the biology of hemoglobin, red blood cell lifespan, or glycation differs from the assumptions built into the test. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00592-020-01606-5 "Diagnosing type 2 diabetes using Hemoglobin A1c: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the diagnostic cutpoint based on microvascular complications | Acta Diabetologica | Springer Nature Link"), [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/193229680900300307 "A Review of Variant Hemoglobins Interfering with Hemoglobin A1c Measurement - Randie R. Little, William L. Roberts, 2009"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

The strongest position is not "HbA1c is useless" or "HbA1c is perfect." It is: **HbA1c is a strong single marker, but a weak standalone truth test.**

## What HbA1c is and is not

HbA1c forms when glucose non-enzymatically binds hemoglobin inside red blood cells. Because red blood cells circulate for roughly 120 days, HbA1c reflects glycemic exposure over the prior 2 to 3 months. ([Annals of Palliative Medicine](https://doi.org/10.21037/apm-21-2944 "Systematic review and meta-analysis: influence of iron deficiency anemia on blood glycosylated hemoglobin in diabetic patients - Kuang - Annals of Palliative Medicine"), [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"))

HbA1c is clinically attractive because it does not require fasting, is less affected by immediate food intake or short-term day-to-day glucose fluctuation than a single glucose draw, and is easier to obtain than an oral glucose tolerance test. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

But HbA1c does **not** measure glucose itself. It measures glycation of hemoglobin. Anything that changes red blood cell survival, hemoglobin structure, fetal hemoglobin, chemically modified hemoglobin, recent blood loss, transfusion, or the assay's ability to detect glycated hemoglobin can shift HbA1c independently of actual glucose exposure. Conditions that shorten red blood cell survival can falsely lower HbA1c regardless of assay method. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/193229680900300307 "A Review of Variant Hemoglobins Interfering with Hemoglobin A1c Measurement - Randie R. Little, William L. Roberts, 2009"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

## Why HbA1c became a diagnostic marker

Current diabetes criteria commonly use HbA1c >=6.5% as a diagnostic threshold and 5.7-6.4% as a prediabetes range. These thresholds are diagnostic conventions tied to population risk evidence and guideline definitions, not precise biological cliffs for every patient. In asymptomatic people, an abnormal diagnostic result generally requires confirmation with repeat testing or another abnormal diagnostic test. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00592-020-01606-5 "Diagnosing type 2 diabetes using Hemoglobin A1c: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the diagnostic cutpoint based on microvascular complications | Acta Diabetologica | Springer Nature Link"), [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

The 6.5% threshold was anchored to epidemiologic evidence linking HbA1c to retinopathy risk, especially moderate retinopathy. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that retinopathy prevalence was higher in people with HbA1c >=6.5% than in those below that range, and concluded that the strongest evidence supports the 6.5% cutpoint for moderate retinopathy while noting that a single threshold may not work equally well across age and race groups. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00592-020-01606-5 "Diagnosing type 2 diabetes using Hemoglobin A1c: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the diagnostic cutpoint based on microvascular complications | Acta Diabetologica | Springer Nature Link"))

That makes HbA1c a risk-linked marker. It does not make it an infallible diagnostic gold standard. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00592-020-01606-5 "Diagnosing type 2 diabetes using Hemoglobin A1c: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the diagnostic cutpoint based on microvascular complications | Acta Diabetologica | Springer Nature Link"), [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"))

## Diagnostic accuracy: high specificity, modest sensitivity

When HbA1c is compared with OGTT-defined diabetes, it tends to be specific but not very sensitive. A 2023 systematic review and network meta-analysis of 73 studies estimated HbA1c >=6.5% sensitivity at 0.51 and specificity at 0.96 against OGTT-defined diabetes. In plain terms: HbA1c alone is good at ruling in diabetes when clearly high, but it can miss many cases detected by OGTT. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"))

The same analysis estimated fasting plasma glucose sensitivity at 0.49 and specificity at 0.98, while the combined rule of HbA1c **or** fasting glucose increased sensitivity to 0.64 with specificity of 0.95. That supports a multi-marker strategy when the diagnostic stakes are high, though it also means more false positives than the most specific single tests. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"))

This problem is more visible in some populations. A systematic review and meta-analysis of African studies found that HbA1c >=6.5% had sensitivity of 57.7% and specificity of 92.3% compared with OGTT, leading the authors to conclude that HbA1c at the 6.5% threshold may miss almost half of OGTT-defined type 2 diabetes cases in that setting. ([ePrints Soton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/504749/ "The diagnostic accuracy of HbA1c, compared to the oral glucose tolerance test, for screening for type 2 diabetes mellitus in Africa - A systematic review and meta-analysis - ePrints Soton"))

## Iron deficiency and anemia

Iron deficiency anemia can falsely elevate HbA1c. A systematic review concluded that iron deficiency and iron deficiency anemia are associated with a spurious increase in HbA1c, while other non-iron-deficiency anemias may lower HbA1c; the review also emphasized that the effect varies by anemia type and severity. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25994072/ "The effect of anaemia and abnormalities of erythrocyte indices on HbA1c analysis - PubMed"))

A later systematic review and meta-analysis in diabetic patients with iron deficiency anemia found higher HbA1c in iron-deficient patients than controls, with a pooled mean difference of 0.84 percentage points, though heterogeneity was very high. That means the direction of bias is plausible and clinically important, but the exact correction factor is not reliable enough to apply mechanically. ([Annals of Palliative Medicine](https://doi.org/10.21037/apm-21-2944 "Systematic review and meta-analysis: influence of iron deficiency anemia on blood glycosylated hemoglobin in diabetic patients - Kuang - Annals of Palliative Medicine"))

Iron replacement tends to lower HbA1c in iron-deficient patients. A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis concluded that iron replacement therapy decreases HbA1c in both diabetic and nondiabetic populations, which supports the concern that some apparent "glycemic improvement" after iron treatment may reflect correction of HbA1c bias rather than true glucose change. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38068338/ "The Effect of Iron Replacement Therapy on HbA1c Levels in Diabetic and Nondiabetic Patients - PubMed"))

The practical implication is simple: an unexpectedly high HbA1c should be interpreted cautiously when ferritin, hemoglobin, mean corpuscular volume, or clinical context suggests iron deficiency. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25994072/ "The effect of anaemia and abnormalities of erythrocyte indices on HbA1c analysis - PubMed"), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38068338/ "The Effect of Iron Replacement Therapy on HbA1c Levels in Diabetic and Nondiabetic Patients - PubMed"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"))

## Hemoglobin variants and assay method

Hemoglobin variants can cause either biological bias or assay interference. The common variants include HbS, HbC, HbE, and HbD; the direction and size of the error depend on the variant and the laboratory method used. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/193229680900300307 "A Review of Variant Hemoglobins Interfering with Hemoglobin A1c Measurement - Randie R. Little, William L. Roberts, 2009"))

This is why "HbA1c is affected by sickle trait" is incomplete. Some methods are affected; others are not. A technical review emphasized that clinically significant interference exists for some assay-variant combinations, and that laboratories need to know the limitations of the specific method they use. NGSP also maintains method-specific interference information. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/193229680900300307 "A Review of Variant Hemoglobins Interfering with Hemoglobin A1c Measurement - Randie R. Little, William L. Roberts, 2009"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"))

Sickle cell trait also illustrates that the issue is not always falsely high HbA1c. In a cohort study of 4,620 African American participants, those with sickle cell trait had lower HbA1c than those without trait at the same fasting glucose and 2-hour glucose. The difference was about -0.29 to -0.30 percentage points, and HbA1c-defined prediabetes and diabetes were less common in those with sickle cell trait. ([Johns Hopkins University](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/association-of-sickle-cell-trait-with-hemoglobin-asub1csub-in-afr/ "Association of sickle cell trait with hemoglobin A1c in African Americans - Johns Hopkins University"))

In areas where hemoglobin variants are common, assay choice matters a lot. A study comparing immunoassay and capillary electrophoresis in 845 diabetic samples found abnormal hemoglobin types in 34.3% of samples, mostly HbE, and concluded that hemoglobin typing should be considered before HbA1c interpretation in high-prevalence hemoglobinopathy populations. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3271/9/2/43 "Misleading HbA1c Measurement in Diabetic Patients with Hemoglobin Variants | MDPI"))

## Race, ethnicity, and HbA1c-glucose discordance

At the same measured glucose exposure, HbA1c can differ by race or ethnicity. In a Kaiser Permanente study using 1,788 patients, 5,264 HbA1c results, and more than 124 million CGM readings, African American patients had HbA1c values 0.33 percentage points higher than white patients at the same mean glucose. The same study found that most variation was within individuals rather than between racial groups, which argues against crude race-based "correction" and in favor of individualized interpretation supported by direct glucose data. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/dia.2023.0153 "Racial and Ethnic Differences in the Association Between Mean Glucose and Hemoglobin A1c - Andrew J. Karter, Melissa M. Parker, Howard H. Moffet, Lisa K. Gilliam, 2023"))

A GRADE randomized trial substudy also found that, at the same average glucose, non-Hispanic Black participants had HbA1c values about 0.2 to 0.6 percentage points higher than non-Hispanic white participants across a glucose range of 100 to 250 mg/dL, even after adjustment for measured covariates. ([Health Sciences Research Commons](https://hsrc.himmelfarb.gwu.edu/gwhpubs/5600/ "Relationship Between Average Glucose Levels and HbA1c Differs Across Racial Groups - Health Sciences Research Commons"))

Population HbA1c differences are also reported in people without diabetes. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that, compared with white individuals, HbA1c was higher by 0.26 percentage points in Black individuals, 0.24 percentage points in Asian individuals, and 0.08 percentage points in Latino individuals without diabetes. The authors cautioned that even small differences may matter when HbA1c is used as the sole diagnostic cutoff. ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0171315 "Effect of ethnicity on HbA1c levels in individuals without diabetes: Systematic review and meta-analysis | PLOS One"))

The mechanism is still debated. The cited studies and assay guidance point toward several possible contributors, including red blood cell lifespan, glycation rate, hemoglobin variants, iron status, kidney disease, socioeconomic exposure, and residual differences in actual glucose patterns. The important clinical point is that the difference can be large enough to cross decision thresholds in some patients, but the variation is mostly within individuals, not between racial groups, so a simple race-specific adjustment is not justified by these data. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/193229680900300307 "A Review of Variant Hemoglobins Interfering with Hemoglobin A1c Measurement - Randie R. Little, William L. Roberts, 2009"), [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/dia.2023.0153 "Racial and Ethnic Differences in the Association Between Mean Glucose and Hemoglobin A1c - Andrew J. Karter, Melissa M. Parker, Howard H. Moffet, Lisa K. Gilliam, 2023"), [Health Sciences Research Commons](https://hsrc.himmelfarb.gwu.edu/gwhpubs/5600/ "Relationship Between Average Glucose Levels and HbA1c Differs Across Racial Groups - Health Sciences Research Commons"), [PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0171315 "Effect of ethnicity on HbA1c levels in individuals without diabetes: Systematic review and meta-analysis | PLOS One"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"))

## HbA1c hides glucose variability

HbA1c compresses glucose exposure into one number. That is useful, but it hides whether the average came from stable glucose, frequent hypoglycemia plus hyperglycemia, large post-meal spikes, or sustained moderate elevation. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1932296818822496 "The Relationships Between Time in Range, Hyperglycemia Metrics, and HbA1c - Roy W. Beck, Richard M. Bergenstal, Peiyao Cheng, Craig Kollman, Anders L. Carlson, Mary L. Johnson, David Rodbard, 2019"))

CGM studies show why this matters. In a study relating time-in-range metrics to HbA1c in adults with type 1 diabetes, time in range of about 70% corresponded roughly to HbA1c 7%, and every 10 percentage point increase in time in range corresponded to about 0.6 percentage points lower HbA1c on average. But the study also found wide HbA1c variation for any given time-in-range value and emphasized that HbA1c does not show hypoglycemia, glycemic variability, or daily glucose patterns. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1932296818822496 "The Relationships Between Time in Range, Hyperglycemia Metrics, and HbA1c - Roy W. Beck, Richard M. Bergenstal, Peiyao Cheng, Craig Kollman, Anders L. Carlson, Mary L. Johnson, David Rodbard, 2019"))

Evidence linking glucose variability to outcomes is suggestive but not as definitive as the classic HbA1c complication literature. Reviews report associations between glycemic variability and microvascular complications, macrovascular complications, hypoglycemia, and mortality, but they also note inconsistent definitions, observational confounding, and the need for better prospective evidence. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12933-020-01085-6 "Glycemic variability: adverse clinical outcomes and how to improve it? | Cardiovascular Diabetology | Springer Nature Link"))

Long-term HbA1c variability also appears clinically relevant. In a cohort of 1,869 people with type 2 diabetes followed for a median of 9.5 years, HbA1c variability predicted several outcomes, including kidney deterioration, all-cause mortality, and cardiovascular mortality. ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016882272200883X "Mean HbA1c and HbA1c variability are associated with differing diabetes-related complications in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus - ScienceDirect"))

CGM improves the measurement of patterns, but current diagnostic guidance still relies on laboratory glycemic tests rather than CGM for diagnosis, and the long-term outcome evidence for CGM-derived metrics is still developing. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of CGM trials in adults with type 2 diabetes found that CGM modestly reduced HbA1c and improved time-in-range, time-below-range, time-above-range, and glycemic variability, but no included trials reported microvascular outcomes. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-024-06107-6 "Continuous glucose monitoring in adults with type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis | Diabetologia | Springer Nature Link"), [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

## What the competing claims get right

The claim that **HbA1c is a highly practical single marker** is mostly right for routine screening and monitoring when the result fits clinical context. It is convenient, standardized, clinically validated for diagnosis and monitoring, and linked to complication risk. But "best practical marker" is not the same as "gold standard for true glucose exposure." ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00592-020-01606-5 "Diagnosing type 2 diabetes using Hemoglobin A1c: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the diagnostic cutpoint based on microvascular complications | Acta Diabetologica | Springer Nature Link"), [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

The claim that **HbA1c misclassifies significant numbers of people** is also right, especially when HbA1c is compared with OGTT or used in people with anemia, hemoglobin variants, sickle cell trait, pregnancy, chronic kidney disease, advanced liver disease, recent blood loss, transfusion, hemodialysis, erythropoietin therapy, or discordant glucose readings. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25994072/ "The effect of anaemia and abnormalities of erythrocyte indices on HbA1c analysis - PubMed"), [Johns Hopkins University](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/association-of-sickle-cell-trait-with-hemoglobin-asub1csub-in-afr/ "Association of sickle cell trait with hemoglobin A1c in African Americans - Johns Hopkins University"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29305791/ "Utility and Limitations of Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) in Patients with Liver Cirrhosis as Compared with Oral Glucose Tolerance Test for Diagnosis of Diabetes - PubMed"))

The claim that **HbA1c hides glucose variability** is clearly right. HbA1c cannot show time below range, time above range, post-prandial spikes, overnight hypoglycemia, or day-to-day instability. CGM captures these patterns better, though the long-term outcome evidence for CGM-derived variability metrics is still developing. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1932296818822496 "The Relationships Between Time in Range, Hyperglycemia Metrics, and HbA1c - Roy W. Beck, Richard M. Bergenstal, Peiyao Cheng, Craig Kollman, Anders L. Carlson, Mary L. Johnson, David Rodbard, 2019"), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-024-06107-6 "Continuous glucose monitoring in adults with type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis | Diabetologia | Springer Nature Link"))

The claim that **HbA1c should be interpreted alongside another marker** is the most clinically defensible position. "Always" may be too absolute for stable monitoring in a person with known diabetes and no reason to suspect interference, but for diagnosis, borderline values, discordant symptoms, high-risk populations, anemia, hemoglobinopathy, pregnancy, kidney disease, advanced liver disease, recent transfusion, or treatment decisions with major consequences, HbA1c should not stand alone. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29305791/ "Utility and Limitations of Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) in Patients with Liver Cirrhosis as Compared with Oral Glucose Tolerance Test for Diagnosis of Diabetes - PubMed"))

## Quick reference values

- **Diagnostic convention:** HbA1c >=6.5% is commonly used for diabetes; 5.7-6.4% is commonly used for prediabetes. In asymptomatic people, abnormal diagnostic results generally require confirmation. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

- **HbA1c vs OGTT diagnostic accuracy:** pooled sensitivity about 0.51 and specificity about 0.96 in a 2023 systematic review and network meta-analysis. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"))

- **HbA1c or fasting glucose combined:** sensitivity about 0.64 and specificity about 0.95 against OGTT-defined diabetes. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"))

- **Africa meta-analysis:** HbA1c >=6.5% sensitivity 57.7% and specificity 92.3% versus OGTT. ([ePrints Soton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/504749/ "The diagnostic accuracy of HbA1c, compared to the oral glucose tolerance test, for screening for type 2 diabetes mellitus in Africa - A systematic review and meta-analysis - ePrints Soton"))

- **Iron deficiency anemia:** pooled HbA1c difference about +0.84 percentage points in diabetic patients with iron deficiency anemia, but with very high heterogeneity. ([Annals of Palliative Medicine](https://doi.org/10.21037/apm-21-2944 "Systematic review and meta-analysis: influence of iron deficiency anemia on blood glycosylated hemoglobin in diabetic patients - Kuang - Annals of Palliative Medicine"))

- **CGM-measured mean glucose discordance:** African American patients had HbA1c about +0.33 percentage points higher than white patients at the same mean CGM glucose in one large health-system study. ([Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/dia.2023.0153 "Racial and Ethnic Differences in the Association Between Mean Glucose and Hemoglobin A1c - Andrew J. Karter, Melissa M. Parker, Howard H. Moffet, Lisa K. Gilliam, 2023"))

- **Sickle cell trait:** HbA1c may be lower, not higher, at the same glucose exposure; one cohort found differences around -0.29 to -0.30 percentage points. ([Johns Hopkins University](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/association-of-sickle-cell-trait-with-hemoglobin-asub1csub-in-afr/ "Association of sickle cell trait with hemoglobin A1c in African Americans - Johns Hopkins University"))


## Practical interpretation

HbA1c is appropriate as a first-line marker when the result fits the clinical picture and there is no obvious reason for red blood cell or hemoglobin distortion. ([American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis "Diabetes diagnosis - American Diabetes Association"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

HbA1c is **not** enough when the value is borderline, surprising, or being used to make a major diagnostic or treatment decision. In those cases, fasting glucose, OGTT, fructosamine or glycated albumin, SMBG, CGM, complete blood count, ferritin, kidney function, and hemoglobin variant assessment can all clarify whether HbA1c is reflecting glucose or reflecting hematology. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/193229680900300307 "A Review of Variant Hemoglobins Interfering with Hemoglobin A1c Measurement - Randie R. Little, William L. Roberts, 2009"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"))

HbA1c may also be falsely low when red blood cell survival is shortened, such as with hemolysis, recent blood loss, or recovery after bleeding. In chronic kidney disease, especially dialysis, interpretation can be difficult because anemia, erythropoietin use, altered red blood cell survival, and uremia-related assay issues may distort the HbA1c-glucose relationship. In cirrhosis, HbA1c can also be difficult to interpret, especially when anemia or advanced liver disease is present. ([NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"), [American Diabetes Association Standards](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes "2. Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2026"), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29305791/ "Utility and Limitations of Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) in Patients with Liver Cirrhosis as Compared with Oral Glucose Tolerance Test for Diagnosis of Diabetes - PubMed"))

The most balanced conclusion is: **HbA1c is not the gold standard for glucose; it is the best-standardized shortcut for long-term glycemic risk. Shortcuts are useful. They are also where misclassification happens.** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00592-020-01606-5 "Diagnosing type 2 diabetes using Hemoglobin A1c: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the diagnostic cutpoint based on microvascular complications | Acta Diabetologica | Springer Nature Link"), [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1016381/full "Frontiers | Comparison of diagnostic accuracy for diabetes diagnosis: A systematic review and network meta-analysis"), [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1932296818822496 "The Relationships Between Time in Range, Hyperglycemia Metrics, and HbA1c - Roy W. Beck, Richard M. Bergenstal, Peiyao Cheng, Craig Kollman, Anders L. Carlson, Mary L. Johnson, David Rodbard, 2019"), [NGSP](https://ngsp.org/factors.asp "Factors that interfere with HbA1c test results - NGSP"))
