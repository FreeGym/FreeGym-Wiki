# The Kraft Test: Insulin Curves as Early Warning, or Fringe Diagnostic?

The Kraft test occupies an awkward middle ground. Its core physiological premise is credible: measuring insulin during an oral glucose tolerance test can directly characterize the timing, persistence, and magnitude of the post-load insulin response, information that fasting insulin, fasting glucose, and HbA1c do not directly measure. Prospective evidence also shows that a late insulin peak during a standard OGTT predicts future type 2 diabetes. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

The cited Kraft studies did not prospectively test the stronger claim that Joseph Kraft's particular Pattern I-V categories constitute distinct diagnostic stages of "occult diabetes," reliably identify disease decades early, predict cardiovascular events, or improve outcomes when used to guide treatment. The most defensible description is therefore **a nonstandard dynamic metabolic phenotyping protocol built around a real early-risk signal, but not a validated clinical diagnosis or routine screening test**. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

## What the Kraft test actually is

Kraft's original protocol was not simply a contemporary standard 75 g OGTT with extra blood draws. His 1975 work used a 100 g oral glucose load, with glucose and insulin generally measured fasting and at 30, 60, 120, and 180 minutes. ([Kraft, 1975](https://academic.oup.com/labmed/article-abstract/6/2/10/2641019)) Later descriptions of the archive report approximately 15,000 referred patients and healthy volunteers tested at St. Joseph Hospital in Chicago between 1972 and 1992, with venous sampling extending between three and five hours in some tests. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))

Under Kraft's 1975 definitions, a normal response required:

- Fasting insulin below 30 μU/mL.

- An insulin peak at 30 or 60 minutes.

- A relatively rapid decline, with the sum of the two- and three-hour insulin concentrations below 60 μU/mL. ([Kraft, 1975](https://academic.oup.com/labmed/article-abstract/6/2/10/2641019), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))


An elevated fasting value, a peak delayed until two hours or later, or a slow decline could place someone into a hyperinsulinemic category. A low-insulin pattern was assigned when every value was 30 μU/mL or lower. Later Crofts/Kraft reanalyses reconstructed and simplified the original algorithm while reducing the number of unclassified cases, producing the commonly used labels I, IIA, IIB, III, IV, and V. This is why modern descriptions sometimes refer to five patterns and sometimes to six labels. ([Crofts et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27344544/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))

Pattern V should not automatically be interpreted as beta-cell exhaustion. The category itself establishes only that peripheral insulin concentrations remained low during the test. Circulating insulin reflects both pancreatic secretion and insulin clearance, and those processes cannot be separated from an insulin curve alone. ([Polidori et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26993071/)) In a 2022 adapted mixed-meal study, insulin-sensitive participants were primarily assigned to Pattern I or Pattern V. However, that study lowered the Pattern V cutoff from 30 to 15 μU/mL and used an insulin-sensitivity cutoff the authors described as not universally accepted and somewhat arbitrary. It therefore supports caution against automatically interpreting a low curve as pancreatic failure, but it does not validate the original Kraft Pattern V. ([Newman et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.877696/full))

## What Kraft's database found

A 2016 peer-reviewed reanalysis examined 4,185 people in Kraft's database who had normal glucose tolerance. Of these, 2,079 were classified as hyperinsulinemic by the reconstructed Kraft criteria. That is approximately 49.7 percent - about half, rather than a vast majority. Among participants with impaired glucose tolerance or diabetes, 93 percent had a concurrent hyperinsulinemic classification. Fasting insulin alone identified relatively few of the people categorized as hyperinsulinemic. ([Crofts et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27344544/))

This is a striking cross-sectional finding, but it does not establish prevalence in the general population. The reanalysis included men older than 20 years and women older than 45 years with recorded age, height, weight, normal glucose tolerance, and BMI above 17.9 kg/m²; women aged 20-45 were excluded because pregnancy was considered a potential confounder. The database consisted of referred patients and volunteers rather than a randomly sampled community cohort, and its reanalysis applied retrospective classifications to measurements collected over several decades. It can show that normal glucose tolerance and a high or delayed insulin curve frequently coexisted in that selected database; it cannot show what proportion of those people later developed diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or no clinically important disease at all. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))

The 2016 paper's authors proposed that hyperinsulinemia with normal glucose tolerance might provide earlier recognition of metabolic risk, but the wording was appropriately tentative and called for further research. Joseph Kraft was also a coauthor, making the paper a reanalysis of the original investigator's archive rather than an independent replication. ([Crofts et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27344544/))

## The part that has prospective support

A directly relevant prospective study by Hayashi and colleagues examined a separate insulin-curve classification, not the Kraft classifications. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/))

The investigators followed 400 nondiabetic Japanese Americans for 10 to 11 years after a standard 75 g, two-hour OGTT. Insulin was measured fasting and at 30, 60, and 120 minutes. Eighty-six participants developed type 2 diabetes. Cumulative diabetes incidences were 3.2, 9.8, 15.4, 47.8, and 37.5 percent for patterns 1 through 5. Adjusted odds ratios were 12.55 (95 percent CI 4.79-32.89) for pattern 4 and 8.34 (95 percent CI 2.38-29.27) for pattern 5 compared with patterns 1 and 2. The wide confidence intervals indicate limited precision. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/))

This study supports an important principle: **the timing and persistence of post-load insulin can carry prognostic information**. It does not validate Kraft's thresholds, his 100 g glucose load, the three- to five-hour protocol, or the diagnostic label "diabetes in situ." Hayashi's five patterns were independently defined from a shorter 75 g OGTT and should not be confused with Kraft Patterns I-V merely because both systems use numbered categories. Because this was a single Japanese-American cohort, the study itself did not establish transportability to other populations. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/))

Longitudinal metabolic data also support a compensatory phase before diabetes. In the Whitehall II cohort, trajectories of glucose, HOMA insulin sensitivity, and estimated beta-cell function began changing more sharply approximately three to six years before diabetes diagnosis, although observations extended as far as 13 years before diagnosis. This demonstrates that metabolic deterioration precedes diagnostic hyperglycemia, but it does not demonstrate that Kraft categories reliably identify disease "decades" in advance. ([Tabák et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19515410/))

The careful conclusion is therefore that **post-load insulin abnormalities can precede overt diabetes and predict risk over roughly a decade of follow-up, while the cited evidence does not establish a decades-long Kraft detection advantage**. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Tabák et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19515410/))

## What an insulin curve can - and cannot - tell us

A fasting insulin measurement cannot encode the timing of the insulin peak, the persistence of the response, or the speed at which insulin returns toward baseline. HOMA-IR is calculated from fasting glucose and insulin, so it likewise does not contain post-load timing or decay. In that structural sense, advocates are correct: dynamic testing measures information that fasting insulin and HOMA-IR do not contain. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919))

However, an elevated insulin concentration is not a direct measurement of either insulin resistance or beta-cell "stress." The concentration appearing in peripheral blood is the net result of insulin secretion, first-pass hepatic extraction, and extrahepatic clearance. Reduced insulin clearance can increase circulating insulin even without a proportionate increase in secretion. Day-to-day biological variation can also shift OGTT measurements and pattern assignments even when preparation is standardized. ([Polidori et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26993071/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

This means that a delayed or exaggerated curve may be a useful risk phenotype without uniquely identifying its mechanism. Treating each visual pattern as a discrete pathological stage imposes more mechanistic precision than the peripheral insulin concentration data provide. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Polidori et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26993071/))

## Does the Kraft test outperform HOMA-IR?

The Kraft literature shows that fasting insulin can miss people who satisfy Kraft's own dynamic definition. That is unsurprising because Kraft's classification is partly defined by post-load peak timing and decay. Those studies did not establish that Kraft patterns predict clinical outcomes better than HOMA-IR. ([Crofts et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27344544/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))

A 2019 analysis attempted to replace the full Kraft curve with fewer measurements. Among 4,185 normal-glucose participants, a two-hour insulin above 45 μU/mL gave sensitivity of 0.85 and specificity of 0.95 against the dichotomized Kraft classification, while a threshold above 30 μU/mL increased sensitivity to 0.98 but reduced specificity to 0.62. But the reference standard was the Kraft classification itself - not future diabetes, cardiovascular disease, clamp-measured insulin resistance, or treatment response. The reported sensitivity and specificity therefore measure agreement with Kraft's labels, not diagnostic validity against an independent clinical endpoint. The authors explicitly acknowledged that the database lacked long-term outcomes and that prospective studies were needed. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))

By comparison, several standard OGTT-derived insulin-sensitivity indices have been tested against the hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp. The original Matsuda index study included 153 participants across a range of glucose tolerance and found a correlation of 0.73 with clamp-measured whole-body glucose disposal. ([Matsuda and DeFronzo, 1999](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/insulin-sensitivity-indices-obtained-from-oral-glucose-tolerance-/))

A 2026 longitudinal study compared 18 surrogate indices in 2,260 Indigenous Americans from the southwestern United States followed for up to 14.5 years, during which 509 developed type 2 diabetes. Indices derived from fasting and two-hour OGTT glucose and insulin performed best overall. The Gutt and Cederholm indices each had an area under the curve of 0.728, while HOMA-IR and QUICKI each reached 0.701 among fasting-insulin methods. The outcome definition included two-hour OGTT glucose, which could favor OGTT-derived indices, although a sensitivity analysis excluding that criterion produced similar results. The study did not evaluate Kraft patterns, and the authors cautioned that the high-risk study population may limit generalizability. It shows that shorter standardized dynamic indices can modestly outperform fasting-insulin indices in that cohort; it does not establish that they capture "most" of the physiological or clinical value of the full Kraft curve. ([Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919))

The evidence therefore supports the following hierarchy:

- A dynamic OGTT can provide post-load information that fasting values alone do not contain. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Matsuda and DeFronzo, 1999](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/insulin-sensitivity-indices-obtained-from-oral-glucose-tolerance-/))

- In the cited 2026 cohort, the best OGTT-derived indices modestly outperformed fasting-only indices. ([Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919))

- The cited studies do not establish that the complete three- to five-hour Kraft classification adds clinically meaningful prediction beyond these shorter methods. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919))


## Insulin assay variability

Insulin assays are substantially less standardized than glucose or HbA1c measurements. A 2023 laboratory guideline described glucose enzymatic methods as relatively well standardized, HbA1c as standardized through the NGSP system, and serum insulin as lacking a standardized measurement method. In a 2007 comparison of 11 commercial insulin assays using 150 serum samples, insulin results differed by as much as a factor of two between methods despite strong rank correlations. The investigators found assay-specific offsets and concentration-dependent differences attributable to calibration, specificity, and conversion procedures. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244), [Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517))

The problem has not disappeared. A 2025 comparison tested 40 serum samples using 12 immunoassays from nine manufacturers. Repeatability within individual assays was excellent, but substantial differences remained between platforms, and only one assay fully agreed with the isotope-dilution mass-spectrometry reference method. ([Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/))

This has two consequences for Kraft testing. First, an absolute threshold developed with a historical radioimmunoassay may not transfer directly to a current laboratory platform. Second, a person close to a cutoff could receive a different category depending on the assay used. The cited assay studies evaluated concentration agreement, not whether Kraft peak-timing classification remains stable across platforms. The robustness of Kraft peak classification across assay platforms was not established in the cited studies. ([Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517), [Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

## Biological repeatability

A direct repeatability study of Kraft response patterns was very small. Eight participants completed at least two weekly 100 g OGTTs, and six completed all four planned tests. Missing pattern results were imputed for the kappa calculation. The estimated Fleiss kappa for Kraft categories was 0.290, and no participant exhibited Kraft Pattern IV or V. The article's printed confidence interval does not include the reported point estimate, so its inferential precision cannot be relied upon without correction. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162))

A sample of eight cannot establish clinically acceptable reproducibility across age groups, ethnicities, metabolic states, laboratories, or assay platforms. The study nevertheless recorded within-person pattern changes despite repeated standardized testing, so category stability cannot be assumed from protocol length alone. Multiple measurements may provide a richer curve, but the observed variation also shows that additional sampling does not eliminate day-to-day category changes. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162))

## Modern studies cited here used adapted protocols

A 2022 study explored Kraft-like phenotypes using a mixed macronutrient tolerance test containing palm oil, sucrose, and egg-white protein rather than the original 100 g glucose drink. Blood was collected fasting and at 0.5, three, and six hours. An insulin-sensitivity index derived from this meal test correlated at 0.77 with a standard OGTT Matsuda index. ([Newman et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.877696/full))

However, the investigators had to redefine the pattern rules for their meal composition and sampling schedule. For example, the low-insulin Pattern V threshold was reduced from 30 to 15 μU/mL. The study therefore demonstrates that postprandial metabolic phenotyping is feasible; it is not an independent replication of Kraft's original thresholds or a prospective validation of his diagnostic categories. ([Newman et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.877696/full))

A 2024 retrospective hospital study applied a Kraft-style glucose tolerance insulin response classification to 397 referred adults and classified 69.3 percent as hyperinsulinemic. Follow-up was incomplete, and the reported outcome regression evaluated HOMA-IR rather than whether the Kraft category independently predicted diabetes. The study adds adapted observational data, but it does not prospectively validate Kraft's original patterns. ([Kardus et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.4103/DSHMJ.DSHMJ_64_24))

The 2022 study received support from the National Dairy Council and Campbell Soup Company, in addition to USDA and NIH funding. The authors stated that the funders had no involvement in the study design, data collection, analysis, interpretation, writing, or publication decision. The commercial support remains relevant context when weighing the evidence, particularly because the study did not provide prospective outcome validation. ([Newman et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.877696/full))

## Can continuous glucose monitoring replace it?

Continuous glucose monitoring and insulin-augmented OGTTs measure different things. CGM records interstitial glucose; it does not measure insulin. It can characterize post-meal glucose peaks, variability, and episodes of hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia, but it cannot directly show how much insulin accompanied a given glucose curve. CGM data alone therefore cannot determine whether a high or low insulin response accompanied a given glucose trace. ([Liao et al., 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0))

A 2026 systematic review included 23 studies and 1,074 nondiabetic participants. Only five studies involving 149 participants contributed to quantitative meta-analysis, and the estimated reduction in mean glucose came from two small studies. BMI was not significantly reduced. Benefits were more evident in people with prediabetes than in healthy normoglycemic participants, but the evidence was limited and heterogeneous, with most randomized trials carrying at least some risk-of-bias concerns. The authors characterized CGM primarily as a biofeedback tool to accompany structured lifestyle interventions. The review evaluated CGM-based interventions, not diagnostic equivalence with an insulin-augmented OGTT. ([Liao et al., 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0))

CGM may therefore complement a glucose or insulin challenge, especially when the clinical question concerns everyday glycemic excursions. It cannot be considered a substitute for an insulin curve on the basis of the cited review, because that review did not test diagnostic equivalence and CGM does not measure insulin. ([Liao et al., 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0))

## Burden, cost, and clinical utility

A full Kraft protocol requires at least five blood draws over three hours, with some historical versions extending to four or five hours. It requires prolonged patient attendance, repeated phlebotomy, multiple insulin assays, and standardized pre-test preparation. Even the researchers working with Kraft's database described the complete protocol as resource-intensive and attempted to reduce it to fasting and two-hour measurements. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180))

The more important limitation is not inconvenience alone. For clinical utility, the important question is whether a test result changes a decision and improves an outcome beyond ordinary risk assessment. None of the Kraft-related studies cited above was a prospective trial of Kraft-guided treatment, a validation of pattern-specific treatment thresholds, or a Kraft-specific cost-effectiveness analysis. ([Crofts et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27344544/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162), [Newman et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.877696/full), [Kardus et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.4103/DSHMJ.DSHMJ_64_24), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

The 2023 evidence-based laboratory guideline from the American Diabetes Association and Association for Diagnostics and Laboratory Medicine states that routine insulin or proinsulin testing is not recommended for most people with diabetes or at risk of diabetes or cardiovascular disease, and that these measurements are useful primarily in research. The same guideline calls for better standardization of insulin assays and states that insulin measurement should not be used in an OGTT to diagnose diabetes. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

Non-adoption by a guideline does not by itself prove that a test is invalid. In this case, however, the recommendation aligns with limitations visible in the cited Kraft evidence: uncertain assay portability, weak repeatability data, no prospectively validated Kraft diagnostic cutoffs, no demonstrated incremental outcome prediction, and no evidence from the cited studies that assigning a Kraft category changes management beneficially. ([Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517), [Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

## The risk of overdiagnosis

The problem with using the Kraft test for healthy-person "optimization" is not that every dynamic insulin finding is meaningless. It is that a physiologically interesting deviation can be converted into a disease label even though the cited Kraft studies do not establish a validated boundary between normal variation and clinically actionable risk. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

A person near a threshold might move between categories because of assay choice or day-to-day biological variation. Because the cited Kraft studies did not report absolute prospective risks for mild Pattern II or III results, those results cannot be translated from these studies into an individual's absolute future diabetes risk beyond what is already indicated by age, adiposity, family history, lipids, blood pressure, and accepted glucose measurements. ([Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517), [Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

The resulting danger is that a test changes the patient's label without demonstrating a corresponding improvement in evidence-based care. General recommendations such as physical activity, weight management where appropriate, adequate sleep, and dietary improvement may be sensible, but their general value does not validate the diagnostic category that prompted them. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

## Assessment of the competing claims

### 1. "The Kraft test detects insulin dysregulation years to decades before glucose-based tests"

**Partly supported, but overstated.** Dynamic insulin testing can reveal compensation and delayed responses that fasting markers do not directly represent. Late OGTT insulin peaks prospectively predict type 2 diabetes, and metabolic deterioration begins before diagnostic hyperglycemia. However, the cited prospective studies did not evaluate the exact Kraft patterns or demonstrate a "decades earlier" detection interval. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Tabák et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19515410/))

### 2. "Kraft's patterns were observational and lack prospective outcome validation in the cited evidence"

**Substantially correct.** The Kraft database was cross-sectional and retrospective, and its modern investigators acknowledged the absence of long-term outcomes. A 2024 adapted retrospective study did not remedy this gap. Prospective evidence exists for other insulin-curve classifications, especially late two-hour peaks, but that evidence cannot be transferred automatically to Kraft's categories and thresholds. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Kardus et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.4103/DSHMJ.DSHMJ_64_24), [Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/))

### 3. "The information is real, but the five-hour protocol is impractical and simpler tests capture most of it"

**Mostly correct, but "most" has not been demonstrated.** Shorter OGTT-derived indices have clamp validation and prospective outcome evidence and capture useful dynamic information with fasting and two-hour samples. In the cited 2026 cohort, the best OGTT-derived indices modestly outperformed fasting-insulin indices, but the cited studies include no head-to-head outcome comparison showing that they capture most of the clinical value of the full Kraft curve. HOMA-IR is less informative than these dynamic indices in that cohort but remains reasonably predictive. The cited CGM review does not establish that CGM can substitute for insulin measurement, because it evaluated CGM interventions rather than diagnostic equivalence and CGM records glucose rather than insulin. ([Matsuda and DeFronzo, 1999](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/insulin-sensitivity-indices-obtained-from-oral-glucose-tolerance-/), [Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Liao et al., 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-026-03920-0))

### 4. "The functional-medicine and biohacker revival has outrun the evidence"

**The evidence-to-claim mismatch is a justified concern.** The cited literature does not support presenting a mild Kraft-pattern deviation as a validated disease stage or using it as a standalone basis for treatment. The strongest criticism is not who offers the test; it is that fixed diagnostic labels and clinical promises are being attached to measurements with unresolved assay standardization, repeatability, action-threshold, and outcome-validation problems. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162), [Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517), [Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

## Evidence needed

A decisive validation program would require:

- A preregistered, prospective, multiethnic cohort using a standardized glucose load, preparation protocol, sampling schedule, and harmonized insulin assay.

- Kraft categories specified before outcome collection rather than reconstructed or modified afterward.

- Comparison against fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, [Matsuda](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/insulin-sensitivity-indices-obtained-from-oral-glucose-tolerance-/) or [Gutt](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919) indices, fasting glucose, one- and two-hour glucose, HbA1c, and standard clinical risk factors.

- Incident type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular events, kidney disease, mortality, and patient-relevant harms as outcomes - not agreement with another surrogate classification.

- Repeat testing in a substantial subset to quantify within-person category stability and the effect of assay platform.

- Decision-impact trials showing whether Kraft-guided intervention improves outcomes compared with the same intervention offered through ordinary risk-based screening.

- Formal cost-effectiveness and overdiagnosis analyses.


## Quick reference

- **Dynamic insulin responses contain real information:** supported. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Matsuda and DeFronzo, 1999](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/insulin-sensitivity-indices-obtained-from-oral-glucose-tolerance-/))

- **Late post-load insulin peaks predict future diabetes:** supported prospectively, using non-Kraft definitions. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/))

- **Kraft's exact Patterns I-V are validated diagnostic stages:** not established by the cited evidence. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

- **Pattern V necessarily represents beta-cell exhaustion:** not established by a peripheral insulin curve alone. ([Polidori et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26993071/), [Newman et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.877696/full))

- **Kraft testing detects disease decades before glucose tests:** not demonstrated by the cited prospective evidence. ([Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/), [Tabák et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19515410/))

- **It outperforms HOMA-IR or shorter OGTT indices for clinical outcomes:** not demonstrated by the cited studies. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919))

- **Insulin assay variability materially complicates fixed cutoffs:** supported. ([Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517), [Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

- **Routine population screening or healthy-person optimization:** not supported by the cited outcome evidence or current laboratory guidance. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))


## Practical interpretation

The Kraft test should not be dismissed as physiologically meaningless. It anticipated an important modern idea: normal glucose tolerance can coexist with a high or delayed insulin response, and the shape of a metabolic response can carry information that a fasting snapshot does not contain. ([Crofts et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27344544/), [Hayashi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275353/))

But **early-warning physiology is not the same as a validated diagnostic system**. The named Kraft patterns currently function best as descriptive phenotypes or research hypotheses. They should not be presented to a healthy person as a confirmed stage of diabetes, a validated cardiovascular diagnosis, or proof that pancreatic exhaustion is occurring. ([Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/49/180), [Polidori et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26993071/), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

For routine risk assessment, accepted glucose-based testing and conventional cardiometabolic risk factors remain the clinical foundation. For research or specialist metabolic phenotyping, standardized OGTT-derived indices have more direct clamp-validation and longitudinal evidence than assigning a three- to five-hour Kraft category. This does not make routine insulin-augmented OGTT screening guideline-recommended. Any insulin result should be interpreted using the laboratory's assay and the full clinical context. Given the observed assay differences and limited repeatability data, a borderline or consequential result should not be treated as a rigid disease-stage label on the basis of a single test. ([Matsuda and DeFronzo, 1999](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/insulin-sensitivity-indices-obtained-from-oral-glucose-tolerance-/), [Vazquez et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/8/2251/8512919), [Manley et al., 2007](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article-abstract/53/5/922/5627517), [Rohlfing et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40802520/), [Crofts et al., 2019](https://journalofmetabolichealth.org/index.php/jmh/article/view/44/162), [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))
