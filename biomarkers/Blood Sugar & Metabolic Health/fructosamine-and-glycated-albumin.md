# Fructosamine and Glycated Albumin: When HbA1c May Be Misleading

Fructosamine and glycated albumin address one specific limitation of HbA1c: they measure glucose attached to circulating serum proteins rather than hemoglobin. They can therefore bypass distortions caused by abnormal red blood cell turnover and some hemoglobin-related analytical problems. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

They do not, however, provide a confounder-free measure of glucose. Both depend on serum-protein metabolism, and their clinical interpretation is less standardized than HbA1c. Current evidence and guidance support using them selectively when HbA1c is plausibly misleading or when a shorter-term measure is needed, rather than replacing HbA1c routinely. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

## What fructosamine and glycated albumin measure

Fructosamine measures stable ketoamine products formed when glucose binds to circulating serum proteins. Albumin accounts for most of the signal, but other glycated proteins contribute as well. Glycated albumin is narrower: it measures the glycated fraction of albumin and is usually reported as a percentage of total albumin. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/))

Because circulating proteins turn over faster than red blood cells, fructosamine and glycated albumin place substantially more weight on glucose exposure during approximately the preceding two to four weeks. HbA1c instead reflects a weighted average over approximately two to three months, with recent glucose contributing more than older glucose. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

That shorter window makes the protein markers potentially useful after a treatment change. By the same kinetic logic, a single value can be more influenced by short-lived illness, temporary hyperglycemia, or other brief changes that would be diluted in HbA1c. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

Neither fructosamine nor glycated albumin shows daily glucose variability, time below range, or individual hypoglycemic episodes. Continuous glucose monitoring or structured capillary glucose measurements are required when those patterns matter. ([Meek et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8051277/); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

## Why HbA1c can become misleading

HbA1c depends on both glucose exposure and the average age of circulating red blood cells. Conditions that shorten red cell survival generally reduce the time available for hemoglobin glycation and can produce an HbA1c lower than the true glucose exposure. Conditions that increase mean red cell age may move HbA1c in the opposite direction. ([English et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25994072/); [Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes Diabetes Work Group, 2022](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/KDIGO-2022-Clinical-Practice-Guideline-for-Diabetes-Management-in-CKD.pdf))

A systematic review found that iron deficiency, with or without overt anemia, generally produced a spuriously higher HbA1c without a corresponding increase in glucose. Limited and heterogeneous evidence suggested that some non-iron-deficiency anemias may lower HbA1c, particularly when erythrocyte survival is shortened. Therefore, "anemia makes HbA1c unreliable" is directionally incomplete: the type and mechanism of anemia determine the likely distortion. ([English et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25994072/))

Hemolysis, recent blood loss, transfusion, erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy, dialysis-related changes in erythrocyte survival, and some hemoglobin variants can also create discordance between HbA1c and actual glucose. Hemoglobin variants may interfere biologically, analytically, or both, depending on whether normal hemoglobin A is present and which assay is used. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

Fructosamine and glycated albumin remove the red-cell component of this problem. That does not make them free of demographic or population-associated differences. Body mass index, albumin turnover, kidney function, protein loss, and other characteristics can shift their relationship with mean glucose. Avoiding hemoglobin-related bias is not equivalent to eliminating every source of biological bias. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [Hiramatsu et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22166921/); [Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

## Do the alternatives outperform HbA1c when HbA1c is suspect?

A 2025 study directly compared HbA1c, glycated albumin, and fructosamine with continuous glucose monitoring in 192 African adults with type 2 diabetes. Correlations with mean CGM glucose were 0.88 for HbA1c and 0.84 for both glycated albumin and fructosamine. Their ability to classify mean CGM glucose above 8 mmol/L was also similar, including in the subgroup with conditions reported to affect HbA1c. ([Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011))

Sickle cell trait did not materially alter the relationship between mean glucose and any of the three markers in that cohort. Anemia was associated with underestimation by HbA1c and fructosamine, but not glycated albumin. This illustrates why switching automatically from HbA1c to fructosamine may simply exchange one bias for another; glycated albumin may be the more appropriate alternative in selected, but not all, anemic patients. ([Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011))

The study received public research support, while Asahi Kasei Pharma supplied the glycated albumin assays without charge. That material support should be considered when weighing the findings, but it does not by itself determine their validity. ([Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011))

Correlation alone does not establish agreement, absence of systematic bias, accurate calibration, or clinical interchangeability. HbA1c therefore should not be declared invalid solely because a potentially interfering diagnosis is present. The suspected mechanism, magnitude of discordance, albumin status, and direct glucose measurements should determine whether an alternative marker adds useful information. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

## Rapid changes in glucose control

The shorter biological window is a genuine advantage when glucose control is changing quickly. A marker that responds over approximately two to four weeks can indicate direction of travel before HbA1c has fully incorporated the new glucose exposure. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

In an exploratory post hoc CGM analysis of a small comparative study, glycated albumin correlated with CGM time in range earlier and generally more strongly than HbA1c or fructosamine during the first several weeks of follow-up. The analysis included 34 participants, of whom 22 experienced a treatment change. The findings support glycated albumin as a possible intermediate marker when CGM is unavailable and it is too early to expect a full HbA1c response. ([Desouza et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10583977/))

This analysis had substantial commercial involvement. It was supported and conducted by Asahi Kasei Pharma, the manufacturer of the glycated albumin assay; one author was a company employee, and other authors reported company grants or honoraria. The findings are hypothesis-generating and should not be treated as definitive evidence that glycated albumin improves clinical outcomes or should routinely replace CGM or HbA1c. ([Desouza et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/108/11/e1193/7187539))

By inference from their shorter biological window, these markers may also be considered after interventions expected to change glucose rapidly, including insulin initiation, GLP-1 receptor agonist treatment, and bariatric surgery. However, this is an extrapolation from biomarker kinetics: the cited studies did not specifically establish improved treatment decisions or patient outcomes in those settings. The claim that the markers are inherently "superior" after every rapidly acting intervention therefore goes beyond this evidence. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [Desouza et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10583977/))

## Pregnancy

Pregnancy is often presented as an ideal use case because glucose physiology, plasma volume, iron status, and red cell turnover can change over a relatively short period. However, diagnosis of gestational diabetes and monitoring of established diabetes are separate questions, and the evidence does not support replacing established glucose-based methods with fructosamine or glycated albumin. ([Hiramatsu et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22166921/); [Meek et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8051277/); [Kodama et al., 2025](https://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13643-025-02894-0))

A 2025 meta-analysis included 20 diagnostic studies comparing glycated albumin or fructosamine with HbA1c for gestational diabetes. Relative sensitivity was 1.00 and relative specificity was 0.99, indicating no significant overall diagnostic advantage for the alternative markers. The pooled positive likelihood ratio was somewhat higher for glycated albumin or fructosamine than for HbA1c, at 5.11 versus 3.75. The negative likelihood ratios did not support using glycated albumin, fructosamine, or HbA1c alone for screening, although glycated albumin or fructosamine was somewhat more useful than HbA1c as a rule-in test for diagnosis. Substantial heterogeneity and possible publication bias limit certainty. ([Kodama et al., 2025](https://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13643-025-02894-0))

For monitoring pre-existing type 1 diabetes, a prespecified secondary analysis of CONCEPTT evaluated 157 pregnancies using HbA1c, CGM metrics, glycated albumin, fructosamine, and other biomarkers. CGM time in range and time above range were the most consistently informative measures. HbA1c also remained consistently associated with pregnancy outcomes, while glycated albumin and fructosamine generally had lower predictive ability and did not substantially improve prediction when added to HbA1c or CGM. ([Meek et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8051277/))

Pregnancy-specific reference values require context. In 574 healthy Japanese pregnant women, the reported glycated albumin reference interval was 11.5% to 15.7%. Glycated albumin decreased as pregnancy advanced and was lower in women with obesity or proteinuria. Those findings establish a pregnancy-specific interval for that population and method. They do not establish a universal pregnancy threshold or transferability across populations or assays. ([Hiramatsu et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22166921/); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [Zheng et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/clinchem/hvag055/8702417))

The evidence therefore does not support the claim that fructosamine or glycated albumin captures pregnancy-related glucose changes that HbA1c "misses entirely." They may provide additional short-term information in selected patients, but they should not replace established glucose-based diagnostic pathways, for which the oral glucose tolerance test remains the reference standard. CGM or structured capillary glucose measurements provide the actionable information needed for day-to-day treatment. ([Kodama et al., 2025](https://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13643-025-02894-0); [Meek et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8051277/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

## Chronic kidney disease and dialysis

The case for considering glycated albumin is stronger in advanced chronic kidney disease, where anemia, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, transfusions, and altered red cell survival can all influence HbA1c. ([Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes Diabetes Work Group, 2022](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/KDIGO-2022-Clinical-Practice-Guideline-for-Diabetes-Management-in-CKD.pdf); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

A meta-analysis of 24 studies involving 3,928 participants compared correlations between average glucose and either glycated albumin or HbA1c. In stages 1-3 CKD, the pooled correlations were 0.61 for glycated albumin and 0.71 for HbA1c, with no statistically significant difference. In stages 4-5 CKD, including dialysis, the corresponding correlations were 0.57 and 0.49, representing a significantly stronger pooled correlation for glycated albumin. This does not by itself establish superior calibration, agreement, or clinical utility. ([Gan et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29854962/))

More recent CGM evidence complicates the idea that glycated albumin is universally superior. A 2026 prospective study enrolled 251 people receiving maintenance dialysis and compared the three biomarkers with approximately 10 days of CGM. Overall correlations with mean CGM glucose were 0.85 for HbA1c, 0.87 for glycated albumin, and 0.70 for fructosamine. Among participants with diabetes, HbA1c and glycated albumin both correlated at 0.84, while fructosamine correlated at 0.64. Because the comparator CGM period covered only about 10 days, while the biomarkers integrate different and generally longer periods, these correlations should not be interpreted as pure tests of agreement or interchangeability. ([Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

The sources of bias differed. HbA1c was influenced by erythropoiesis-stimulating agent dose, hemoglobin, serum albumin, and body mass index. Glycated albumin and fructosamine were influenced by dialysis modality, dialysis duration, residual kidney function, and body mass index. All three biomarkers showed clinically relevant bias in at least some subgroups. ([Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

The study was funded by the US National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases. Dexcom provided CGM equipment and supplies, and Asahi Kasei Pharma provided Lucica glycated albumin reagents without charge. Investigators also disclosed relevant consulting, honoraria, research support, or supply relationships with diabetes drug and device companies. ([Zelnick et al., 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/5/835/164560/Accuracy-Variability-and-Bias-of-Glycemic))

Outcome evidence also exists. A meta-analysis of 12 studies involving 25,932 dialysis patients with diabetes mellitus found that higher glycated albumin was associated with greater all-cause mortality. Associations with cardiovascular mortality and cardiovascular events did not reach statistical significance. This supports prognostic relevance, but it does not establish a glycated albumin target at which changing therapy improves survival. ([Copur et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32862262/))

Glycated albumin is therefore a reasonable alternative or adjunct in advanced CKD and dialysis, especially when HbA1c is discordant with observed glucose. ADA guidance permits alternative biomarkers when HbA1c interpretation is problematic. The published KDIGO 2022 guideline recommends HbA1c as the default biomarker in CKD, while recognizing reduced reliability in advanced CKD, especially dialysis, and supporting CGM-derived assessment when HbA1c is discordant with direct glucose measurements. Current evidence does not justify calling glycated albumin a universal winner, and fructosamine appeared less strongly correlated with CGM than either HbA1c or glycated albumin in the newest dialysis study. ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic); [KDIGO Diabetes Work Group, 2022](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/KDIGO-2022-Clinical-Practice-Guideline-for-Diabetes-Management-in-CKD.pdf); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

## Evidence linking the markers to complications

It is no longer accurate to say that fructosamine and glycated albumin have no long-term outcome evidence. ([Nathan et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990364/); [Selvin et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24703046/); [Selvin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26022911/); [Copur et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32862262/))

In an analysis of stored samples from the DCCT/EDIC cohort, glycated albumin and HbA1c showed broadly similar associations with retinopathy and nephropathy. Their combination strengthened some microvascular associations. HbA1c, but not glycated albumin, retained an independent association with cardiovascular disease in the relevant models. ([Nathan et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990364/))

In the prospective ARIC cohort, fructosamine and glycated albumin were strongly associated with retinopathy and with future chronic kidney disease and diabetes. Their discrimination for incident diabetes was weaker than HbA1c, but the microvascular associations demonstrated that the markers carry clinically relevant prognostic information. ([Selvin et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24703046/))

A separate ARIC analysis found that higher fructosamine and glycated albumin were associated with coronary heart disease, ischemic stroke, heart failure, and mortality over long-term follow-up. The associations were generally similar in direction to those observed with HbA1c. ([Selvin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26022911/))

These studies provide substantial observational prognostic evidence, not treatment thresholds or intervention trials. Current ADA guidance likewise notes that fructosamine and glycated albumin have been linked to long-term complications, but that there have been few clinical trials and the evidence base for monitoring remains weaker than for HbA1c. ([Nathan et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990364/); [Selvin et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24703046/); [Selvin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26022911/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

By contrast, intensive glycemic strategies reduced microvascular complications in the randomized DCCT and UKPDS programs, creating the clinical-outcome framework within which HbA1c is interpreted. The observational marker studies cited above do not provide an equivalent treatment-target framework for glycated albumin or fructosamine. ([Diabetes Control and Complications Trial Research Group, 1993](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199309303291401); [UK Prospective Diabetes Study Group, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9742976/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

"Insufficient outcome data" is therefore only partly correct. Prognostic data are now substantial. What remains insufficiently established is how a particular glycated albumin or fructosamine value should guide a specific treatment change and whether marker-guided decisions improve outcomes. ([Nathan et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990364/); [Selvin et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24703046/); [Selvin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26022911/); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

## Standardization and reference ranges

HbA1c benefits from extensive international standardization and established reporting systems. Fructosamine and glycated albumin do not yet have an equivalent level of comparability. A 2023 laboratory-medicine guideline concluded that neither assay was fully standardized, although reference-method development for glycated albumin was underway. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))

Progress has been made, but a 2026 analytical study showed that important questions remain. Investigators compared three glycated albumin methods in 129 donor specimens and certified reference material. Although the methods correlated strongly, the Lys-525-targeted site-specific isotope-dilution LC-MS/MS method developed increasing negative bias at higher glycated albumin concentrations. The authors concluded that standardization still requires a universally accepted definition of exactly which glycated albumin species constitute the measurand. ([Zheng et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/clinchem/hvag055/8702417))

Given these unresolved comparability issues, a numerical glycated albumin result should be interpreted using the method-specific reference interval and assay information supplied by the testing laboratory. Serial values are most defensibly compared when measured with the same assay in the same laboratory. Conversion equations that present a glycated albumin or fructosamine result as an exact HbA1c equivalent may create a false sense of precision. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [Zheng et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/clinchem/hvag055/8702417))

## Albumin and protein-related interference

Fructosamine is influenced by both the proportion of serum proteins that are glycated and the concentration and composition of those proteins. Hypoalbuminemia, altered immunoglobulin concentrations, protein-losing states, and rapid changes in protein turnover may therefore shift fructosamine independently of glucose. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/))

Reporting glycated albumin as a percentage of total albumin reduces sensitivity to albumin concentration alone. It does not eliminate problems caused by altered albumin production, distribution, loss, or degradation. Proteinuria, nephrotic-range protein loss, liver disease, thyroid disorders, inflammation-associated changes in protein metabolism, and dialysis-related factors may all complicate interpretation. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

Body mass index is another recurring confounder. Glycated albumin tends to be lower at a given glucose exposure in people with obesity, an effect observed in pregnancy and dialysis studies. A low glycated albumin result in a person with obesity should not automatically be interpreted as proof of better glucose control. ([Hiramatsu et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22166921/); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

The phrase "not affected by red blood cell turnover" is accurate. The broader claim "not affected by confounders" is not. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

## Quick reference

- **HbA1c:** Reflects approximately two to three months of weighted glucose exposure. It has the strongest standardization, treatment-trial framework, and established clinical targets, but can be misleading when red cell lifespan or hemoglobin measurement is abnormal. ([English et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25994072/); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

- **Fructosamine:** Reflects approximately two to four weeks and measures glycated serum proteins collectively. It avoids hemoglobin-related interference but is comparatively sensitive to serum-protein concentration, composition, and turnover. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

- **Glycated albumin:** Reflects approximately two to four weeks and is more albumin-specific. It may be more informative than fructosamine in selected patients with anemia or advanced CKD, but it remains affected by albumin turnover, protein loss, body mass index, dialysis-related factors, and incomplete assay standardization. ([Gan et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29854962/); [Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))


## How the competing claims hold up

- **"They should be the default whenever HbA1c is unreliable."** Too broad. They should be considered when a specific HbA1c-distorting mechanism is present, but the alternative marker must be chosen around its own confounders. Recent CGM comparisons do not show universal superiority. ([Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

- **"Their shorter window makes them superior for rapid treatment changes."** Partly supported. They respond faster, and a small post hoc study found earlier or stronger associations with CGM improvement. The cited study did not specifically evaluate GLP-1 initiation or bariatric surgery and did not show improved clinical outcomes. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [Desouza et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10583977/))

- **"Lack of outcome evidence is the main barrier."** The important problem is no longer absence of outcome correlation. The missing evidence is intervention-based: validated targets, decision thresholds, and a stronger clinical-trial framework showing that treatment guided by these markers improves outcomes. ([Nathan et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990364/); [Selvin et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24703046/); [Selvin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26022911/); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

- **"Reserve them for specific HbA1c limitations rather than routine use."** This is the position most consistent with the current evidence. The markers are valuable alternatives or adjuncts, but routine replacement would introduce additional biological and analytical uncertainty for most patients. ([American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244))


## Practical interpretation

- HbA1c should remain the default long-term marker for most people when its result is consistent with glucose measurements and no important red-cell or hemoglobin-related distortion is present. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

- When HbA1c appears implausible, compare it with CGM, structured capillary glucose, or laboratory glucose before assuming that the discrepancy is caused by HbA1c. Then choose an alternative marker according to the likely mechanism. ([Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic); [Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes Diabetes Work Group, 2022](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/KDIGO-2022-Clinical-Practice-Guideline-for-Diabetes-Management-in-CKD.pdf))

- Glycated albumin may be preferable to fructosamine in selected patients when red-cell abnormalities are the primary concern and albumin production, loss, and turnover appear reasonably stable. Fructosamine may still be useful when it is the available assay, but total protein and albumin status require attention. ([Danese et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25591856/); [Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/))

- On the basis of biomarker kinetics, repeating the same glycated albumin or fructosamine assay after approximately two to four weeks may provide an earlier trend estimate. This interval follows the markers' biological window rather than an outcome-validated testing schedule. Marker-specific treatment thresholds remain insufficiently established, and the result should not be converted mechanically into a universal HbA1c-equivalent target. ([Tahara and Shima, 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7497851/); [Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))

- In pregnancy, neither marker should replace established glucose-based testing for diagnosis or direct glucose measurements for treatment. In dialysis, glycated albumin can be a useful alternative or complementary marker, but current CGM evidence supports considering both HbA1c and glycated albumin rather than assuming either one is unbiased. ([Kodama et al., 2025](https://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13643-025-02894-0); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/); [KDIGO Diabetes Work Group, 2022](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/KDIGO-2022-Clinical-Practice-Guideline-for-Diabetes-Management-in-CKD.pdf))


The best-supported conclusion is selective use, not broad replacement: fructosamine and glycated albumin are most valuable when the reason HbA1c may be wrong is understood and the alternative marker's own limitations are explicitly considered. ([Sacks et al., 2023](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/69/8/808/7226244); [Niwaha et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dme.70011); [Zelnick et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13094876/); [American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes, 2026](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S132/163927/6-Glycemic-Goals-Hypoglycemia-and-Hyperglycemic))
