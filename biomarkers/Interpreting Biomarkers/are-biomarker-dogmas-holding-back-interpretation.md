# Are Biomarker Dogmas Holding Back Interpretation?

Biomarker thresholds are useful conventions, but they are often mistaken for biological laws. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

A value can mark a population reference interval, a risk category, a diagnostic criterion, a treatment threshold, or a nutrient-adequacy target. Those are not interchangeable. Total cholesterol of 200 mg/dL was historically an action boundary for screening and dietary counseling. TSH around 4.5 mIU/L is largely a population-derived reference boundary. Fasting glucose of 100 mg/dL defines an intermediate-risk category, not diabetes. Vitamin D targets attempted to translate uncertain outcome evidence into a statement about “sufficiency.” [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Cholesterol program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [TSH reference range](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/) [Impaired fasting glucose](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14578255/) [Vitamin D dietary reference report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118827/) [Vitamin D clinical guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646368/)

Across these examples, the evidence most strongly supports the claim that hard cutoffs create false precision. Observed risk is often continuous, while treatment benefit is a separate causal question that depends on the intervention, baseline absolute risk, competing risks, treatment burden, adverse effects, and costs. A threshold is selected because a decision has to be made somewhere. Thresholds therefore remain necessary, but they should be presented as decision rules with uncertainty, not as cliffs separating healthy from unhealthy. [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/) [Continuous cholesterol risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/) [LDL-lowering trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21067804/) [TSH outcome data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/) [Fasting-glucose outcome data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

The claim that many thresholds were based on limited or indirect evidence is also partly correct. The stronger assertion that industry influence established these particular numbers is not established by the historical and clinical sources cited here. [Cholesterol program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Vitamin D guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/) [Diabetes threshold report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14578255/)

## A threshold can serve several different purposes

A **reference interval** describes where most values fall in a selected population. It does not necessarily identify the point where risk begins or treatment becomes beneficial. [CLSI reference-interval standard](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/ep28/) [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/)

A **risk threshold** divides a continuous probability distribution into categories so that screening, counseling, or prevention can be organized. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

A **diagnostic threshold** uses a selected value to classify whether a defined condition is present, with corresponding tradeoffs in sensitivity and specificity. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Asturias Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

A **treatment threshold** is the point at which expected treatment benefits are judged to exceed burdens, adverse effects, and costs. [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

A **nutrient-adequacy target** estimates the concentration or intake likely to meet the needs of most healthy people. It may not predict whether giving more of the nutrient improves clinical outcomes. [Vitamin D dietary reference report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118827/) [Vitamin D guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)

Much of the apparent disagreement about biomarkers comes from applying one kind of threshold as though it were another. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/)

## Total cholesterol: 200 mg/dL was an action boundary, not a biological wall

### How the number entered practice

The National Cholesterol Education Program operationalized 200 mg/dL as a major clinical boundary in the late 1980s. A 1988 description of the program recommended cholesterol measurement for all adults at least every five years. A result above 200 mg/dL, confirmed by a second measurement, was to trigger a fat-controlled diet; values above 240 mg/dL could trigger more intensive dietary treatment and, depending on other risk factors and LDL cholesterol, medication. (Hulley, 1988). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/)

Physician-reported treatment thresholds changed substantially during the program's early years. In repeated national surveys involving approximately 1,600 physicians at each time point, the median range at which physicians initiated dietary treatment fell from 260-279 mg/dL in 1983 to 200-219 mg/dL in 1990. The reported drug-treatment range fell from 340-359 to 240-259 mg/dL. By 1990, more than 90 percent of surveyed physicians reported awareness and use of the National Cholesterol Education Program recommendations. (Schucker et al., 1991). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2012446/)

These surveys document rapid contemporaneous changes in reported practice after the recommendations were introduced. They do not isolate the effect of the 200 mg/dL number from the broader educational program, and they do not establish the causal origins of broader public attitudes toward dietary fat. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2012446/)

### Outcomes do not show a cliff at 200

The Multiple Risk Factor Intervention Trial followed 356,222 middle-aged men and found that the relationship between serum cholesterol and coronary mortality was continuous, graded, and strong. Risk was progressively higher across cholesterol levels, including below the traditionally “high” categories; the investigators explicitly concluded that the relationship was not a threshold phenomenon. This observational cohort described the association in that population, not treatment benefit or an identical risk function in every demographic group. (Stamler et al., 1986). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/)

Later randomized evidence shows that the proportional reduction in major vascular events tracks the amount of LDL cholesterol reduction across trials. An individual-participant meta-analysis of 26 randomized statin trials found a rate ratio of 0.78 for major vascular events per 1 mmol/L, or approximately 39 mg/dL, reduction in LDL cholesterol, corresponding to a reduction of just over one-fifth. These trials did not test an outcome discontinuity at total cholesterol 200 mg/dL. (Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' Collaboration, 2010). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21067804/)

Current practice has therefore moved away from using total cholesterol of 200 mg/dL as a standalone treatment trigger. The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline replaced the 2018 cholesterol guideline and addresses LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, lipoprotein(a), absolute cardiovascular risk, and risk-dependent treatment goals. It reintroduced LDL cholesterol and non-HDL cholesterol treatment goals according to risk category and recommends selective apolipoprotein B measurement to improve risk assessment and guide therapy, rather than using a universal total-cholesterol boundary. [American Heart Association](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2026-guideline-on-the-management-of-dyslipidemia) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41824552/)

### What 200 still means

A total cholesterol value of 199 mg/dL is not biologically different from 201 mg/dL. The historical boundary was useful for organizing screening and counseling, but it was never evidence of a sudden change in arterial biology. [Historical program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Continuous risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/)

The modern question is closer to: What is the person's absolute cardiovascular risk, what are the LDL and non-HDL concentrations, are major risk-enhancing conditions present, and how much absolute benefit is likely from treatment? [2026 dyslipidemia guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41824552/)

## Vitamin D: the clearest example of a threshold being withdrawn

### The 20-versus-30 dispute was partly a dispute about the question

The Institute of Medicine's 2011 report focused primarily on dietary requirements and skeletal health in generally healthy populations. It concluded that a serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration of approximately 20 ng/mL would meet the needs of nearly all healthy people, about 97.5 percent of the population. (Ross et al., 2011). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118827/)

The Endocrine Society's 2011 clinical guideline approached vitamin D from the perspective of evaluating and treating deficiency. It defined deficiency as below 20 ng/mL, “insufficiency” as 21-29 ng/mL, and proposed at least 30 ng/mL as sufficiency. (Holick et al., 2011). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646368/) [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/96/7/1911/2833671)

These bodies were not answering precisely the same question. One was establishing dietary reference values for broadly healthy populations; the other was developing clinical guidance for people considered at risk of deficiency. The two documents therefore presented different benchmarks even though the bodies were addressing different questions. [Institute of Medicine report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118827/) [2011 Endocrine Society guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646368/) [2024 guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)

### The Endocrine Society no longer endorses 30 ng/mL as a universal sufficiency threshold

In 2024, the Endocrine Society no longer endorsed its previous definitions of vitamin D sufficiency at 30 ng/mL or higher and insufficiency between 20 and 30 ng/mL for generally healthy people without established indications for testing or treatment. Its guideline panel concluded that randomized trials did not permit identification of a 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration that reliably predicted meaningful benefit from supplementation; the certainty of evidence for such a threshold was rated very low. (McCartney et al., 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)

The 2024 guideline also suggested against routine 25-hydroxyvitamin D screening in generally healthy adults without another established indication for testing, because outcome-specific concentrations had not been established in trials. (Demay et al., 2024). [Endocrine Society](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/vitamin-d-for-prevention-of-disease) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828931/)

Thus, the statement that authoritative bodies still endorse competing universal cutoffs of 20 and 30 ng/mL needs updating. That conflict accurately describes much of the period from 2011 to 2024. It does not fully describe the present Endocrine Society position. [2011 guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646368/) [2024 guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)

### Outcome trials do not identify a benefit cliff at 30

In the VITAL randomized trial, 25,871 generally healthy middle-aged and older adults received vitamin D₃ at 2,000 IU per day or placebo. Supplementation did not significantly reduce invasive cancer or major cardiovascular events over a median of 5.3 years. The hazard ratios were 0.96 for invasive cancer and 0.97 for major cardiovascular events. The trial was primarily funded by the National Institutes of Health; manufacturers donated the study agents and matching placebos. (Manson et al., 2019). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415629/) [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6425757/)

In VITAL's fracture analysis, supplementation did not reduce total, nonvertebral, or hip fractures. Importantly for the threshold question, the treatment effect was not meaningfully modified by baseline serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration. Participants were not, however, selected for severe deficiency, osteoporosis, or low bone mass, so the results should not be extended indiscriminately to those clinical groups. (LeBoff et al., 2022). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35939577/)

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis incorporated 69 randomized trials with 153,902 participants. Vitamin D alone had essentially no effect on the risk of any fracture compared with placebo or no treatment: risk ratio 1.00, 95 percent confidence interval 0.95-1.06. Combined calcium plus vitamin D produced statistically significant relative reductions in any fracture, with a risk ratio of 0.91 and a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.84-0.99; hip fracture, 0.84 and 0.74-0.96; and nonvertebral fracture, 0.87 and 0.78-0.96. These estimates corresponded to absolute reductions of approximately 1.0, 0.3, and 1.6 percentage points. The authors judged the effects below their prespecified thresholds for clinical importance. Because validated minimal important differences were unavailable, those thresholds were selected by author consensus, and one high-risk trial exerted substantial influence on several estimates. Most participants were community-dwelling and not at high fracture or fall risk, so evidence for high-risk and institutionalized populations remained more limited. The review reported no industry financial support for the submitted work. (Massé et al., 2026). [BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/393/bmj-2025-088050) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42161415/)

These findings do not address all patients with severe deficiency, rickets, osteomalacia, hypocalcemia, malabsorption, or other established clinical indications. They show that a universal target of 30 ng/mL for generally healthy people was not supported by outcome-based randomized evidence. [2011 clinical guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646368/) [2024 prevention guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828931/) [VITAL fracture analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35939577/)

## TSH: a reference limit is not automatically a treatment threshold

### Why 2.5 and 4.5 can both appear plausible

The upper TSH limit is usually estimated from the distribution in a selected reference population. The result depends on age, assay, iodine exposure, exclusion of people with thyroid antibodies or ultrasound abnormalities, medication use, and how strictly “healthy” is defined. [TSH reference-range review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/) [Age-specific reference data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/)

Arguments for an upper limit near 2.5 mIU/L arose partly from highly selected reference populations and concern that people in the 2.5-4.5 range might include early autoimmune thyroid disease. Arguments for retaining a limit around 4-4.5 emphasize the population distribution, biological variation, and the risk of labeling many people who will not develop clinically important disease. (Biondi, 2013). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/)

Age is particularly important. In a US population analysis, the estimated 97.5th percentile for TSH was 3.56 mIU/L among people aged 20-29 but 7.49 mIU/L among those aged 80 or older. Seventy percent of older adults whose TSH exceeded 4.5 mIU/L were still within their age-specific reference range. (Surks and Hollowell, 2007). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/)

A universal 2.5 limit would therefore not merely detect a few additional cases. It would reclassify large numbers of otherwise healthy people, especially older adults. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/)

### Observed cardiovascular associations are strongest at higher TSH levels

An individual-participant meta-analysis of 11 prospective cohorts included 55,287 adults. Compared with TSH of 0.5-4.49 mIU/L, the observed hazard ratio for coronary heart disease events was: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/)

- 1.00 for TSH 4.5-6.9 mIU/L, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.86-1.18
- 1.17 for TSH 7.0-9.9 mIU/L, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.96-1.43
- 1.89 for TSH 10-19.9 mIU/L, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 1.28-2.80. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/)

The increase was clearest at TSH concentrations of 10 mIU/L or higher. Total mortality was not increased across the subclinical-hypothyroidism group as a whole. (Rodondi et al., 2010). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/)

This observational result does not prove that 10 is a treatment boundary or that starting treatment at that value prevents coronary events. A laboratory reference limit near 4.5 identifies a value outside a conventional population interval, while the observational data show clearer risk associations at higher TSH concentrations. They do not by themselves establish a treatment threshold. [TSH reference-range review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/) [Coronary outcome data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/)

### Normalizing TSH does not necessarily improve how people feel

The TRUST randomized trial enrolled 737 adults aged 65 or older with persistent subclinical hypothyroidism. Levothyroxine lowered TSH but provided no apparent clinical benefit in thyroid-related symptoms or tiredness. The trial was funded primarily through European public research funding. (Stott et al., 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28402245/)

A subsequent systematic review of 21 randomized trials involving 2,192 nonpregnant adults found that thyroid hormone therapy lowered TSH into the reference range but did not improve general quality of life or thyroid-related symptoms. Overall risk of bias was considered low, and evidence quality was moderate to high. One contributing trial received study drug from a manufacturer, but most reported conflicts were related to publicly funded thyroid research rather than commercial sponsorship. (Feller et al., 2018). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30285179/)

These findings apply primarily to mild subclinical hypothyroidism in nonpregnant adults. They do not resolve management in pregnancy, overt hypothyroidism, marked TSH elevation, progressive biochemical change, or selected symptomatic or antibody-positive cases. [TRUST trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28402245/) [Systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30285179/) [TSH reference-range review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/)

## Fasting glucose: lowering the cutoff improved sensitivity while reducing specificity

### The diabetes and prediabetes thresholds have different histories

In 1997, an expert committee lowered the fasting plasma glucose threshold for diabetes from 140 to 126 mg/dL. The change was intended to align fasting glucose more closely with the 2-hour oral glucose tolerance criterion and with the glucose range associated with diabetes-specific microvascular complications, especially retinopathy. (Expert Committee, 1997). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9203460/)

The 100 mg/dL cutoff has a different purpose. In 2003, the American Diabetes Association lowered its lower boundary for impaired fasting glucose from 110 to 100 mg/dL. This created a broader intermediate-risk category; it did not redefine 100 mg/dL as diabetes. (Expert Committee, 2003). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14578255/)

Consequently, saying “fasting glucose under 100 is fine” is overly reassuring, but saying “100 is disease” is also wrong. Risk can rise below 100 and continues across the threshold in the cited cohorts. [Normal-range fasting glucose cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16207847/) [Asturias Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

### The Asturias Study shows the tradeoff directly

In a prospective population study, lowering the impaired-fasting-glucose threshold from 110 to 100 mg/dL tripled the number of people classified as having impaired fasting glucose. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

Diabetes incidence was:

- 3.8 per 1,000 person-years with baseline fasting glucose below 100 mg/dL
- 19.5 per 1,000 person-years at 100-109 mg/dL
- 58.0 per 1,000 person-years at 110-125 mg/dL. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

Lowering the threshold increased sensitivity for future diabetes from 43.2 to 75 percent, but reduced specificity from 94.5 to 77.3 percent and reduced positive predictive value from 36.5 to 19.9 percent. The additional 100-109 group therefore had higher risk than people below 100, but substantially lower risk than the 110-125 group. Other risk factors improved stratification further. (Valdés et al., 2008). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

This is not evidence that either 100 or 110 is uniquely correct. It demonstrates that the choice depends on how much missed risk, additional risk labeling, follow-up testing, anxiety, and intervention burden a health system is willing to accept. [Asturias Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/) [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

### Prevention works in selected high-risk people, not necessarily everyone just above 100

The Diabetes Prevention Program randomized 3,234 high-risk adults to intensive lifestyle intervention, metformin, or placebo. Lifestyle intervention reduced diabetes incidence by 58 percent and metformin by 31 percent over approximately 2.8 years. (Diabetes Prevention Program Research Group, 2002). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/)

However, participants were not selected merely for a fasting glucose of 100-109 mg/dL. They were overweight or obese and had impaired glucose tolerance on oral testing, making them substantially higher risk than many people identified solely through the expanded impaired-fasting-glucose definition. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/)

Over ten years, the intensive lifestyle program cost approximately $10,000 per quality-adjusted life-year gained, while metformin was marginally cost-saving or cost-neutral compared with placebo. These results support intervention in DPP-like high-risk populations; they do not establish that lowering the population cutoff from 110 to 100 was itself cost-effective. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22442395/) [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308273/)

## What happens just above versus just below the cutoffs?

### Total cholesterol: 199 versus 201 mg/dL

There is no demonstrated outcome discontinuity. Coronary risk is continuous, and contemporary treatment decisions use a broader risk profile. The difference may still change the category displayed on a laboratory report, but it does not represent a physiological transition. [Continuous risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/) [Current guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41824552/)

### Vitamin D: 29 versus 31 ng/mL

Randomized trials have not established that people below 30 derive a meaningful benefit that people above 30 do not. The Endocrine Society stopped endorsing this distinction for generally healthy people in 2024. [VITAL fracture analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35939577/) [Guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)

### TSH: 4.4 versus 4.6 mIU/L

The difference may move a result outside a laboratory reference interval, but age-specific distributions and outcome studies do not show a clinical cliff at 4.5. In the large cohort synthesis, coronary-event risk at TSH 4.5-6.9 was not statistically different from the reference group, with a hazard ratio of 1.00 and a 95 percent confidence interval of 0.86-1.18, while the observed association was appreciably stronger at 10 or above. [Age-specific reference data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/) [Coronary outcome data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/)

### Fasting glucose: 99 versus 101 mg/dL

Risk overlaps substantially. Moving the boundary to 100 detects more future diabetes but classifies many more people who do not progress during the study follow-up. The category is best understood as an invitation to assess the full metabolic risk profile, not as proof that a disease process began between the two measurements. [Normal-range fasting glucose cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16207847/) [Asturias Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

## Are the thresholds arbitrary?

They are generally **conventional rather than arbitrary**. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

The cited reports show that the thresholds were based on differing combinations of outcome associations, population distributions, assay performance, feasibility, precedent, and expert judgment. The weakness is that the final number can appear much more certain than the evidence used to choose it. [Cholesterol program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Vitamin D dietary reference report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118827/) [TSH reference-range review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/) [Diabetes threshold report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14578255/)

The four thresholds fall on a spectrum:

- **Total cholesterol 200** was introduced for population screening and dietary action but never represented a risk cliff. [Historical program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Continuous risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/)
- **Vitamin D 30** was proposed in the 2011 clinical guideline but was not validated as a universal outcome-based threshold and is no longer endorsed as a universal sufficiency target. [2011 guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21646368/) [2024 guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)
- **TSH 4-4.5** is commonly used as a general adult reference boundary, but it is not universal across age groups and should not automatically trigger treatment. [Age-specific reference data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/) [Treatment review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30285179/)
- **Fasting glucose 100** favors sensitivity over specificity and identifies a heterogeneous risk category. [Diabetes threshold report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14578255/) [Asturias Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

## Cost-effectiveness evidence for changing thresholds

The economic evidence cited here evaluates downstream interventions rather than directly comparing old and proposed biomarker cutoffs. [Statin microsimulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26172894/) [DPP economic analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22442395/)

For cholesterol, a US microsimulation evaluated statin treatment according to 10-year cardiovascular risk rather than total cholesterol. A 7.5 percent treatment threshold had an estimated incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of $37,000 per quality-adjusted life-year compared with a 10 percent threshold. Thresholds of 4 and 3 percent produced ratios of approximately $81,000 and $140,000 per quality-adjusted life-year. The result was highly sensitive to statin price, the burden attributed to taking a daily pill, and the assumed risk of statin-associated diabetes. This was a model based on the prices and assumptions available at the time, and it evaluated risk-based statin strategies rather than the biological validity of total cholesterol 200 mg/dL. (Pandya et al., 2015). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26172894/)

This illustrates why treatment thresholds should be tied to absolute risk and expected benefit, not merely to whether a single biomarker is outside a reference range. [Statin microsimulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26172894/) [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

For fasting glucose, the DPP economic analysis shows that intensive intervention can be good value in a highly selected high-risk population. It does not resolve whether every person newly labeled by the 100 mg/dL boundary benefits enough to justify testing, clinical visits, medication discussions, and long-term surveillance. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22442395/) [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308273/)

No direct cutoff-comparison economic analysis is cited here for universal cutoffs of 20 versus 30 ng/mL or 2.5 versus 4.5 mIU/L. Any future analysis would need to include the cost of expanded testing, repeat measurements, additional or potentially unnecessary labels, treatment, adverse effects, and the number of meaningful clinical outcomes prevented, not merely the number of laboratory values normalized.

## What physician surveys show

The historical cholesterol survey documents rapid changes in physicians' reported thresholds and uptake of the National Cholesterol Education Program recommendations. More than 90 percent of surveyed physicians reported using the recommendations by 1990, and their reported diet and drug treatment thresholds fell sharply. The survey does not isolate the causal effect of the threshold from the accompanying educational program. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2012446/)

In the cited prediabetes survey, knowledge was incomplete. Only 17 percent of primary care clinicians correctly identified the diagnostic laboratory ranges for prediabetes using both fasting glucose and hemoglobin A1c. Nearly 90 percent nevertheless reported close follow-up of patients carrying the diagnosis. The study was supported by US public research grants, and the authors reported no conflicts of interest. The survey measured knowledge of guideline ranges and reported practice, not understanding of the statistical basis of thresholds. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28730532/)

Case-based thyroid surveys show substantial international variation. In one multinational survey, which had a 21.9 percent response rate, North American clinicians were more likely than clinicians elsewhere to treat an older person with mild subclinical hypothyroidism, with an odds ratio of 2.24. A separate general-practitioner survey likewise found large country-level variation in treatment strategies. These studies demonstrate variation in application, although they do not directly measure whether clinicians understand the statistical limitations of the reference interval. [Multinational survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8314758/) [General-practitioner survey](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25624308/)

The survey literature cited here does not include a study specifically testing clinicians' understanding of the 20-versus-30 vitamin D controversy. That is an evidence gap in this article's cited record, not evidence that physicians either do or do not understand the dispute.

## Was industry influence responsible?

When a new cutoff expands the population eligible for testing, medication, or supplements, financial conflicts warrant specific scrutiny. That concern should not substitute for documentation specific to each threshold.

The cited record does not establish that industry created the cholesterol boundary of 200 mg/dL or the vitamin D target of 30 ng/mL:

- The early cholesterol program and its national physician surveys were sponsored by the National Institutes of Health. [Program description](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Physician surveys](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2012446/)
- VITAL was funded primarily by the National Institutes of Health, with study agents and matching placebos donated by manufacturers, and found no broad cardiovascular or cancer benefit from vitamin D supplementation. Its separately published fracture analysis found no reduction in total, nonvertebral, or hip fractures. [Cancer and cardiovascular outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415629/) [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6425757/) [Fracture outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35939577/)
- The 2026 vitamin D and calcium meta-analysis reported no industry financial support for the submitted work. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42161415/)
- TRUST was primarily supported through European public research funding and found no symptomatic benefit from treating mild subclinical hypothyroidism in older adults. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28402245/)

Funding of later trials cannot by itself establish how older thresholds originated. This does not prove that commercial incentives played no role in dissemination, testing volume, supplement marketing, or guideline debate. It means that a causal claim that industry **set** these specific thresholds requires direct archival and conflict-of-interest evidence that is not contained in the sources cited here.

## Quick reference values

- **Total cholesterol 200 mg/dL:** Historically a screening and dietary-action boundary. Cardiovascular risk is continuous; it is not a current standalone medication threshold. [Historical program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Continuous risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/) [Current guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2026-guideline-on-the-management-of-dyslipidemia)
- **25-hydroxyvitamin D 30 ng/mL:** Former Endocrine Society sufficiency target. The Society stopped endorsing this universal definition in 2024 because trials could not identify an outcome-based benefit threshold. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)
- **TSH approximately 4-4.5 mIU/L:** A commonly used adult reference boundary, not a universal treatment threshold. The appropriate upper reference value rises with age, and observed outcome associations are clearer at TSH of 10 mIU/L or higher. [Age-specific reference data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/) [Coronary outcome data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/)
- **Fasting glucose 100 mg/dL:** Lower boundary of an expanded intermediate-risk category. Lowering the cutoff improved sensitivity but reduced specificity and positive predictive value. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)

## Practical interpretation

- Ask what the threshold was designed to do: describe a population, predict an outcome, establish a diagnosis, or trigger treatment. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)
- Avoid interpreting “within range” as “zero risk” or “outside range” as “disease.” [Continuous cholesterol risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/) [Normal-range fasting glucose cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16207847/) [Age-specific TSH data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911171/)
- Values close to a cutoff should generally be interpreted with measurement variability, repeat testing, trends, symptoms, medications, age, and the broader risk profile in mind. [CLSI reference-interval standard](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/ep28/) [Current dyslipidemia guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41824552/)
- Treatment should be justified by evidence that intervening in people with that degree of abnormality improves outcomes, not merely by evidence that treatment moves the laboratory value. [TRUST trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28402245/) [Thyroid-treatment review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30285179/) [VITAL trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415629/)
- Laboratories and patient materials should describe categories as ranges of risk or uncertainty wherever the underlying evidence is continuous. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/) [Continuous cholesterol risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/)
- Bright-line rules can remain for workflows, insurance, screening programs, and public-health communication, but the report should state what action the line is intended to trigger. [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

## Conclusion

Biomarker dogmas hold back interpretation when a decision threshold is presented as a fact of biology. [Reference intervals and decision limits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683305/)

The remedy is not to abolish thresholds. Clinical systems need boundaries for screening, diagnosis, treatment eligibility, and communication. The remedy is to make their function explicit: what evidence produced the number, what outcome it predicts, how risk behaves on either side, what action changes at the line, and whether that action produces net benefit. [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)

Of the competing claims, the evidence supports a synthesis:

1. The reviewed reports show that expert panels used defined goals such as screening, dietary guidance, population reference distributions, or improved sensitivity, but the outcome evidence was not equally strong for every biomarker. [Cholesterol program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3287928/) [Vitamin D dietary reference report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118827/) [TSH reference-range review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24014812/) [Diabetes threshold report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14578255/)
2. Some thresholds persisted despite limited outcome-based evidence, with the former vitamin D target of 30 ng/mL providing the clearest example. [Guideline communication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828961/)
3. False precision is the central interpretive problem; the cited evidence does not demonstrate an abrupt outcome discontinuity at the commonly quoted number for any of the four biomarkers in the populations studied. [Continuous cholesterol risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3773199/) [Vitamin D fracture analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35939577/) [TSH coronary outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20858880/) [Fasting-glucose outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18249214/)
4. Thresholds remain necessary, but they should function as transparent decision tools rather than declarations that one side is healthy and the other diseased. [Clinical decision thresholds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)
