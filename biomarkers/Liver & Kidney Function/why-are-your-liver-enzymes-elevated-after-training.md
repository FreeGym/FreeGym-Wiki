# Why Are Your Liver Enzymes Elevated After Training?

ALT and AST are commonly called “liver enzymes,” but that label does not tell you where an elevation came from. AST is abundant in skeletal muscle and several other tissues. ALT is more concentrated in the liver, but it is also present in muscle. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438350/))

Strenuous exercise - particularly unaccustomed resistance training, eccentric exercise, and prolonged endurance work - can therefore raise both enzymes. Depending on the exercise protocol and the individual, the change may be negligible, modest, or large enough to resemble liver injury. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

The important correction is that exercise-related elevations do not always resolve within 48 to 72 hours. After sufficiently muscle-damaging resistance exercise, AST, ALT, and creatine kinase can remain elevated for at least seven days. After extreme endurance events, abnormalities may persist even longer. Conversely, the cited treadmill study produced modest mean ALT changes, and no participant in the half-marathon study exceeded the laboratory ALT reference limit. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

Recent exercise should therefore be treated as a competing explanation that is routinely checked - not as the presumed diagnosis and not as an automatic reason to dismiss an abnormal result. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

## “Liver enzymes” is an imprecise label

AST, or aspartate aminotransferase, is found in the liver, skeletal muscle, cardiac muscle, kidneys, brain, pancreas, lungs, leukocytes, and red blood cells. ALT, or alanine aminotransferase, is more liver-enriched but is also present in skeletal muscle at lower concentrations. Damage or increased membrane permeability in any of these tissues can release the enzymes into the circulation. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438350/))

This means that ALT and AST are better described as aminotransferases or markers of cellular injury than as direct tests of liver function. They do not measure hepatic function directly, and they cannot identify their tissue of origin when interpreted in isolation. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020)) ([Guideline PDF](https://acgcdn.gi.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/ACG-Abnormal-Liver-Chemistries-Guideline-Summary.pdf); [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7081005/))

AST is generally more responsive to skeletal-muscle injury than ALT. After substantial muscle damage, AST commonly rises earlier and reaches a higher concentration, producing an AST-to-ALT ratio above one. But the ratio is not tissue-specific: liver disorders can also produce AST-predominant patterns, so the ratio cannot determine the source by itself. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438350/))

## How much can exercise raise ALT and AST?

There is no single reliable “typical” post-exercise elevation across exercise protocols and individuals. Published studies range from modest mean changes that remain within reference intervals to AST approaching 1,000 U/L after highly damaging, unaccustomed resistance exercise. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

### High-intensity treadmill exercise

In a study of 44 sedentary postpubertal boys and girls, a high-intensity treadmill protocol produced relatively modest changes at 48 hours. The published group means correspond to AST increases of approximately 28 percent in boys and 38 percent in girls, and ALT increases of approximately 26 and 30 percent, respectively. Mean AST at 48 hours was approximately 31 U/L in boys and 26 U/L in girls; mean ALT was approximately 25 U/L in both groups. CK increased by 84 percent in boys and 35 percent in girls. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28767482/))

In this cohort, a hard exercise bout measurably altered the panel without producing markedly abnormal mean ALT or AST. The results do not support presenting ALT of 50-80 U/L and AST of 70-120 U/L as an expected result after every difficult workout. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167))

### Half-marathon running

After a half-marathon, AST, CK, LDH, and total and direct bilirubin increased and remained above baseline 24 hours later. However, no participant exceeded the laboratory upper reference limit for ALT, ALP, or GGT. ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21925040/))

The bilirubin increase also illustrates an important complication: endurance exercise can alter markers beyond the aminotransferases and CK. A single marker should therefore not be treated as perfectly specific for one tissue or mechanism. ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007))

### Unaccustomed heavy resistance exercise

A prospective single-group repeated-measures study involved 15 healthy men who were accustomed to moderate activity but not weightlifting. They completed an approximately one-hour weightlifting session and underwent repeated blood testing for seven days, with follow-up at 10 to 12 days. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2291230/))

Among the 14 men included in the main analysis ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x)):

- All had AST above the reference interval on days three through five. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- The highest observed AST was 16.0 μkat/L, approximately 960 U/L. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- The highest observed ALT was 4.1 μkat/L, approximately 246 U/L. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- AST, ALT, CK, LDH, and myoglobin remained significantly elevated for at least seven days. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- Bilirubin, GGT, and ALP were almost unchanged overall. No participant exceeded the GGT reference interval, while one participant transiently exceeded the reference interval for ALP and one for bilirubin. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- Fifty-seven percent developed CK above 10,000 U/L. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2291230/))


These results establish that muscle-damaging resistance exercise can produce aminotransferase values that look highly pathological on a liver panel. But they should not be generalized to familiar routine training: the participants were not accustomed to weightlifting, and CK above 10,000 U/L is a marked biochemical elevation consistent with substantial muscle injury, although CK alone does not establish the clinical diagnosis or severity of exertional rhabdomyolysis. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344))

The study therefore shows what exercise _can_ do under this protocol, not what a familiar routine squat or deadlift session _usually_ does. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

### Extreme endurance exercise

In nine athletes completing a 1,600-km ultramarathon, mean AST, ALT, and CK remained elevated after the race and at measurements on days four and eleven. ALP and GGT did not show the same persistent above-reference pattern. ([Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1756186/))

This was an extreme exposure in a very small cohort, but it further demonstrates that the effect cannot always be confined to a 48-72-hour window. ([Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

## Why 48-72 hours is not a universal washout period

The kinetics of muscle-associated aminotransferase elevation are delayed. In clinically significant muscle injury, CK often begins rising within the first day, peaks around 72 hours, and then declines over the following week or longer. AST commonly rises by approximately 24 hours and peaks around days three to four. ALT may not rise clearly until approximately 48 hours and can peak around days four to five. In severe rhabdomyolysis, aminotransferase abnormalities may persist for two to three weeks. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7081005/))

A blood draw 24 to 48 hours after a damaging workout may therefore occur while the enzymes are still rising. Waiting 48 to 72 hours may reduce interference after some exercise sessions, but it is not guaranteed to return results to baseline after unaccustomed lifting, high-volume eccentric work, severe delayed-onset muscle soreness, ultradistance exercise, or exertional rhabdomyolysis. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

The cited studies did not directly compare 48-hour, 72-hour, and seven-day exercise abstinence to determine which interval best prevents exercise-related aminotransferase abnormalities. Claims that 48-72 hours will “eliminate most false positives” are therefore plausible but not established by these studies. ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

## What points toward a muscle source?

No individual finding proves that an elevation comes from muscle, but the following combination makes a muscle contribution more likely. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

### Recent muscle-damaging exercise

The most relevant history is not simply whether someone “exercises.” The clinician needs to know the timing, familiarity, type, intensity, and symptoms associated with the recent session ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344)):

- When the last session occurred. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- Whether it was familiar or unaccustomed. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- Whether it involved heavy resistance or eccentric loading. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- Whether volume or intensity was unusually high. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- Whether there is substantial soreness, weakness, swelling, or impaired function. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344))


Training experience can reduce the muscle-injury response to a familiar workload. A novel or unusually demanding session is more likely to produce marked biomarker leakage than a familiar session of comparable type. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

### Elevated CK and compatible kinetics

CK is much more closely associated with skeletal-muscle injury than ALT or AST. A high CK accompanying AST and ALT elevation, particularly when AST and CK decline together during recovery, supports a muscle contribution. LDH and myoglobin can provide additional context. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438350/))

CK is not a perfect answer. A muscle contribution does not exclude a separate liver disorder, and CK may already be declining by the time testing occurs. Nevertheless, omitting CK in an active person with unexplained aminotransferase elevation removes one of the most informative clues to a muscle source. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

### Normal GGT, ALP, and bilirubin

GGT is not meaningfully present in skeletal muscle, and bilirubin is not expected to rise from isolated muscle-cell leakage. Normal GGT, ALP, and bilirubin alongside high CK and aminotransferases therefore support a substantial muscle contribution. They do not exclude concurrent liver disease. In a study of 528 patients with rhabdomyolysis, CK was associated with ALT but not with ALP, bilirubin, or GGT. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7081005/))

This pattern is supportive rather than definitive. Endurance exercise can transiently affect bilirubin, and a muscle-associated aminotransferase pattern does not by itself rule out liver disease. ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

### AST higher than ALT early in the course

AST generally rises earlier and more strongly after muscle injury. An AST-to-ALT ratio above one is common during the first week after damaging resistance exercise. But this ratio is not sufficient to distinguish muscle injury from a hepatic source. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2291230/))

### Resolution under standardized conditions

A fall in AST, ALT, and CK after adequate exercise abstinence supports an exercise-related explanation. It does not prove that the liver is normal, but it provides stronger evidence than simply assuming the elevation was caused by the last workout. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

## Findings that should not be dismissed as exercise

Recent training should not end the evaluation when the pattern is atypical. Liver-focused investigation becomes more important when there is:

- Persistent elevation despite an adequate period without strenuous exercise. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- ALT that continues to rise beyond the expected delayed muscle-injury pattern, particularly after substantial resolution of CK and AST. A brief lagging ALT rise during the first several days can still occur after muscle injury because ALT often peaks later than AST. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

- Elevated GGT, ALP, or bilirubin without another satisfactory explanation. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

- Elevated prothrombin time or other evidence of impaired hepatic function or advanced disease. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

- Jaundice, persistent right-upper-quadrant pain, unexplained systemic illness, or other concerning symptoms. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

- Relevant medication, supplement, alcohol, viral, metabolic, autoimmune, or family-history risk. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517); [Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- An ALT concentration far outside the range ordinarily attributed to isolated muscle injury. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))


A review of rhabdomyolysis data proposed that ALT above approximately 800 U/L, an atypical trajectory, or accompanying bilirubin or GGT elevation is inconsistent with isolated muscle injury and warrants evaluation for liver disease. This is a clinical heuristic rather than a validated cutoff: lower ALT does not exclude liver disease, and ALT above 800 U/L does not identify a specific diagnosis. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7081005/))

## Exercise-induced does not automatically mean benign

An exercise-associated laboratory abnormality can be transient without being harmless. In the weightlifting study, more than half the participants developed CK above 10,000 U/L. That represents substantial skeletal-muscle injury, even though bilirubin, GGT, and ALP were almost unchanged overall. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2291230/))

Rhabdomyolysis has no universally accepted CK cutoff. CK of at least five times the upper reference limit, often around 1,000 U/L, has historically been used as a sensitive general criterion, but it can have limited specificity after strenuous exercise. A 2025 international expert workshop proposed severe muscle symptoms, CK above 10,000 IU/L, and a compatible rise-and-fall pattern for exertional rhabdomyolysis. These are expert-consensus criteria, not a universal biochemical definition. Severe muscle pain, swelling, weakness, dark or tea-colored urine, reduced urine output, dehydration, or systemic illness after exercise warrants prompt medical assessment because exertional rhabdomyolysis can cause electrolyte disturbances and acute kidney injury. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40220353/))

“Caused by exercise” and “benign physiological response” are therefore not interchangeable conclusions. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344))

## What do the competing claims get right?

### Claim 1: Exercise should be the default diagnosis in a young, healthy, active person

**The evidence does not support this claim as written.** ([Fragala et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840); [Balou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.2.246))

Exercise is a well-documented cause of elevated AST and ALT, and it may be missed when recent training is not considered. But the cited literature does not establish that exercise is the most common cause of aminotransferase elevation in young, active people presenting for routine blood work. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22); [Fragala et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840); [Balou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.2.246))

A large cross-sectional study included 80,111 adults aged 18-34 and examined self-reported days per week of aerobic and resistance activity. More frequent activity was associated with some differences in AST, while ALT and GGT were generally lower in more active groups. However, the study did not record the timing, duration, or intensity of the most recent exercise session. It therefore cannot estimate the prevalence of acute post-exercise false positives. ([Fragala et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5653181/))

A 2025 cohort of 10,519 adults similarly found that greater habitual physical activity was associated with a lower prevalence of elevated ALT and GGT. This addresses long-term activity patterns, not whether a workout in the preceding days transiently raised an individual’s results. ([Balou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.2.246)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12188904/))

The defensible default is therefore: **ask about exercise and investigate muscle as a possible source**, not **assume exercise until proven otherwise**. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

### Claim 2: Exercise is real but should not be used to dismiss liver disease

**This is the best-supported position.** ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

Active and lean people can still have liver disease. A systematic review and meta-analysis using older NAFLD definitions estimated that lean NAFLD affected approximately 5.1 percent of the general population and that approximately 19.2 percent of people with NAFLD were lean. The exact prevalence varies by population, definition, and diagnostic method, but normal body weight does not rule out metabolic liver disease. ([Ye et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253%2820%2930077-7)) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32413340/))

Exercise and liver disease are also not mutually exclusive. Someone can have a muscle contribution to AST after training and a separate hepatic condition. A high CK or recent workout changes the interpretation; it does not exclude liver disease. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

### Claim 3: Standardized exercise abstinence before testing would reduce false positives

**The concept is reasonable, but a universal 48-72-hour interval is not adequately validated by the cited studies.** ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

The evidence supports controlling recent strenuous exercise when a clean baseline is important. It does not establish one ideal interval for every exercise exposure:

- Some exercise protocols produce only modest aminotransferase changes at 24 to 48 hours. The cited treadmill study reported modest mean ALT values, and no participant in the half-marathon study exceeded the laboratory ALT reference limit. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007))

- Unaccustomed heavy resistance exercise can affect results for at least seven days. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- Extreme endurance exercise can affect results for longer. ([Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

- Severe exertional muscle injury should be followed according to clinical recovery and CK trajectory rather than a fixed calendar interval. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344))


A pragmatic, tiered proposal is more defensible than a rigid rule:

- For routine baseline testing after ordinary training, avoiding vigorous exercise for **48-72 hours** is a pragmatic option intended to reduce short-term variability, not a validated universal cutoff. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007))

- After an unusually hard, novel, eccentric, or high-volume session - or when repeating a previously abnormal panel - approximately **seven days without strenuous training** provides a stronger test of whether the abnormality resolves with recovery. ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

- After extreme endurance exercise, very high CK, or suspected rhabdomyolysis, the repeat interval should be individualized and may need to be longer. ([Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344))


These intervals are an evidence-informed proposal. The cited studies did not compare them in a head-to-head trial. ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

### Claim 4: Laboratory reports should mention recent exercise

**This is plausible, but its effect on clinical outcomes has not been tested in the cited literature.** ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

A laboratory footnote could reduce the tendency to interpret every AST or ALT elevation as hepatic. However, the cited clinical and observational papers did not test whether such a footnote reduces hepatitis panels, imaging, referrals, biopsies, cost, or diagnostic delay. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

A useful footnote might read:

> **AST and ALT may increase after strenuous or unaccustomed exercise because skeletal muscle also contains these enzymes. Interpret with recent exercise history, CK, bilirubin, ALP/GGT, symptoms, and repeat testing after recovery when clinically appropriate.** ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

The footnote should not tell clinicians to disregard the result. It should prompt clinical correlation and appropriate retesting. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

A structured collection question may be even more useful:

> **Have you performed strenuous, unaccustomed, or prolonged exercise in the past seven days? If yes, record the type and date of the last session.** ([Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

## How often does exercise trigger unnecessary liver workups?

The available evidence shows that unnecessary workups can happen, but it does not establish how often. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

A 2023 case report described an active 39-year-old man with aminotransferases approximately 1.4 to 2.3 times the upper reference limit. He underwent viral and autoimmune testing, abdominal ultrasonography, and gastroenterology referral, with liver biopsy being considered. After seven days without exercise, AST fell into the reference interval and ALT fell from 101 to 47 U/L, remaining slightly above that laboratory’s upper limit of 44 U/L. CK had not been measured, so the response to exercise abstinence supported, but did not prove, an exercise-associated explanation. Exercise history and CK had not been incorporated early in the pathway. ([Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10071916/))

Reviews of rhabdomyolysis cases similarly report negative liver serology, normal or nonspecific imaging, and occasional liver biopsy when the muscle source of aminotransferases was not recognized. But these reports involve selected patients with substantial muscle injury and provide no denominator from which to calculate the rate of unnecessary exercise-related workups. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7081005/))

Accordingly, statements that this happens “routinely” or accounts for a particular fraction of abnormal liver panels are not supported by the cited prevalence studies or case-based literature. ([Fragala et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840); [Balou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.2.246); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

## Quick reference

- AST is widely distributed in muscle and other tissues; ALT is more liver-enriched but is not liver-exclusive. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005)) ([Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438350/))

- Exercise-related elevations range from small changes that remain within reference intervals to AST near 1,000 U/L after highly damaging, unaccustomed resistance exercise. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- CK, LDH, myoglobin, the exercise history, and the time course help identify a muscle contribution. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

- Normal bilirubin, GGT, and ALP support - but do not prove - a muscle source. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007))

- AST greater than ALT is common after muscle injury but is not diagnostic. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

- A 48-72-hour washout may reduce short-term variability after some workouts, but heavy unaccustomed lifting can affect results for at least seven days. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x))

- Exercise should be considered early, not presumed automatically. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

- The cited literature does not provide a reliable prevalence of acute exercise-induced aminotransferase elevation in routine clinical testing. ([Fragala et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840); [Balou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.2.246))

- The cited literature does not quantify the proportion of liver imaging, referrals, or biopsies that could be prevented by exercise standardization. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))


## Practical interpretation

- A red flag on a laboratory report means that a value is outside that laboratory’s reference interval. It does not identify the organ that released the enzyme. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517); [Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- Ask about the exact timing and nature of recent exercise before attributing an aminotransferase elevation to the liver. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

- Include CK when recent strenuous exercise or muscle symptoms make a muscle source plausible. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

- Interpret AST and ALT alongside bilirubin, GGT, ALP, symptoms, medications, alcohol exposure, metabolic risk, and relevant liver-disease history. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

- When the person is clinically well and the overall pattern supports muscle leakage, repeating the panel after a sufficiently long period without strenuous training can test whether the abnormality resolves before escalation. ([Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

- Do not use exercise to dismiss persistent, progressive, cholestatic, synthetic-function, or clinically symptomatic abnormalities. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Kwo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ajg.2016.517))

- Do not describe marked CK elevation or suspected exertional rhabdomyolysis as merely a harmless laboratory artifact. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [276th ENMC Workshop, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105344))


The most defensible conclusion is not “the liver panel is wrong.” It is that ALT and AST are nonspecific markers whose interpretation depends on tissue context. Recent hard training belongs in that context whenever aminotransferase results are interpreted. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020))

## Evidence gaps

The studies summarized here leave several central clinical questions unanswered:

- The prevalence of acute exercise-related AST or ALT elevation among people receiving routine blood work. ([Fragala et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840); [Balou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.22088/cjim.16.2.246))

- The influence of training status, sex, exercise modality, session novelty, eccentric load, and total work on clinically abnormal results. ([Pal et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002167); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005))

- Direct comparisons of 24-hour, 48-hour, 72-hour, and seven-day exercise washout periods. ([Lippi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2011.02.007); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Fallon et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsm.33.4.264))

- The reduction in false positives achieved by standardized pre-test instructions. ([Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

- The number and cost of hepatitis panels, imaging studies, referrals, and biopsies caused by unrecognized exercise effects. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

- Whether laboratory footnotes or structured exercise-history questions improve diagnostic accuracy without delaying genuine liver-disease diagnosis. ([Lim, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))


Until better evidence is available, a tiered rest interval and explicit collection of recent exercise history are reasonable evidence-informed proposals, but they should not be presented as settled clinical rules. ([Kim and Wu, 2020](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005); [Pettersson et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x); [Tiller and Stringer, 2023](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22))

## Funding and conflict-of-interest context

The [Pettersson weightlifting study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.2007.03001.x) included authors affiliated with AstraZeneca R&D and was partly motivated by the problem of exercise confounding liver-safety measurements in clinical trials. It did not test a commercial exercise or supplement product, but the institutional context should be noted.

The [80,111-person Fragala study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0180840) was conducted by Quest Diagnostics employees; the company supported author salaries, and the authors reported company stock ownership. Its findings concern habitual activity rather than acute post-exercise testing and should be interpreted within that design and employment context.

The [Kim and Wu narrative review](https://doi.org/10.14218/JCTH.2020.00005) and the [Lim rhabdomyolysis review](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i10.1020) reported no relevant conflicts. The [Tiller and Stringer case report](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1923_22) reported nil study sponsorship and no conflicts of interest while also disclosing that one author was supported by a Tobacco-Related Disease Research Program postdoctoral fellowship.

Where a complete funding or conflict disclosure is not available in an accessible study record, the absence of a disclosure should not be interpreted as confirmation that no conflict existed.
