# Total Testosterone vs Free Testosterone: Which Matters?

**Total testosterone is the appropriate starting measurement for most evaluations, but reliable free testosterone can materially change the interpretation when total testosterone is borderline or sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) is abnormal.** The evidence supports using these measurements together selectively, not treating total testosterone as universally sufficient, or free testosterone as automatically superior regardless of how it was measured. This is also the approach recommended in the Endocrine Society’s clinical guideline. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465))

The central distinction is between a measurement that correlates with androgen-deficiency symptoms and a measurement that identifies who benefits from treatment. Free testosterone has useful evidence for the first purpose. Its superiority over total testosterone for selecting treatment is not established by a direct comparison of testing strategies in the studies reviewed here. ([Enlighten Publications](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/163468/7/163468.pdf); [University Medical Center Utrecht](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/))

## What the different measurements represent

Total testosterone includes unbound testosterone and testosterone bound to circulating proteins. Free testosterone is the unbound fraction. “Bioavailable testosterone” conventionally means free testosterone plus the more readily dissociable albumin-bound fraction. These measurements describe overlapping components of the same circulating hormone pool, not separate kinds of testosterone. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/84/10/3666/2660660))

The percentages are not fixed. SHBG, albumin, and testosterone concentrations influence the distribution, so “60-70% SHBG-bound, 20-30% albumin-bound, and 2% free” should not be presented as a universal partition for adult men. In one modern equilibrium-dialysis validation study, the median free fraction in male samples was approximately 1.5%, with substantial variation between individuals. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600))

Calling SHBG-bound testosterone “biologically inactive” also needs qualification. It is not immediately available for passive diffusion while bound, but binding is reversible. Albumin’s weaker binding allows testosterone to dissociate more readily; “weakly bound” does not mean that the testosterone released has weak biological activity. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/84/10/3666/2660660); [Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600))

## Why SHBG can change the interpretation

SHBG tends to be lower in obesity, diabetes, and hypothyroidism, and higher with aging, hyperthyroidism, and cirrhosis. Some medications also alter SHBG. These conditions can make total testosterone a less reliable indicator of the unbound concentration. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465))

Importantly, SHBG changes do not simply alter free testosterone while leaving total testosterone unchanged. In obesity, low SHBG can accompany a lower total testosterone concentration while free testosterone remains within its reference range. Conversely, higher SHBG can accompany relatively preserved total testosterone despite low free testosterone. Both patterns have been documented in population studies. ([Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146))

The practical question is therefore not whether one man’s total testosterone is numerically higher than another’s. It is whether the combination of total testosterone, binding proteins, symptoms, and repeat measurements indicates androgen deficiency. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465))

## What symptom studies show

### Discordant total and free testosterone can be clinically meaningful

Antonio and colleagues studied **3,334 men aged 40-79** in the European Male Ageing Study, or EMAS. Using study thresholds of total testosterone below 10.5 nmol/L (approximately 303 ng/dL) and calculated free testosterone below 220 pmol/L (approximately 63 pg/mL), they identified 277 men with normal total but low free testosterone, and 96 with low total but normal free testosterone. Approximately **11% had discordant classifications** under these thresholds. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/101/7/2647/2810108))

Men with normal total but low free testosterone had higher SHBG and more sexual and physical symptoms. Men with low total but normal free testosterone were more often obese, had lower SHBG, and did not show the same excess of sexual and physical symptoms relative to men with both measurements normal. This supports adding free testosterone when total testosterone alone may not adequately characterize androgen status; it does not establish that every discordant result represents a treatment-responsive disorder. The analysis was cross-sectional, with a single testosterone measurement rather than repeated diagnostic confirmation; biochemical discordance is not itself a measured misdiagnosis rate. ([Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146))

### Longitudinal evidence supports that distinction

A prospective EMAS analysis followed men for a median of **4.3 years**. It used calculated free testosterone below **170 pmol/L**, rather than the 220 pmol/L cutoff in the earlier analysis, and focused on incident secondary hypogonadism. Among men developing low total testosterone, 101 retained normal free testosterone and 38 developed low free testosterone as well. Increased sexual symptoms were associated with the decline in both measurements, rather than with isolated low total testosterone. For new or worsening erectile dysfunction, the low-total/low-free group had an odds ratio of 4.53, with a 95% confidence interval of 2.05-10.01, compared with men whose testosterone status remained normal. ([Enlighten Publications](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/163468/7/163468.pdf))

This strengthens the case that free testosterone helps distinguish clinically different low-total-testosterone states. Nevertheless, it remains observational evidence, the group developing low values for both measurements was small, and it comes from the same underlying EMAS population, not an independent replication. The 170 pmol/L cutoff had a prior guideline rationale but was selected partly through exploratory symptom analyses in the same dataset, which can make associations appear stronger than in independent validation. Men with normal total but low free testosterone were excluded, so this analysis does not provide longitudinal validation for that subgroup. ([Enlighten Publications](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/163468/7/163468.pdf))

### Free testosterone is not consistently a better symptom marker

Ramasamy and colleagues examined **231 clinic patients with total testosterone between 250 and 350 ng/dL**. They found no significant difference in questionnaire-assessed hypogonadal symptoms between men with lower and higher free testosterone, and no clear free-testosterone threshold separating symptomatic groups. That finding argues against assuming that free testosterone will resolve every borderline evaluation. The selected clinic population and restricted testosterone range limit generalization; failure to detect an association does not establish equivalence between the measurements. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26199166/))

Newer evidence is more favorable for physical function. A **2025 cross-sectional study of 557 Danish men**, including 231 older than 65, found that calculated free testosterone showed more consistent adjusted associations than total testosterone with measures of lean mass, strength, power, and physical performance in older men. Blood samples were taken at varying times without fasting, and the study did not establish superior diagnostic accuracy. The study did not test whether treating a low free-testosterone value improves those outcomes. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40520-025-03107-3))

Funding and conflicts are relevant on both sides of this debate. EMAS received public and charitable support, but its papers disclosed author relationships with pharmaceutical and diagnostic companies. ([Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146)) The Ramasamy paper disclosed a coauthor’s clinical-trial, consulting, and speaking relationships with Endo. ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4549166/)) The 2025 Danish study reported no competing interests. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40520-025-03107-3))

**Overall, free testosterone adds clinically useful information in some men, but neither total nor free testosterone functions as a stand-alone symptom test.** The evidence is more consistent for selected sexual and physical outcomes than for a universal syndrome identifiable from one laboratory cutoff. ([Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146); [Ramasamy et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26199166/))

## The measurement problem is real, but method-specific

### Direct analog assays are the weakest option

A laboratory experiment by Fritz and colleagues tested an analog free-testosterone immunoassay against carefully manipulated serum fractions. When total testosterone was held constant while actual free testosterone changed, the analog assay did not appropriately track the free concentration. It also failed to detect free testosterone in the dialysis fraction. Its reported “free testosterone” signal was related to total testosterone rather than specifically measuring the unbound fraction. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5677688_Analog-Based_Free_Testosterone_Test_Results_Linked_to_Total_Testosterone_Concentrations_Not_Free_Testosterone_Concentrations))

That is more serious than ordinary imprecision: the assay may not be measuring what its label suggests. The study received university support rather than external funding, although one author disclosed consulting for Antech Diagnostics. Its findings support the guideline recommendation against direct analog free-testosterone immunoassays. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5677688_Analog-Based_Free_Testosterone_Test_Results_Linked_to_Total_Testosterone_Concentrations_Not_Free_Testosterone_Concentrations); [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465))

“Direct” alone is not an adequate description of a laboratory method. Direct analog immunoassay and direct measurement after equilibrium dialysis are fundamentally different approaches. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465))

### Calculated free testosterone is useful, but formulas are not interchangeable

Calculated free testosterone combines measured total testosterone, SHBG, and usually albumin using a formula or binding model. It therefore inherits errors in the input measurements and assumptions in the formula. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/84/10/3666/2660660))

Fiers and colleagues compared calculations with equilibrium dialysis followed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. In male EMAS samples, the median calculated-to-measured ratio was **1.19 for Vermeulen**, **1.00 for Ly**, and **2.05 for the evaluated Zakharov calculation**. The Zakharov ratio used the original EMAS testosterone and SHBG inputs, whereas the other quoted ratios used remeasured inputs. With the original inputs, the Vermeulen and Ly ratios were 1.33 and 1.05, respectively. Vermeulen showed an upward bias but was more consistent across varying SHBG concentrations; a median ratio near one did not make the Ly calculation uniformly accurate. The authors reported no disclosures. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600))

The implication is not that calculation is useless. It is that a cutoff validated for one method should not automatically be applied to another. ([Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600); [Walravens et al.](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01KAGX7T02GSSKRKN8Y1Y0RE51/file/01KAGX8XMGYNKD12EY7CQ23ZTZ.pdf))

### Equilibrium dialysis is a reference method, not a guarantee of identical results

Equilibrium dialysis physically separates the free fraction before quantification, but implementation and standardization still matter. Walravens and colleagues’ **2026 paper**, published online in 2025, measured free testosterone in samples from 1,202 men using dialysis and mass spectrometry. The authors emphasized that harmonization and cross-validation remain necessary before results from different laboratories can be treated as equivalent. ([Ghent University Academic Bibliography](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01KAGX7T02GSSKRKN8Y1Y0RE51/file/01KAGX8XMGYNKD12EY7CQ23ZTZ.pdf))

They established a reference interval of **184-749 pmol/L** in **369 healthy, non-obese men aged 18-39**. This is a population reference interval, not proof that treatment helps everyone below 184 pmol/L, or that this boundary transfers to every assay. The study received public research funding and reported no disclosures. ([Ghent University Academic Bibliography](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01KAGX7T02GSSKRKN8Y1Y0RE51/file/01KAGX8XMGYNKD12EY7CQ23ZTZ.pdf))

Thus, measurement concerns justify identifying the method, using appropriate reference intervals, and checking discordant results. They do not justify treating every free-testosterone result as equally unreliable. ([Endocrine Society guideline](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465); [Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600))

## Is bioavailable testosterone better than free testosterone?

Bioavailable testosterone has a reasonable physiological rationale because albumin-bound testosterone can dissociate and contribute to tissue delivery. However, free and bioavailable testosterone are closely related when albumin concentrations are not markedly abnormal, so measuring both may provide overlapping information. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600))

A cross-sectional study by Liang and colleagues measured total and free testosterone and calculated bioavailable testosterone in **965 men aged 40-80**. Men with higher aging-symptom questionnaire scores had lower free and bioavailable testosterone and higher SHBG. This supports an association with both fractions, but it does not establish that bioavailable testosterone outperforms free testosterone. The groups also differed substantially in age, and questionnaire-defined symptoms were not equivalent to a confirmed endocrine diagnosis. The authors declared no conflicts of interest. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33715769/))

The measurement distinction also matters here. **Directly measuring** bioavailable testosterone requires laboratory separation of the non-SHBG-bound fraction. **Calculating** it can use the same total-testosterone, SHBG, and albumin inputs already available for calculated free testosterone; it is not necessarily an additional difficult test. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/84/10/3666/2660660))

The evidence reviewed here therefore supports bioavailable testosterone as an alternative index of androgen availability, but does not establish it as a superior diagnostic or treatment target. ([Vermeulen et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/84/10/3666/2660660); [Liang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33715769/))

## What treatment trials can and cannot tell us

### Total-testosterone selection can identify men who benefit

A 2023 systematic review and individual-participant-data meta-analysis by Hudson and colleagues identified **35 trials involving 5,601 participants**, with individual data available from 17 trials involving 3,431 participants. Eligible populations had baseline total testosterone below 12 nmol/L. Testosterone treatment produced modest improvements in sexual function; within the studied low-testosterone range, baseline total testosterone did not clearly identify a subgroup with a larger treatment effect. The literature search ended in 2018, so this synthesis did not include the later TRAVERSE results. ([University Medical Center Utrecht](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/))

The review was publicly funded by the UK National Institute for Health and Care Research, but authors disclosed pharmaceutical relationships, including a Logixx Pharma research grant. Public funding of a meta-analysis should not be confused with absence of conflicts among its authors or included trials. ([University Medical Center Utrecht](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/))

The newer **TRAVERSE sexual-function substudy** enrolled 1,161 men with low libido from a trial requiring symptoms and two morning total-testosterone concentrations below 300 ng/dL. Participants were aged 45-80 and had cardiovascular disease or increased cardiovascular risk. Treatment improved sexual activity, desire, and hypogonadal symptoms, but not erectile function. The trial was funded by a consortium of testosterone manufacturers led by AbbVie. These findings support benefit in a total-testosterone-selected population; they do not compare that selection strategy against free-testosterone-based selection. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/109/2/569/7244351))

### Hormone-response associations do not establish a treatment cutoff

In the Testosterone Trials’ sexual-function study, **470 men aged at least 65** had low libido and an average total testosterone below 275 ng/dL. Increases in total testosterone, free testosterone, and estradiol were associated with improvements in sexual desire and activity, but the investigators did not identify a clear hormone threshold for those outcomes. These associations do not isolate each hormone’s independent causal contribution. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/101/8/3096/2835036))

The trials had substantial public funding, but AbbVie also supplied funding, testosterone gel, and placebo. The investigators reported that AbbVie did not participate in trial design, conduct, analysis, or reporting. This study supports a treatment effect in selected men, not the superiority of a free-testosterone treatment threshold. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/101/8/3096/2835036))

### Some trials have selected men using free testosterone

It would be incorrect to say that free-testosterone thresholds have never been used in randomized trials. Legros and colleagues randomized **322 symptomatic men aged at least 50 with calculated free testosterone below 0.26 nmol/L** to oral testosterone undecanoate or placebo. The primary overall symptom-score outcome did not improve significantly versus placebo, although the 160 mg/day group showed improvement in the sexual-symptom domain. That secondary finding warrants caution given the multiple doses and outcomes examined. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article-pdf/160/5/821/45870227/821.pdf))

The trial was sponsored by Schering-Plough and included company-employed authors. The sponsor also participated in study design, data management, and statistical analysis. Its result does not show that free-testosterone-guided treatment is ineffective in general: it tested a particular formulation and population, not competing diagnostic strategies. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19211706/))

### The decisive comparison is missing from these studies

The treatment evidence reviewed here did not include a randomized trial that assigned otherwise comparable patients to a **total-testosterone-only evaluation** versus a **total-plus-free-testosterone evaluation**, then compared symptoms, unnecessary treatment, adverse effects, and costs. ([Hudson et al.](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/); [Cunningham et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/101/8/3096/2835036); [Pencina et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/109/2/569/7244351); [Legros et al.](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article-pdf/160/5/821/45870227/821.pdf))

Taken together, the studies above establish symptom associations, analytical differences, and treatment effects within selected populations. They do not establish that routinely adding free testosterone improves patient outcomes, nor do they establish that doing so is wasted expense. Evidence from these studies is particularly limited for treatment decisions in men with **repeatedly normal total testosterone but isolated low free testosterone**. ([Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146); [Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600); [Hudson et al.](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/); [Legros et al.](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article-pdf/160/5/821/45870227/821.pdf))

## How the four claims compare

|Claim|Assessment|
|---|---|
|**Free testosterone should be the standard assessment.**|Supported as an important adjunct when SHBG or borderline total testosterone complicates interpretation. Universal replacement of total testosterone is not established by these studies. ([Endocrine Society guideline](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465); [Hudson et al.](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/))|
|**Total testosterone is sufficient for most decisions.**|Recommended as the initial biochemical assessment, but that does not establish sufficiency for most decisions. Some discordant cases require further assessment; minority frequency alone does not establish poor value. ([Endocrine Society guideline](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465); [Antonio et al.](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146))|
|**Measurement problems undermine free testosterone.**|Concerns are well supported for analog assays and for transferring cutoffs between methods. Too broad when applied to validated calculations or well-performed equilibrium dialysis. ([Fritz et al.](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/54/3/512/5628433); [Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600); [Walravens et al.](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01KAGX7T02GSSKRKN8Y1Y0RE51/file/01KAGX8XMGYNKD12EY7CQ23ZTZ.pdf))|
|**Bioavailable testosterone is more clinically relevant.**|Physiologically plausible, but superiority over free testosterone for diagnosis or treatment selection is not established by the cited studies. ([Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600); [Liang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33715769/))|

These judgments synthesize the cited studies and distinguish diagnostic association from measurement validity and treatment benefit; the evidence is not equally strong across those three questions. ([Enlighten Publications](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/163468/7/163468.pdf); [Fiers et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/6/2167/4956600); [Hudson et al.](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/))

## Practical interpretation

For an adult man being evaluated for testosterone deficiency, the evidence-supported approach is to begin with symptoms or signs and a reliable morning fasting total-testosterone measurement, and confirm a low result on a separate fasting morning. Avoid diagnostic testing during acute illness or recovery from it. When total testosterone is borderline or SHBG is likely abnormal, measure SHBG and calculate free testosterone using an appropriate formula with the required testosterone, SHBG, and albumin inputs, or use a reliable equilibrium-dialysis method. Interpretation should use the reference interval appropriate to that method. A confirmed diagnosis also requires evaluation of its cause. Treatment decisions should consider reversible contributors, fertility goals, contraindications, and monitoring. The Endocrine Society guideline recommends this selective approach; its panel disclosed relationships with testosterone manufacturers, but the guideline itself was funded solely by the Society. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465))

**Free testosterone matters most when it changes the interpretation of total testosterone. But a better explanation of the laboratory results is not, by itself, proof that testosterone treatment is indicated.** ([Endocrine Society guideline](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465); [Antonio et al.](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/373146))
