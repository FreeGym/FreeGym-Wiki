# What Testosterone Level Is Actually “Low”?

A testosterone result can be **low relative to healthy men of the same age** without necessarily establishing hypogonadism. Conversely, a result just above a guideline cutoff does not rule out clinically important androgen deficiency, especially when sex hormone-binding globulin changes make total testosterone misleading. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Antonio et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2015-4106))

The evidence therefore does not support either extreme: treating 300 ng/dL as a biological cliff, or assuming that every symptomatic man below an “optimal” target of 500 to 700 ng/dL will benefit from testosterone. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

## The 300 ng/dL cutoff is not universal

The Endocrine Society does **not** define hypogonadism using a universal cutoff of 300 ng/dL. Its guideline defines hypogonadism as compatible symptoms or signs plus unequivocally and consistently low total and/or free testosterone. For CDC-certified assays, it identifies **264 ng/dL** as the lower limit derived from harmonized measurements in healthy, non-obese young men. When assays are not harmonized, the guideline warns that the appropriate lower limit can vary substantially with the assay and reference population. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)

The **American Urological Association** is the organization that recommends using total testosterone below **300 ng/dL as a reasonable diagnostic cutoff**. It also requires two separate early-morning measurements and compatible symptoms or signs; the number alone is not sufficient. ([Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115)) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29601923/)

A 2026 JAMA review summarizes the current diagnostic range as approximately **264 to 300 ng/dL**, confirmed on at least two fasting samples collected between 7 and 10 am using an accurate, externally quality-controlled assay. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526)) [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2849760)

These are three different concepts:

- A **reference interval** describes the statistical distribution in a selected population. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))
- A **diagnostic threshold** helps identify a clinical syndrome when symptoms and biochemical findings agree. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))
- A **treatment target** is the level sought after therapy has already been judged appropriate. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))

For example, the AUA treatment target of approximately 450 to 600 ng/dL and the Endocrine Society's recommendation to aim for the mid-normal range are dosing targets for men with diagnosed hypogonadism. They are not diagnostic thresholds for untreated men. ([Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

## What healthy age-stratified data show

A major widely used normative dataset harmonized testosterone measurements across four US and European cohorts. It included 9,054 community-dwelling men, with age-specific modeling in 6,933 non-obese men. Measurements were standardized to the CDC reference method. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/4/1161/2884621)

### Model-based total testosterone percentiles in non-obese men

|Age|2.5th percentile|5th percentile|10th percentile|25th percentile|Median|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|19-39|267|304|344|424|531|
|40-49|235|273|310|386|481|
|50-59|219|256|297|374|477|
|60-69|218|254|296|374|477|
|70-79|218|252|292|372|477|

Values are total testosterone in ng/dL. The paper's primary pooled reference interval for healthy, non-obese men aged 19 to 39 was 264 to 916 ng/dL; the slightly different 267-ng/dL figure above comes from its age-specific percentile model. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/4/1161/2884621)

These data support part of the concern about using one threshold for everyone:

- A 28-year-old with testosterone of **310 ng/dL** is above the fifth percentile but below the tenth percentile among healthy, non-obese men aged 19 to 39. His result is low **relative to that selected age-matched reference distribution**. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))
- A 65-year-old at **310 ng/dL** is above the tenth percentile but below the twenty-fifth percentile for healthy, non-obese men aged 60 to 69. That is not average for his age, but it is less unusual than 310 in a younger man. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

The crucial limitation is that these percentiles describe the selected reference population; the study did not validate them as disease boundaries or treatment thresholds. Being in the lowest 5% or 10% therefore does not by itself establish that symptoms are caused by testosterone or that raising the level will improve health outcomes. Percentile estimates also depend on cohort selection, assay harmonization, and statistical modeling. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928))

## Why different healthy-population studies produce different lower limits

An earlier Framingham analysis of 456 healthy, non-obese men aged 19 to 40 reported a median total testosterone of **698.7 ng/dL** and a 2.5th percentile of **348.3 ng/dL**. Under that particular reference interval, a young man at 320 ng/dL would have been classified below the lower limit. ([Bhasin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2010-3012)) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21697255/)

The later four-cohort harmonization study reported a much lower healthy-young-adult boundary of 264 ng/dL. The difference illustrates how strongly a reference interval can depend on cohort selection, assay calibration, exclusions, pooling across populations, and statistical modeling. It does not prove that one result is the biologically correct threshold and the other is wrong. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Bhasin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2010-3012))

It is therefore defensible to say that **320 ng/dL would have been flagged under some carefully selected healthy-young reference intervals**. It is not established that 320 would universally have been flagged by clinical laboratories a generation ago, or that the difference between historical and current limits is entirely caused by declining population testosterone. ([Bhasin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2010-3012); [Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9))

## Proposed young-adult cutoffs around 350 to 410 ng/dL

A 2022 analysis of US men aged 20 to 44 proposed age-specific values based on the lower boundary of each age group's **middle tertile**. ([Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928))

- Ages 20-24: 409 ng/dL
- Ages 25-29: 413 ng/dL
- Ages 30-34: 359 ng/dL
- Ages 35-39: 352 ng/dL
- Ages 40-44: 350 ng/dL

([Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928)) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36282060/)

Interpreting these figures as age-specific deficiency cutoffs goes beyond the study. The lower boundary of the middle tertile is approximately the **33rd percentile**, not the 2.5th or 5th percentile used in the healthy-reference studies discussed above. The study also relied on a single testosterone measurement and did not establish that men below these values had a treatment-responsive syndrome. ([Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928); [Bhasin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2010-3012); [Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

These data can help answer, “Is this result relatively low for my age?” They cannot yet answer, “At what age-specific value does testosterone therapy produce more benefit than harm?” ([Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

## Age-stratified interpretation is supported more strongly than age-stratified treatment thresholds

Healthy-population data clearly show that the lower portion of the testosterone distribution changes with age. A young man at 310 should therefore not be dismissed simply because he is a few points above 300. Repeat testing, free testosterone assessment, and investigation of the cause are reasonable when symptoms are convincing. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))

What has not been established is that a 25-year-old below 400 or 410 should automatically be offered testosterone. The 2022 age-specific analysis did not validate the 33rd percentile, tenth percentile, age-specific median, or another age-stratified percentile as a treatment threshold, and current reviews do not identify randomized validation of such a cutoff in otherwise healthy young men. ([Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2))

The evidence therefore supports **age-aware clinical interpretation**, but not yet age-specific prescribing cutoffs. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

## Symptoms matter, but some symptoms matter more than others

Hypogonadism is a clinical syndrome, not an isolated laboratory result. Both major US guidelines require symptoms or signs in addition to consistently low testosterone. An asymptomatic man with a repeated value of 280 ng/dL may warrant evaluation for an underlying testicular, pituitary, medication-related, nutritional, or systemic cause, but he does not automatically have symptomatic hypogonadism requiring treatment. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))

At the same time, symptoms do not override the biochemical evidence. Fatigue, depressed mood, impaired concentration, weight gain, loss of fitness, sleepiness, and reduced muscle performance are nonspecific and can accompany depression, sleep disorders, obesity, chronic illness, medication use, overtraining, undernutrition, and deconditioning. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

The European Male Ageing Study found that three sexual symptoms - **reduced sexual desire, fewer morning erections, and erectile dysfunction** - had the clearest syndromic association with low testosterone. The investigators proposed late-onset hypogonadism as the presence of all three symptoms together with total testosterone below approximately 317 ng/dL and free testosterone below 220 pmol/L. Several physical and psychological symptoms were statistically associated with testosterone but did not form an equally specific syndrome. The study involved men aged 40 to 79, so the diagnostic performance of this symptom combination should not be assumed to be identical in younger men. ([Wu et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911101)) [University of Manchester](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/identification-of-late-onset-hypogonadism-in-middle-aged-and-elde/)

A systematic review similarly found that individual symptoms and physical findings generally had weak diagnostic power. Reduced libido and erectile dysfunction modestly increased the likelihood of low testosterone, but neither symptom could reliably distinguish testosterone deficiency from other causes. ([Millar et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.150262)) [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5026531/)

Thus, a man at 350 ng/dL with major libido loss and disappearance of spontaneous erections deserves a more focused androgen evaluation than a man at the same level whose only complaint is afternoon fatigue. Neither presentation, however, proves that testosterone therapy will help. ([Wu et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911101); [Millar et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.150262))

## Free testosterone can change the interpretation

Total testosterone is strongly influenced by sex hormone-binding globulin, or SHBG. Obesity and type 2 diabetes commonly lower SHBG, which can produce a low total testosterone result while free testosterone remains adequate. Aging and some other conditions can raise SHBG, allowing total testosterone to appear normal even though free testosterone is low. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)

In the European Male Ageing Study, men with normal total testosterone but low calculated free testosterone were older, had higher SHBG, and reported more sexual and physical symptoms. Men with low total testosterone but normal free testosterone tended to be younger and more obese and did not show the same androgen-deficiency phenotype. This was an observational association, not a treatment trial, but it demonstrates why total testosterone alone can misclassify men near the lower boundary. This analysis also involved men aged 40 to 79 and does not directly validate the same phenotype in younger men. ([Antonio et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2015-4106)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/101/7/2647/2810108)

The Endocrine Society recommends free testosterone assessment when total testosterone is near the lower limit - roughly the 200 to 400 ng/dL zone - or when a condition affecting SHBG is present. The preferred approaches are equilibrium dialysis or calculation from total testosterone, SHBG, and albumin using a validated formula. Direct analog free-testosterone immunoassays are considered inaccurate. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)

## What treatment studies show below 300 ng/dL

The Endocrine Society's commissioned review included placebo-controlled trials of symptomatic men with screening testosterone below 300 ng/dL. Mean baseline concentrations were much lower, approximately **201 to 239 ng/dL**. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

Treatment produced small improvements in libido, erectile function, sexual activity, and sexual satisfaction. It did not significantly improve energy or mood. Testosterone also substantially increased the risk of erythrocytosis, defined as hematocrit above 54%. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)

This supports treatment for appropriately diagnosed hypogonadism, but it should not be extrapolated directly to a man at 380 or 450 ng/dL. The participants generally started well below the low-normal range being debated. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

## Treatment evidence from trials enrolling men below approximately 346 ng/dL

A 2023 individual-participant-data meta-analysis included trials enrolling men with baseline total testosterone below 12 nmol/L, approximately **346 ng/dL**. Across 35 studies there were 5,601 participants; the individual-level analysis included 3,431 men with a median age of 67, and 97% were at least 40 years old. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1)) [University of Southern Denmark](https://portal.findresearcher.sdu.dk/en/publications/symptomatic-benefits-of-testosterone-treatment-in-patient-subgrou/)

Testosterone improved the overall 15-item International Index of Erectile Function by 5.52 points and the erectile-function subscore by 2.14 points. The latter reached the reported minimal clinically important difference for men with mild erectile dysfunction. Effects were not clearly modified by age, obesity, diabetes, or baseline testosterone within the included low-testosterone range. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1))

This means that the claim “testosterone never helps above 300” is too categorical. However, 12 nmol/L was an eligibility ceiling for the contributing trials, not an experimentally established treatment threshold. The analysis did not isolate men with baseline concentrations specifically between 300 and approximately 346 ng/dL, and failure to detect an interaction with baseline testosterone does not prove equal benefit throughout that range. Evidence of benefit specifically in the 300 to 346 ng/dL subgroup therefore remains indirect. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1))

The analysis does not establish benefits for young men at 350 to 500 ng/dL, nor does it establish improvements in longevity, cardiovascular events, fractures, work performance, or other hard outcomes. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1))

## Why broader reviews sometimes find little benefit

A 2024 Cochrane review included 43 randomized trials and 11,419 men aged 40 or older with sexual dysfunction. Across six erectile-function trials, testosterone improved the erectile-function score by 2.37 points, below the review's prespecified four-point threshold for a clinically important improvement. It also found little or no meaningful difference in sexual quality of life or short-term cardiovascular mortality, although cardiovascular deaths were very uncommon. ([Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2)) [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10788910/)

The Cochrane review and the Hudson meta-analysis are not necessarily contradictory. Hudson required baseline testosterone below approximately 346 ng/dL, whereas the Cochrane review addressed sexual dysfunction more broadly, did not require the same biochemical eligibility ceiling, and excluded classical primary or secondary hypogonadism. Including men whose sexual dysfunction was not androgen-related may partly have diluted the treatment effect. Differences in eligibility criteria, outcome instruments, included trials, follow-up, and statistical methods may also contribute. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2))

Together, the reviews suggest that treatment effects become less reliable as the population moves from confirmed biochemical deficiency toward sexual symptoms with normal or unselected testosterone. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2))

## What happens when low-normal testosterone is pushed to 500-900 ng/dL?

The TEAAM randomized trial is especially relevant to the “optimization” claim. It enrolled 308 men aged 60 or older with total testosterone between 100 and 400 ng/dL or free testosterone below 50 pg/mL. Mean baseline total testosterone was approximately 307 ng/dL. Testosterone gel was adjusted to produce levels between **500 and 900 ng/dL** and compared with placebo for three years. ([Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881)) [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2425744)

Despite reaching that higher range, testosterone did not significantly improve sexual desire, erectile function, overall sexual function, partner intimacy, or health-related quality of life. Hematocrit and prostate-specific antigen increased more in the testosterone group. ([Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881)) [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2425744)

The trial has important limitations: it studied older men, sexual outcomes were secondary, and participants were not selected for a clearly defined symptomatic hypogonadal syndrome. It therefore cannot show that no individual man at 320 or 350 will benefit. But it directly challenges the assumption that moving a low-normal level into the 500-900 range predictably improves well-being. ([Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881))

The investigator-initiated study was supported by Solvay Pharmaceuticals and later AbbVie, by the Aurora Foundation, and by grants to the Boston Older Americans Independence Center and Boston University's Clinical and Translational Science Institute. Solvay and AbbVie also supplied testosterone and placebo gel. The investigators reported that the pharmaceutical sponsor and the Aurora Foundation had no role in study design, data collection or interpretation, manuscript preparation, or publication decisions. The industry involvement should be noted, although the trial's largely negative findings do not favor the sponsor's product. ([Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881)) [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2425744)

## What the largest cardiovascular safety trial adds

The TRAVERSE trial randomized 5,246 men aged 45 to 80 who had symptoms of hypogonadism, preexisting cardiovascular disease or a high cardiovascular risk, and two fasting testosterone concentrations below 300 ng/dL. Testosterone gel was adjusted to maintain concentrations between 350 and 750 ng/dL. Over a mean follow-up of 33 months, a major adverse cardiovascular event occurred in 7.0% of the testosterone group and 7.3% of the placebo group. Testosterone was noninferior to placebo for the primary cardiovascular safety outcome. Atrial fibrillation, acute kidney injury, and pulmonary embolism were observed more often in the testosterone group. ([Lincoff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2215025)) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37326322/)

A prespecified fracture substudy found clinical fractures in 3.50% of men assigned to testosterone and 2.46% assigned to placebo, with a hazard ratio of 1.43 (95% CI, 1.04 to 1.97). Testosterone did not reduce fractures in this population, and fracture incidence was higher in the testosterone group. These findings provide useful medium-term safety evidence in selected middle-aged and older men with confirmed low testosterone and high cardiovascular risk. They do not establish lifetime safety, justify treatment in young men, or validate prescribing at untreated concentrations of 350 to 500 ng/dL. ([Snyder et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2308836)) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38231621/)

## The major evidence gap: symptomatic young men at 350-500 ng/dL

The available randomized evidence summarized in the 2026 JAMA review and recent systematic reviews does not directly establish benefit for young or middle-aged men who have all of the following. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2))

1. Repeated fasting morning total testosterone between 350 and 500 ng/dL;
2. Relevant symptoms;
3. Appropriate free-testosterone and etiologic evaluation; and
4. Randomization to testosterone or placebo with clinically important long-term outcomes.

The available trials largely involve classical hypogonadism, substantially lower baseline testosterone, older men, obesity-associated functional suppression, or broad sexual-dysfunction populations. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2))

Consequently, there is no solid evidentiary basis for routinely treating symptomatic men throughout the 350 to 500 ng/dL range or for promising that raising them to 500 to 700 ng/dL will improve energy, mood, body composition, cognition, cardiovascular health, or longevity. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2); [Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881))

That is not proof that benefit is impossible. It means the “optimization” strategy is being offered ahead of outcome-validating trials. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

## Have population testosterone levels declined?

A 2025 systematic review and meta-regression analyzed 1,256 papers, 1,504 study groups, and more than one million ostensibly healthy men studied between 1971 and July 2024. Testosterone declined with later year of measurement, and the association persisted after adjustment for sample size, age, BMI, and assay method. LH also declined, while BMI did not show a temporal trend in the included populations. ([Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9)) [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9)

This supports the possibility of a genuine secular decline. However, it was an aggregate meta-regression across heterogeneous studies, not one repeatedly sampled population measured for decades with an unchanged assay and protocol. It cannot establish how much of the trend reflects biology, unmeasured population differences, sampling, laboratory methods, geography, or publication practices. ([Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9))

Most importantly, evidence of declining population testosterone does not by itself identify the level at which treatment becomes beneficial. ([Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

## Are laboratories using poor reference populations?

This concern is partly justified. The Endocrine Society explicitly warns that local reference limits can vary substantially with the assay and population and may not accurately identify hypogonadism. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)

A 2025 survey of 124 laboratories in 27 European countries found that fewer than half consistently recommended appropriate morning and/or fasting collection, only about one quarter used mass spectrometry for total testosterone, and reported reference limits varied considerably. Age stratification was commonly preferred, but differences in methods and limits hindered comparison between laboratories. ([Narinx et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1515/cclm-2024-1237)) [Ghent University Academic Bibliography](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01JQV9KVY84R31HB13XBGDZ93A)

That supports better standardization, assay-specific interpretation, and carefully selected reference cohorts. It does not demonstrate that laboratories have deliberately lowered their ranges in response to declining population averages. ([Narinx et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1515/cclm-2024-1237); [Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9))

The blanket claim that current lower limits are all derived from obese, sick, medicated men is also incorrect. The influential 2011 and 2017 reference studies explicitly used healthy, non-obese young men and standardized or mass-spectrometry-based measurements. ([Bhasin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2010-3012); [Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

A more defensible conclusion is that **some local ranges may be poorly constructed or poorly transferable**, while the best modern harmonized ranges already attempt to solve exactly that problem. ([Narinx et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1515/cclm-2024-1237); [Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

## How the four competing claims hold up

### 1. “The 300 ng/dL cutoff is too low for younger men”

**Partly supported.** ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928))

A testosterone concentration of 300 to 350 ng/dL is much more unusual in a healthy man under 40 than in an older man. Age-stratified data should influence how seriously a borderline result is investigated. A 28-year-old at 310 should not be told that his result is unremarkable merely because it exceeds 300. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))

What remains unproven is that age-specific percentiles such as 350, 400, or 413 ng/dL are valid treatment thresholds. Age stratification currently provides context, not an outcome-validated mandate to prescribe. ([Zhu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000002928); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

### 2. “Symptoms should matter as much as the number”

**Supported, with an important limit.** ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Wu et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911101))

Symptoms are necessary to diagnose the clinical syndrome, but they are not interchangeable. Sexual symptoms - especially reduced desire and loss of spontaneous or morning erections - have a stronger relationship with low testosterone than fatigue, mood, motivation, or body-composition complaints. ([Wu et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911101); [Millar et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.150262))

Symptoms justify careful evaluation when testosterone is borderline. They do not establish testosterone causation when repeated total and appropriately measured free testosterone are normal. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Antonio et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2015-4106))

### 3. “The evidence does not support treating symptomatic men at 300-500 ng/dL”

**Mostly supported above approximately 350 ng/dL, but too categorical across the whole range.** ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

Trials enrolling men below approximately 346 ng/dL found a modest average improvement in sexual function, based mainly on studies of older men. Because those analyses did not isolate men with baseline concentrations specifically between 300 and 346 ng/dL, evidence for that subgroup remains indirect. Evidence becomes much weaker for the 350 to 500 range, particularly in young men and for nonsexual symptoms. ([Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2))

There is no good evidence for routinely treating that entire range or for targeting 500 to 700 ng/dL as a universal optimum. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526); [Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881))

### 4. “Declining reference ranges are themselves the problem”

**Partly supported.** ([Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9); [Narinx et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1515/cclm-2024-1237))

Secular testosterone decline, assay variation, poor pre-analytical practices, and inconsistent laboratory ranges are real concerns. Healthy, non-obese, age-aware reference data are preferable to indiscriminate tested-population data. ([Santi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40618-025-02671-9); [Narinx et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1515/cclm-2024-1237); [Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

But the claim that modern reference limits have simply been widened to normalize a sick population is not established. High-quality modern studies have used healthy, non-obese cohorts, and discrepancies between their ranges show that calibration and study design matter in addition to population health. ([Bhasin et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2010-3012); [Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

## Quick reference values

- **AUA diagnostic cutoff:** total testosterone below 300 ng/dL, based on two early-morning tests plus compatible symptoms or signs. ([Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115)) [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29601923/)
- **Endocrine Society harmonized lower limit:** 264 ng/dL for CDC-certified assays, again requiring compatible symptoms and consistently low results. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)
- **Healthy non-obese men aged 19-39:** median 531 ng/dL; fifth percentile 304; tenth percentile 344 in the four-cohort harmonized model. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/4/1161/2884621)
- **Healthy non-obese men aged 60-69:** median 477 ng/dL; fifth percentile 254; tenth percentile 296. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/4/1161/2884621)
- **Borderline zone:** approximately 200 to 400 ng/dL, where SHBG and free testosterone are particularly important. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229)) [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/5/1715/4939465)
- **A treatment target of 450-600 or “mid-normal”** applies after hypogonadism has been diagnosed; it is not a universal untreated optimum. ([Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

## Practical interpretation

A defensible evaluation of a borderline result includes:

1. **Repeat total testosterone on two separate mornings**, preferably fasting between 7 and 10 am, using an accurate, externally quality-controlled assay. Testing should not be performed during acute illness or recovery from acute illness, or during short-term use of medications that suppress testosterone. Day-to-day variation is substantial, and approximately 30% of men with an initial result in the hypogonadal range have a normal result on repeat measurement. ([Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

2. **Assess SHBG and free testosterone** when total testosterone is near the lower limit or when obesity, diabetes, aging, liver or thyroid disease, certain medications, or other SHBG-altering conditions are present. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229))

3. **Investigate the cause rather than treating the number alone.** LH and FSH help distinguish primary testicular failure from hypothalamic or pituitary suppression. Depending on the pattern, evaluation may include prolactin, iron status, pituitary assessment, medication review, prior anabolic-steroid use, nutritional status, obesity, sleep disorders, and systemic illness. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

4. **Address potentially reversible suppression first.** Obesity, severe illness, opioids, corticosteroids, nutritional deficiency, excessive exercise, and some sleep disorders can suppress the hypothalamic-pituitary-testicular axis. Weight loss is recommended as first-line treatment for obesity-associated functional hypogonadism. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

5. **Discuss fertility before testosterone.** Exogenous testosterone suppresses gonadotropins and sperm production and should not be started in a man seeking fertility in the near term. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))

6. **Judge treatment by symptoms as well as laboratory normalization.** The Endocrine Society recommends evaluating treatment response and adverse effects after initiation, and the AUA advises discussing cessation when testosterone normalizes but symptoms or signs do not improve. Testosterone treatment also requires monitoring of hematocrit and other formulation- and patient-specific risks. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))


## Bottom line

There is no single testosterone number that separates every healthy man from every hypogonadal man. Diagnosis requires clinical and biochemical concordance rather than one isolated value. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115))

A total testosterone of **310 ng/dL in a 28-year-old is low relative to the harmonized distribution in healthy, non-obese men aged 19 to 39** and deserves careful confirmation and investigation. A value of 310 in a 65-year-old is less unusual but remains in the lower part of the healthy non-obese distribution; it is not necessarily “average for age.” ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935))

Neither result alone proves a treatment-responsive deficiency. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

The best-supported diagnosis still requires the following elements. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

- Relevant symptoms or signs;
- At least two properly collected morning measurements;
- Free-testosterone assessment when total testosterone or SHBG makes the result ambiguous; and
- Evaluation of the underlying cause.

([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Mulhall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115); [Anawalt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.8526))

Evidence supports modest sexual benefits in selected men with clearly low testosterone, especially at concentrations below 300 ng/dL. Trials enrolling men below approximately 346 ng/dL suggest that benefit above 300 is possible, mainly in older men, but they do not establish efficacy specifically for the 300 to 346 ng/dL subgroup. The evidence does **not** currently support treating all symptomatic men at 350 to 500 ng/dL or routinely pushing them to an “optimal” 500 to 700-plus range. ([Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1); [Lee et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013071.pub2); [Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881))

The central distinction is therefore not simply **low versus normal**. It is **low for age**, **biochemically reliable**, **clinically concordant**, and **shown to be treatment-responsive**. ([Travison et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2016-2935); [Bhasin et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229); [Hudson et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-7568\(23\)00169-1))

Solvay and AbbVie supplied the study gels. The authors reported that the pharmaceutical sponsor and the Aurora Foundation had no role in the trial's design, conduct, analysis, or publication. This financial relationship should be kept in view, although the trial did not produce a commercially favorable result for most symptomatic outcomes. ([Basaria et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.8881)) [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2425744)

Their cardiovascular and fracture findings should therefore be interpreted alongside the trial design, prespecified adjudication, and full conflict-of-interest disclosures.
