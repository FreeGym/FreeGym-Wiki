# Is TSH Alone Enough to Assess Thyroid Function?

TSH is usually sufficient for a narrow but important question: **does a stable, untreated person with an intact hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis probably have primary thyroid dysfunction?** TSH is the preferred first-line biochemical test for primary thyroid dysfunction when secondary thyroid disease is not suspected. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))

It is not sufficient for every thyroid-related question. TSH can miss central hypothyroidism, requires pregnancy-specific interpretation, can be misleading during illness, medication effects, or rapid changes in thyroid status, and does not detect thyroid autoimmunity before hormone production changes. At the same time, the cited clinical guidance uses stepwise testing rather than recommending TSH, free T4, free T3, TPO antibodies, and thyroglobulin antibodies for everyone. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([American Thyroid Association 2026 pregnancy guideline](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10507256261445624)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

The best-supported approach is therefore **TSH-first, with additional tests selected according to the clinical question rather than a universal "full thyroid panel."** ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))

## Why TSH works so well for primary thyroid disease

The relationship between TSH and free T4 is approximately inverse log-linear: a relatively small change in free T4 can produce a much larger change in TSH. This makes TSH a sensitive early marker of primary hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism, often becoming abnormal while free T4 remains within its population reference interval. The 2023 American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review therefore describes TSH as the best initial test for primary hypothyroidism. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

But TSH is a valid summary of thyroid status only when the feedback loop is intact. It may also take weeks or months to fully reflect an acute change in thyroid status. Assay interference, pregnancy, intercurrent illness, medications, age, iodine status, biological variation, and the specific laboratory method can further affect interpretation. When results are physiologically inconsistent or discordant with the clinical picture, analytical interference and platform-specific bias should be considered. A normal TSH is therefore strong evidence against primary thyroid failure in the appropriate setting, not proof that every part of the thyroid axis is normal. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

## The clearest exception: central hypothyroidism

Central hypothyroidism results from pituitary or hypothalamic dysfunction rather than failure of the thyroid gland itself. The typical biochemical pattern is a low free T4 accompanied by a TSH that is low, normal, or only mildly elevated. The TSH is "inappropriately normal" because it should be substantially elevated in response to the low free T4. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388))

TSH alone cannot detect this pattern. In some cases, the measured TSH may also have reduced biological activity despite being detectable by the laboratory assay. When central hypothyroidism is clinically plausible, free T4 is therefore essential. The diagnosis should generally be confirmed by repeat low free T4 with an inappropriately low or normal TSH on at least two determinations, after relevant illness, medication effects, and assay-related explanations have been considered. Other pituitary axes, particularly adrenal function, should be assessed before thyroid-hormone treatment is started. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388))

### How often is central hypothyroidism found?

The available evidence reviewed here comes mainly from laboratory-selected populations, not from cohorts defined simply by symptoms and a normal TSH. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

A 2008 prospective audit evaluated 73,650 thyroid-function results and identified 266 adults with low free T4 and a TSH below 10 mU/L. Reflective pituitary testing led to 10 new diagnoses of hypopituitarism, approximately four cases per 100,000 adults per year. Only one of the 10 would have been identified through a TSH-only strategy. However, most initially discordant results were excluded because of non-thyroidal illness, thyroid treatment, assay imprecision, or normalization on repeat testing. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100))

A 2024 study using the All-Wales laboratory information system identified 15 new cases of central hypothyroidism in two participating health boards, corresponding to approximately two cases per 100,000 people per year. Low free T4 appeared in approximately 0.5% of primary-care thyroid tests, with assay-related differences in detection rates, but its positive predictive value for central hypothyroidism was only 2% to 4%. In other words, measuring free T4 did detect otherwise missed cases, but most low-free-T4 results did not represent central hypothyroidism. ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/))

A 2025 primary-care reflex-testing study examined 41,487 thyroid-test requests. Fifty-four results, or 0.13%, showed low free T4 with a normal TSH, and three cases of hypopituitarism were identified. There were 129 additional tests per diagnosis, while 74% of the discordant patterns had a plausible alternative explanation such as medication use, known thyroid dysfunction, non-thyroidal illness, or pregnancy. ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

### The important evidence gap

These studies do **not** establish the prevalence of central hypothyroidism among people selected only because they have fatigue, weight change, hair loss, cold intolerance, constipation, or similar symptoms despite a normal TSH. They began with a low free T4 or another abnormal laboratory pattern. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

The cited literature does not provide a reliable estimate for "symptomatic patients with normal TSH." Central hypothyroidism is real and clinically important, but the cited service studies do not show that symptoms alone define a group with a known high prevalence. Suspicion should be driven especially by pituitary or hypothalamic disease, other pituitary hormone abnormalities, relevant treatment history, or a laboratory pattern inconsistent with primary thyroid physiology. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

## Should free T4 be measured in everyone?

Adding free T4 to every initial TSH test would identify some central hypothyroidism, but the service studies also found many low-free-T4 or otherwise discordant results that had alternative explanations, normalized on repeat testing, or did not represent central hypothyroidism. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

A UK Markov-model analysis estimated that adding free T4 to every adult primary-care thyroid request would cost approximately £71,437 per quality-adjusted life-year gained. Under that model, the strategy would cross a £20,000-per-QALY threshold only if the cost of free T4 fell to about £0.61 per test. The result is specific to the costs and assumptions used in that health system, but it argues against assuming that universal TSH-plus-free-T4 testing is automatically cost-effective. ([UK cost-utility analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6677967/))

The newer diagnostic-yield studies reinforce the trade-off. Additional free T4 testing can uncover rare pituitary disease, but most low-free-T4/normal-TSH combinations in these studies had other explanations. Taken together, the cited service studies and cost-effectiveness model are more consistent with **targeted or reflex free T4 testing** than with either TSH-only under all circumstances or free T4 for every person regardless of context. This is an inference from diagnostic-yield studies and model assumptions rather than randomized evidence of patient-important outcomes. ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891)) ([UK cost-utility analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6677967/))

Free T4 is particularly important when:

- Central hypothyroidism or other pituitary dysfunction is plausible. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))

- Thyroid results are discordant with a convincing clinical context. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- The patient is pregnant, because pregnancy-specific physiology and assay effects require trimester-, laboratory-, and method-specific interpretation. Maternal TSH remains the best marker of maternal thyroid status, while free T4 can add information but is itself assay-sensitive. ([American Thyroid Association 2026 pregnancy guideline](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10507256261445624))

- The patient is significantly ill or taking drugs that alter TSH secretion or thyroid-hormone metabolism. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- Thyroid status has recently changed or treatment has recently started, because TSH may lag behind free T4. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

- An abnormal TSH needs to be classified as overt or subclinical disease. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))


## Free T3 and the "poor converter" hypothesis

The biological premise behind the conversion hypothesis is correct: the thyroid secretes mainly T4, while approximately 80% of circulating T3 is generated outside the thyroid through deiodination of T4. Deiodinase activity can be altered by illness, nutritional status, medications, and other physiological factors. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

The diagnostic conclusion often drawn from that physiology is much less secure. The cited assay review, non-thyroidal illness review, treatment meta-analyses, and genetic study do not establish a single, validated disorder in which an isolated low T3 identifies patients who predictably benefit from T3 treatment. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4)) ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([type-2 deiodinase genetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16144953/))

Free T3 immunoassays are generally less precise and reproducible than free T4 assays. T3 can remain within its reference interval even in genuine primary hypothyroidism, making it a poor test for ruling hypothyroidism in or out. When T3 measurement is clinically indicated, total T3 is generally preferred to free T3. Total T3 is most useful when TSH is suppressed and T3-predominant hyperthyroidism is being considered. Reverse T3 has not been validated as a routine method for diagnosing impaired conversion or directing T3 treatment. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

The cited literature does not establish a widely accepted, outcome-validated definition of a common disorder characterized by all of the following features: normal TSH, normal free T4, isolated low free or total T3, symptoms attributed specifically to inadequate conversion, and predictable improvement when T3 is administered. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4)) ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6)) ([type-2 deiodinase genetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16144953/))

An isolated low T3 result therefore does not establish that peripheral tissues are hypothyroid or that T3 treatment will improve symptoms. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/))

## A more nuanced issue in levothyroxine-treated patients

The conversion question is more directly relevant in people taking levothyroxine than in untreated people with normal TSH and free T4. A person without substantial endogenous thyroid function must generate most circulating T3 from administered T4. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 systematic review and meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/12/4533/5074280))

A systematic review of 99 studies found that levothyroxine-treated patients with normal TSH had, on average, LDL cholesterol approximately 3.3 mg/dL higher and total cholesterol approximately 9.6 mg/dL higher than healthy control groups included in the contributing studies. The groups were not uniformly matched, and residual confounding was possible. The review concluded that normalization of TSH did not necessarily normalize every measured marker of thyroid-hormone signaling. This supports the possibility of biochemical differences, but it does not prove that lower T3 caused the differences or that adding T3 would improve clinical outcomes. ([2018 systematic review and meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/12/4533/5074280))

Clinical trials are less supportive of routine T3 addition. A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of 18 studies involving 1,563 participants found no consistent advantage of T4/T3 combination therapy over levothyroxine alone for clinical status, quality of life, psychological distress, depression, or fatigue. Forty-three percent preferred combination therapy, compared with 23% preferring levothyroxine and 30% reporting no preference, but the certainty of evidence was low to moderate. ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/))

A 2024 meta-analysis of 16 randomized trials reported the expected increase in T3 and decrease in T4 with combination therapy and no significant differences in lipid measurements, heart rate, TSQ-36 scores, or BDI depression scores. However, its abstract and main results section report incompatible GHQ-28 effect estimates and contradictory interpretations of statistical significance. The GHQ-28 result should therefore not be relied upon without a correction or independent reconstruction. Heterogeneity among the included trials was moderate to high. ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6))

A trial-linked genetic study also found that two commonly discussed type-2 deiodinase polymorphisms did not predict well-being, neurocognitive function, or preference for combination treatment in 141 adequately treated patients with autoimmune hypothyroidism. That does not exclude every possible genetically defined subgroup, but it does not support using these variants as established clinical "poor converter" tests. ([type-2 deiodinase genetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16144953/))

The evidence therefore supports two conclusions at once:

1. TSH normalization may not reproduce every biochemical feature seen in people with an intact thyroid. ([2018 systematic review and meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/12/4533/5074280))

2. Existing trials have not demonstrated a consistent symptomatic advantage from routinely adding T3. ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6))


A responsive subgroup may exist, but it has not yet been reliably defined by serum T3, symptoms, or the deiodinase variants examined in the cited study. ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6)) ([type-2 deiodinase genetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16144953/))

## What an isolated low T3 more commonly reflects

Low T3 is a characteristic feature of non-thyroidal illness syndrome. During significant illness, inflammation, altered deiodinase activity, medications, stress-hormone changes, and reduced food intake can lower T3 without the compensatory TSH elevation expected in primary hypothyroidism. ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4))

Caloric intake itself can influence the result. In a randomized study of 35 critically ill patients, those assigned to receive 100% rather than 40% of calculated caloric needs had a 54% higher T3-to-reverse-T3 ratio on the first post-intervention day, driven partly by a 23% higher T3 concentration. The difference diminished over the following days. This illustrates that T3 can move in response to energy exposure independently of primary thyroid failure. ([randomized caloric-exposure study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31581295/))

Non-thyroidal illness, medication effects, and reduced caloric intake are established alternative explanations for low T3 and should be evaluated before the result is attributed to an independent thyroid conversion disorder. However, much of this evidence comes from hospitalized or critically ill populations. It demonstrates that T3 is context-sensitive; it does not prove that every low T3 in an ambulatory patient is harmless. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4)) ([randomized caloric-exposure study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31581295/))

## TPO antibodies: current function versus future risk

Thyroid antibodies answer a different question from TSH or free T4. They indicate thyroid autoimmunity and help estimate future risk; they do not directly measure whether hormone production is currently adequate. ([NHANES III](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11836274/)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

In NHANES III, conducted in the United States from 1988 to 1994 using the assays and thresholds of that period, TPO antibodies were present in 11.3% of the population and thyroglobulin antibodies in 10.4%. Antibodies were more common in women and increased with age. TPO antibodies were associated with thyroid dysfunction, whereas thyroglobulin antibodies were not significantly associated with hypo- or hyperthyroidism in that analysis. ([NHANES III](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11836274/))

The 20-year Whickham follow-up showed that antibodies predicted future hypothyroidism. Among women, the odds ratio was approximately 8 for positive antibodies alone, 8 for an elevated TSH alone, and 38 when both were present. Risk increased progressively from TSH values around 2 mU/L and was further amplified by antibody positivity. This describes a continuous epidemiological risk gradient, not a diagnostic threshold, upper reference limit, or treatment cutoff. ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/))

The Tehran Thyroid Study also found that TPO-positive euthyroid adults had a substantially higher risk of later thyroid dysfunction, especially hypothyroidism. Nevertheless, most TPO-positive euthyroid participants remained euthyroid during each reported follow-up interval. A positive antibody test identifies increased probability, not inevitable progression. ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

In a more selected cohort of 790 euthyroid women with relatives affected by autoimmune thyroid disease, 51 thyroid events were observed, including 38 hypothyroid and 13 hyperthyroid events. The crude observed proportion was 6.5%, while the reported Kaplan-Meier estimated five-year cumulative event rate was 7.5%. Baseline TSH, TPO-antibody concentration, and the number of affected relatives all contributed to risk. ([Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/770358))

## Should everyone have TPO antibodies tested once?

The cited cohort evidence supports TPO antibodies as a **risk-stratification and etiological test**, but those studies did not evaluate whether universal once-only antibody testing improves symptoms, prevents complications, or is cost-effective. ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283)) ([Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/770358))

Testing is most directly supported when the result would clarify etiology or follow-up after a TSH above the reference range. NICE recommends considering TPO antibodies in adults with TSH above the reference range and advises against repeated TPO-antibody testing. Population and cohort studies show higher antibody prevalence in women and greater future risk with positivity, but these observations do not establish net clinical benefit from screening every woman or every asymptomatic person. ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([NHANES III](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11836274/)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

Family-history evidence is also more nuanced than it first appears. Family background contributed to risk in the selected Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort, but family history by itself was not independently associated with future hypothyroidism in the population-based Whickham analysis. ([Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/770358)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/))

A positive TPO result with normal TSH identifies increased future risk and may therefore justify closer biochemical follow-up when clinically appropriate. It does not demonstrate current hormone deficiency, and the cited cohort studies did not test whether thyroid-hormone treatment benefits euthyroid antibody-positive people. ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283)) ([Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/770358))

Routine addition of thyroglobulin antibodies has less support for predicting thyroid dysfunction in a general population. In NHANES III, thyroglobulin antibodies were not significantly associated with hypo- or hyperthyroidism after analysis, whereas TPO antibodies were associated with thyroid dysfunction. That cross-sectional result does not establish a benefit from routinely adding thyroglobulin-antibody testing. ([NHANES III](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11836274/))

## How the competing claims hold up

### 1. "TSH alone is sufficient for initial screening in the general population."

**Supported as a test-selection statement, not as a justification for universal population screening.** ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([US Preventive Services Task Force recommendation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/thyroid-dysfunction-screening))

When thyroid testing is clinically indicated, TSH is the best single first-line test for primary thyroid dysfunction in a stable person with an intact pituitary-thyroid axis. The ATA review also documents that assay variation, illness, medications, biological variation, and physiological state can affect thyroid-test results, so an additional abnormal value is not automatically evidence of previously unrecognized thyroid disease. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

This is separate from the question of whether asymptomatic people should be screened at all. For nonpregnant, asymptomatic adults, the US Preventive Services Task Force concludes that the evidence is insufficient to determine the balance of benefits and harms of population screening, while identifying serum TSH as the primary biochemical screening test when screening is undertaken. ([US Preventive Services Task Force recommendation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/thyroid-dysfunction-screening))

The claim becomes incorrect when "sufficient" is interpreted to mean that normal TSH rules out central hypothyroidism or remains reliable in every physiological and clinical context. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

### 2. "A full panel should be standard, especially in symptomatic patients with normal TSH."

**The concern is valid, but the proposed solution is broader than the cited evidence supports.** ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))

Adding free T4 is justified when central hypothyroidism or another TSH-confounding condition is plausible. The cited sources do not establish a benefit from routinely adding free T3, TPO antibodies, and thyroglobulin antibodies to every evaluation solely because nonspecific symptoms are present. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))

The available service studies also do not provide a reliable diagnostic yield for a standardized full panel specifically among symptom-defined patients whose TSH is normal, because they selected patients from abnormal laboratory patterns or testing workflows rather than from that clinical population. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

### 3. "The poor-converter hypothesis is not well established."

**This is the best-supported interpretation of an isolated low-T3 result in the cited evidence.** ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4)) ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/))

Conversion biology is real, and some levothyroxine-treated patients have biochemical differences despite normal TSH. What remains unproven in the cited evidence is a common, independently diagnosable "poor converter" disorder defined by normal TSH and free T4, low T3, and predictable benefit from T3 treatment. Illness, caloric restriction, medications, and analytical limitations are better-established explanations for low T3. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 systematic review and meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/12/4533/5074280)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4)) ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([type-2 deiodinase genetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16144953/))

### 4. "TPO antibodies should be tested at least once."

**Reasonable as targeted risk stratification, but not established as a universal standard by the cited evidence.** ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

TPO antibodies are common and predict an increased probability of future hypothyroidism, particularly when TSH is also elevated or high-normal. In the reported follow-up intervals, most euthyroid antibody-positive participants in the Tehran cohort remained euthyroid. The cited cohort studies were designed to predict risk, not to test whether universal once-only screening improves patient-centered outcomes. ([NHANES III](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11836274/)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283)) ([Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/770358))

## Quick reference

|Clinical question|Most informative initial testing|
|---|---|
|Stable untreated adult; primary thyroid disease suspected; no pituitary concern|TSH, with free T4 added or reflexed if TSH is abnormal or the clinical context warrants it. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))|
|Known or suspected pituitary/hypothalamic disorder|TSH **and free T4**. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))|
|Symptoms strongly discordant with TSH or multiple pituitary features|Free T4 plus targeted pituitary evaluation; do not rely on TSH alone. ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))|
|Pregnancy|TSH interpreted with pregnancy-, laboratory-, and trimester-specific reference intervals; free T4 when clinically indicated, recognizing method-specific limitations. ([American Thyroid Association 2026 pregnancy guideline](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10507256261445624))|
|Suppressed TSH or suspected hyperthyroidism|Free T4; total T3 can add useful information. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))|
|Suspected primary hypothyroidism|TSH and, when indicated, free T4; T3 is usually not a useful screening test. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145))|
|Acute systemic illness or major caloric restriction|Interpret low T3 cautiously; defer or repeat testing after recovery when clinically appropriate. ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4)) ([randomized caloric-exposure study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31581295/))|
|Question of autoimmune cause or future risk|TPO antibodies, selectively, especially when TSH is above the reference range and the result would clarify etiology or follow-up. ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))|
|Routine "conversion" testing|Free T3 and reverse T3 are not validated as universal screening tools. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4))|

This hierarchy follows the distinction between initial evaluation for primary thyroid disease, detecting central disease, investigating hyperthyroidism, interpreting pregnancy-related physiology, and assessing autoimmune risk. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([American Thyroid Association 2026 pregnancy guideline](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10507256261445624)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/))

## Evidence gaps

The main unanswered questions are not whether TSH has limitations - it clearly does - but how much patient-important benefit is gained by expanding testing in particular populations. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388))

- The cited service studies do not provide a prospective estimate of central hypothyroidism prevalence in patients selected solely for thyroid-like symptoms and a normal TSH, because they began with abnormal laboratory patterns or testing workflows. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891))

- The cited assay review, treatment meta-analyses, and genetic study do not define a validated serum-T3 threshold, symptom profile, or genotype that reliably identifies a subgroup benefiting from T3. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6)) ([type-2 deiodinase genetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16144953/))

- Existing combination-therapy trials are heterogeneous and were not generally designed around a rigorously defined low-T3 subgroup. ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6))

- None of the cited service studies or the cited cost model evaluated patient outcomes or cost-effectiveness for a universal TSH-free T4-free T3-TPO-TgAb panel. ([2008 prospective audit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1258/acb.2007.007100)) ([2024 All-Wales study](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/168557/)) ([2025 primary-care reflex-testing study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00045632241298891)) ([UK cost-utility analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6677967/))

- The cited antibody cohorts demonstrate risk prediction, but they did not test whether universal once-only TPO screening improves patient-centered outcomes. ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283)) ([Thyroid Events Amsterdam cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/770358))


## Practical interpretation

TSH alone is usually adequate for **initial biochemical evaluation for primary thyroid dysfunction when testing is clinically indicated**, but it should not be treated as a universal thyroid health score. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388))

Free T4 is the most important additional test when TSH may be misleading. Free or total T3 has a narrower role and is generally more useful in suspected hyperthyroidism than in screening for hypothyroidism. An isolated low T3 is not sufficient to diagnose a conversion disorder. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388)) ([review of non-thyroidal illness syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-020-01482-4))

TPO antibodies can reveal autoimmune risk before TSH becomes abnormal, but they predict susceptibility rather than current hormone deficiency. Their most defensible use in the cited guidance is targeted testing when the result changes etiological interpretation or follow-up, particularly when TSH is above the reference range. ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([20-year Whickham follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7641412/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

The strongest overall strategy supported by the cited guidance and studies is therefore neither TSH-only in every circumstance nor a full panel for everyone. It is **TSH-first testing combined with context-sensitive expansion**. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169)) ([NICE thyroid disease guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng145)) ([2018 European Thyroid Association guideline](https://doi.org/10.1159/000491388))

## Funding and conflict-of-interest assessment

The 2023 ATA-commissioned laboratory review reported no external industry funding, no funding for the article, and no permitted personal financial conflicts among the writing-group members; one author was employed by the ATA. The paper describes itself as a state-of-the-art status report, not a systematic review or a clinical guidance document. ([American Thyroid Association-commissioned laboratory review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/thy.2023.0169))

The 2021 T4/T3 meta-analysis reported no competing financial interests and disclosed public or academic research support for two authors. The 2024 meta-analysis reported no funding and no competing interests, although its internally inconsistent GHQ-28 reporting remains a methodological concern independent of funding. ([2021 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8917901/)) ([2024 combination-therapy meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12902-024-01612-6))

The free-T4 cost-effectiveness analysis declared no competing interests. The Tehran Thyroid Study was funded by a university and reported no conflicts of interest. ([UK cost-utility analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6677967/)) ([Tehran Thyroid Study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0169283))

This article is a structured narrative evidence review rather than a systematic review. Evidence selection prioritized systematic reviews, randomized trials, cohort studies, and relevant guidelines, with attention to study design, generalizability, analytical limitations, funding, and conflicts of interest. Statements that evidence was not identified refer to the literature cited here rather than to a registered exhaustive search.
